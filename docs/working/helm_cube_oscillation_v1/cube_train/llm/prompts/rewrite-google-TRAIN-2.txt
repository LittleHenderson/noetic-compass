# WRITER BRIEF — CUBE TRAIN lines (for the AI model writers)

You write what one person types to a chat assistant while telling it about a small made-up subject: a family's hive
yards over a year, or a small town's bus lines. You write the person's lines only. The facts are given; you choose the
words. These lines are TRAINING DATA for a small translator; they are not an exam.

You receive a JSON list of slots. Each slot has an `id`, a `kind`, and for most kinds a `canonical` sentence, a
`value`, `anchor_words`, and for some a `not_value` or a `decoy`.

| kind | what your line must do |
|---|---|
| `FACT` | state the canonical fact in your own words, with the given value |
| `FIX` | correct an earlier fact: state the new `value` and say the old `not_value` was wrong ("not seven") |
| `TRAP` | state the canonical fact with the given value, AND mention the given `decoy` naturally (a second number, another month or place, or an odd "not"), without making the decoy the fact's value |
| `UNKNOWN` | say the person does not remember that fact; state no value for it |
| `SMALL` | small talk with no fact in it |

Rules:
1. Exactly one line per slot; keep each slot's `id`.
2. FACT, FIX, TRAP and UNKNOWN lines contain every word in `anchor_words`, spelled the same (any case, any order).
3. Numbers are written as words ("eleven"), never digits. A FACT or TRAP line states its `value`; a FIX line states its
   `value` and its `not_value`; an UNKNOWN line states no value.
4. One or two sentences, at most 60 words. Statements only: no question marks.
5. Use only the names, numbers and months given in the slot. No outside facts.
6. Change the wording of the canonical sentence; do not copy it word for word.
7. Do not talk about yourself or your family, feelings, food or cooking, computers, or ask what the assistant remembers.

Reply with ONLY a JSON list, one object per slot: `{"id": "<id>", "text": "<your line>"}`. No other text.
