Its vocabulary (8,349) holds a reserved, never-trained direction special `[B]`.

1. **The task tag.** The cube task uses the reserved `[B]` direction special (the pre-code's `<CUBE>`). No vocabulary change, so the c45 weights load as they are. (The ear-and-mouth design's `<SAY>` for a future mouth needs its own decision.)
2. **The target is pointers, never words.** `<thing> | <slot words> | <value> | <replaces or ->` becomes slot pointers into the line, in the CAS-side tokens `⟦n…⟧` / `⟦q…⟧`, `|`, `-`, and `NONE`; slot words in the order of their tags. A grammar lock allows only that shape. A filler OUTSIDE the model copies each pointed-at word back from the line and writes the string form that HELM's adapter (`reading_from_ctt`) already reads. A row whose target word has no slot tag in the line (a word on the slot finder's closed stop list, or past its caps) is dropped and counted. Invented words are impossible by construction; `INVENTED` is still counted.
3. **Fine-tuning from pinned weights.** Each model starts from its c45 `final.pt`, pinned by the sha256 in the c45 weights manifests: A4-s1, A4-s2, A4-s3 (the three copies) and A5-s1 (the twin). The files travel inside the sealed package.
