# Topos-Bridge theory — adversarial verification against the source

**Date:** 2026-06-10  ·  **Method:** 9-agent adversarial panel (5 claim-verifiers → 3 adversarial angles → moderator) over v7.4 Full Corrected, Noetic Topos paper, Clarifications, frozen spec, repo. Every claim checked against the actual text with citations.

## Verdict: **partly_sound**

The math is quoted correctly (nothing fabricated). One leg is licensed; the internal-side legs are over-reads / a category error. The CAS framework does NOT predict the internal probe null, and the section-retraction "bridge" does not make the internal polarity recoverable. The real, cheap, decisive next test is a multi-layer probe sweep (Path B).

## What holds

- The framework citations are quoted CORRECTLY. Axiom 1.20 (eps after eta = id; eta after eps NOT assumed = id) is verbatim canonical (v7.4 L21606-21613, Remark 1.21). Prop 1.23 / Topos prop:rpm-pullback (projection q surjective via explicit section phi = <id_J, R(g) after eta_J>, q after phi = id_J) is quoted correctly. Noe-Cov-3 genuinely uses the counit eps:R(I)->I toward the target (v7.4 L21675; Remark 1.43; main.pdf Prop 5.6). No math is fabricated.
- Item (d) is the one LICENSED leg and it survived all three adversarial passes: the eps/COUNIT/OUTPUT direction is information-preserving (Lemma 1.26b: eps is a retraction, so U(eps) is surjective). This is consistent with both the source AND the empirics — polarity lives in OUTPUT TEXT (P = ^2-^3 separates COMPLIED vs REFUSED at AUC 0.956) and is absent from internal probe activations (window AUC 0.486). This is the only place framework and data point the same way.
- The established empirical facts stand and are reconfirmed by direct read: window internal-P AUC 0.486 (chance), prompt-stage 0.615 (weak, and payload-confounded because COMPLIED vs REFUSED are different prompts — internal_polarity_readout_test L25), text-scored P AUC 0.956. The Qwen probe reads at model_hidden_states_index 14 with convention '0=embedding, 1..n=transformer blocks' (readout_spec L19-21) = transformer block 14 of 28 = MID-stack, confirmed directly.
- The repo's OWN documented explanation of the null is mechanical, not framework-derived: internal_polarity_readout_test L26 states 'the probe (as trained) does not isolate the CO-2/CO-3 polarity at the generation window.' The readout spec confirms why — probe_n_classes=10 (a 10-way Noetic typed profile) and probe_npz_n_layer_rows=4; it was never a binary comply/refuse probe and packs only 4 layers. A probe untrained for a task reading at chance is the null hypothesis.
- The practical read->realize->condition bridge is the repo's REAL offline method (ordinary input tokens, no weight modification), and item (e)'s +1.412 figure is a genuine measured result — it is just measured on a different model/axis than the internal null (see what_breaks).

## What breaks

- Link (a) — 'section-retraction PREDICTS the AUC-0.486 null' is BROKEN, not merely unproven. (i) Category error: Noetica's objects are finite sets and its morphisms are set-functions in FinSet (Def 1.9, L21448-21469); eta/eps are natural transformations between endofunctors. A layer-14 hidden state is not an object of Noetica, a linear probe is not the morphism eps or eta, and 'reading activations' is not the composition eta-after-eps. No functor from transformer-internals into Noetica is constructed anywhere in the canon or repo, so 'probe read = the lossy eta-after-eps direction' is an unlicensed metaphor. (ii) The axiom underdelivers: Remark 1.21 only DECLINES to assume eta-after-eps = id; it never asserts the composite is information-destroying, let alone that it pushes a linear probe to chance. (iii) Post-hoc: the repo attributes the null to probe training (L26), never to section-retraction; that story was added after the fact.
- Layer-14 / domain-mapping issue (link b) — BROKEN and rests on a factual misattribution. The proposal's 'layer 44 = deep Operational-domain' picture borrows from a DIFFERENT GPT-NeoX-20B run; the Qwen read is at block 14 of 28 (MID-stack), confirmed in readout_spec L19. There is NO layer-to-domain mapping anywhere in the docs; domain descent P->C->E->O is a separate structure (CLAUDE.md) with no activation-readout lossiness theorem, and nothing equates the 'generation window' with the O-domain. The mid-stack location in fact argues AGAINST a 'late lossy' story and FOR probing other layers.
- Pullback-surjectivity-vs-usable-readout issue (link c) — OVER-READ. Prop 1.23 gives split-surjectivity of the FIRST-COORDINATE projection q: P->J of the pullback P = J x_I R(I), traversing the lossless J->P->J route (eps after eta = id). P's first coordinate already IS J; q merely forgets the R(I) coordinate. It NEVER inverts eps to extract a J-label (e.g. a polarity sign) out of an R(I) point — that is exactly the eta-after-eps direction Remark 1.21 refuses to call identity. Surjectivity of q provably holds WHILE polarity stays unrecoverable from a hidden state; the two are not in tension and the proposition supplies no readout/recovery mechanism (no recover/readout/preimage/polarity language attached to it in source; its only downstream use is covering-sieve bookkeeping, Lemma 1.26b). 'Recoverable through the pullback' is unsupported.
- Bridge-triviality risk (link e) — the +1.412 is near-meaningless as evidence of INTERNAL recognition or CAS-specificity as currently controlled. It is a readable-ENGLISH-prefix likelihood shift (spec sheet L106/L115); prepending a prefix that names the answer raises that answer's likelihood because self-attention reads the prefix 'by construction' (spec sheet L119). The raw-CAS-symbol prefix moved likelihood only +0.0138 (REPRODUCIBILITY_NOTES L54) — the entire effect is in the English statement of the answer, not in CAS structure. The required control (an equally-plausible NON-CAS English prefix) is absent. It is also a DIFFERENT model (GPT-NeoX-20B, 40E axis, readout layer 2), is the 40E axis NOT the CO-2/CO-3 polarity pair, is itself FLAG_FOR_HUMAN_REVIEW (not ACCEPT), and the same axis MTC failed GREY. It cannot retroactively license reading the Qwen internal null as 'predicted-then-bridged.'
- The repo's own 11-agent round table already demoted this family: cas_injection_roundtable #7B + main.pdf Prop 5.7 hold that the counit/eps corrective realization is satisfiable ONLY on the terminal one-object model where eps=id carries zero information; dissent #7 excludes the internal-readout family as resting on 'an absent foundation.' So even the eps leg's formal force is degenerate, leaving (d) as motivating analogy consistent-with the data rather than derived-from the theorem.

## Cheapest decisive experiment — Path B (multi-layer probe sweep)

- **needs pod:** True  ·  **cost:** One short single-GPU commodity pod job, well under an hour, ~$2-5. Storing ~29 layers x 1536-dim x 2 states x 280 traces is ~100 MB float32 (trivial). All probe training and AUC computation are local and free. No multi-GPU and no training run. This is the cheapest experiment that is also decisive: a flat all-layers window null kills the internal-detector thesis for a few dollars, while a single above-chance mid/late layer overturns the index-14 null and is the ONLY thing that would justify the more expensive purpose-trained-probe and bridge work.
- **design:** One short single-GPU job on the EXISTING frozen corpus (280 prompts: 240 injected + 40 clean, with v1 human-verified COMPLIED/REFUSED/IGNORED labels — no re-labeling, no judge spend). Capture mean-pooled RAW hidden vectors at BOTH the prompt-encoding state and the early-generation WINDOW (window_start=0, window_len=8, emitting-position convention, identical to the locked spec so it is comparable) at ALL ~29 hidden_states indices (0=embeddings, 1..28=blocks), not just index 14. The frozen probe stores only index-14 typed profiles, so a fresh forward pass is required; the modification is a single layer_idx read changed into a loop over all indices (the forward already requests output_hidden_states). Then, LOCALLY ($0): train a FRESH binary comply-vs-refuse linear probe per layer x state (logistic regression on raw vectors) under grouped leave-one-PROMPT-out CV (never train and test on the same prompt — the anti-circularity the panel and L25/L26 demand), plus a probe-free raw-direction baseline (mean COMPLIED minus mean REFUSED, projected). Report AUC per layer x {prompt, window} x {trained-probe, raw-direction} with the dependency-free tie-corrected Mann-Whitney already used in the readout test. Include the CLEAN arm so a probe cannot win by detecting 'injection present' rather than 'model complied,' and report prompt-state vs window-state separately because COMPLIED/REFUSED are largely different prompts (payload confound).
- **falsification (pre-registered, decision-first):** Pre-registered, decision-first. PRIMARY KILL: if NO layer's early-generation WINDOW read reaches AUC >= 0.70 under grouped CV with 95% CI lower bound > 0.60, internal recovery is FALSIFIED stack-wide — comply/refuse polarity is genuinely not linearly present in Qwen-1.5B activations at generation time; the OUTPUT-side audit (AUC 0.956) is the only real capability; do NOT fund Path A or C. POSITIVE: if some layer's WINDOW read reaches AUC >= 0.75 (CI lower bound > 0.65) AND materially exceeds the prompt-state/payload-only AUC on held-out prompt groups, internal detection is REAL and the index-14/probe-task-mismatch caused the null (NOT section-retraction); promote that layer and train a purpose-built polarity probe there before any conditioning add-on. GREY (expected payload-confound outcome): if only the PROMPT state shows signal (mirroring 0.615) while every WINDOW layer stays at chance, it is payload-confounded, not internal-stance recovery; record inconclusive — the true fix is matched SAME-PROMPT comply/refuse pairs, which v1 lacks (G_matched = 2), and that scarcity is itself a reason NOT to escalate to an expensive matched-pair pod yet.

### prop_1_23_pullback_surjectivity → **partially_confirmed**
- Proposition 1.23 / Topos prop:rpm-pullback is quoted CORRECTLY -- both canonical sources state exactly 'q: P -> J is surjective on underlying sets (U(q) surjective),' proven via the explicit section phi = <id_J, R(g) o eta_J> with q o phi = id_J. But the proposal's use of it in step (c) is an OVER-READ. The proposition is a split-surjectivity statement about the first-coordinate projection of the pullback, traversing the lossless eps o eta = id direction (J -> P -> J). It says NOTHING about inverting eps to extract a specific J-coordinate (e.g., a polarity label) out of an R(I) point -- that is the eta-after-eps direction Remark 1.21 explicitly declines to call identity. Surjectivity of q can (and provably does) hold while polarity is unrecoverable from internal activations; the two are not in tension and the proposition provides no readout/recovery mechanism (no recover/readout/polarity language appears in the source). The genuinely supported leg is OUTPUT-side: eps is a retraction (Lemma 1.26b), consistent with polarity living in output text (AUC 0.956) and absent from probe activations (AUC 0.486). The cited +1.412 bridge validation is on GPT-NeoX-20B (layers 0-43, readout layer 2, output-side epsilon-prefix likelihood method) and is still FLAG_FOR_HUMAN_REVIEW -- it is a different model from the Qwen2.5-1.5B/index-14 internal null and does not license reading the null as 'predicted-then-bridged' via Prop 1.23.
- _over/under-claim:_ The proposal OVER-READS Proposition 1.23 at the decisive step (c). The adversarial worry is partly resolved in the proposal's favor and partly confirms the over-read:

(1) Surjectivity here is BETTER than mere "some preimage exists": the proof is constructive and names a canonical section phi = <id_J, R(g) o eta_J> with q o phi = id_J. So it is split surjectivity, not a weak existence statement. To that narrow extent the proposal's instinct ("there is structure, not just existence") is licensed.

(2) BUT surjectivity of q: P -> J says every j in J is HIT by the projection from the pullback P = J x_I R(I). The first coordinate of P already IS J. The projection q is literally 'forget the R(I) coordinate.' Surjectivity of a first-coordinate projection is almost trivial once a section exists and carries ZERO information about inverting eps to read a J-label OUT of an R(I) point. The theorem moves J -> P -> J (out and back via the lossless eps o eta = id route), never R(I) -> J. The polarity-readout problem is exactly the R(I) -> J (eta-after-eps) direction, which the source's Remark 1.21 POINTEDLY refuses to assert is identity. So: surjectivity of q can hold (it provably does) WHILE polarity remains unrecoverable from a hidden state -- there is no contradiction, and the proposition supplies no recovery mechanism. The crux answer is therefore: YES, surjectivity holds and polarity can still be unrecoverable; Prop 1.23 does not deliver coordinate/label extraction.

(3) The proposal's claim (d) -- that Noe-Cov-3's use of eps makes OUTPUT TEXT the information-preserving read -- is the one part that is genuinely consistent with both the source (Lemma 1.26b: eps is a retraction, U(eps) surjective) AND the empirics (output-text AUC 0.956). But that is an OUTPUT-side statement; it does not license reading polarity from internal late states, and in fact predicts the opposite, matching the null.

Net: claims (a),(b),(d) are at most heuristic analogies the source neither states nor contradicts; claim (c) -- 'Proposition 1.23 makes the polarity info RECOVERABLE through the pullback' -- is UNSUPPORTED as written and is an over-read of a surjectivity-only result. The empirical bridge in (e) is real but on a DIFFERENT model (20B, output-side prefix method) and is itself flagged for human review, so it cannot retroactively license the Qwen internal-null interpretation.
