## CT-01: representable external-product lift

Target: Paper 2 section 5.2 and the `PUB-P2-006` package note.

Outcome: `COUNTEREXAMPLE / BLOCKING CORRECTION CAT-RC-001`

Let `F_A` be the finite `J_A`-sheaf witness and let

```text
H(c,V) = yC_0(c) x F_A(V)
       = Hom_C(c,C_0) x F_A(V).
```

Because the `L` profile registers the null cover at `empty`, any lifted `J_A`-sheaf must satisfy

```text
H(c,empty) ~= 1
```

for every `c`. But `F_A(empty)=1`, so

```text
H(c,empty) ~= Hom_C(c,C_0).
```

This is terminal only when `Hom_C(c,C_0)` is a singleton for every `c`, i.e. only when the selected representable is terminal. A general representable can have zero, one, or several such morphisms.

There is a second failure at a target `V` meeting two `A` blocks. With `S=Hom_C(c,C_0)`, the cover comparison contains

```text
S x F_A(V)
  -> (S x F_A(V intersect A_0)) x (S x F_A(V intersect A_1)),
```

and the two `S` coordinates are forced to be equal. For `|S|>1`, matching families with different `S` coordinates are not in the image.

Candidate repair: use the terminal presheaf `1_C` and define `H(c,V)=F_A(V)` with identity action in the `C` coordinate, after registering the exact lifted topology and nonemptiness of `C`. This may establish ambient distinctness of the component `L` reflectors; it does not establish their product-site composition or interaction with `J_obs`.

## CT-02: finite reflector formula and null cover

Target: `PUB-P2-008`.

Outcome: `SURVIVES IN PSh(L,Set)`

For a partition `P`, the proposed formula is

```text
(R_P F)(V) = product_{B in P, V intersect B nonempty} F(V intersect B).
```

At `empty`, the empty product is `1`, which is exactly the null-cover sheaf condition. On a nonempty partition piece, there is one nonempty factor. On a target spanning blocks, distinct pieces overlap at `empty`; the reflected presheaf has terminal value there, so component sections match automatically. This makes the gluing map a canonical reindexing bijection.

For any `J_P`-sheaf `G`, `G(empty)=1`; applying a map `F->G` componentwise yields a matching family, and unique gluing supplies the unique factorization through the unit. No assumption that one generic plus construction always sheafifies is used.

The registered verifier reproduced the empty-context case, inverse laws, restriction naturality, naturality in presheaf maps, and unit compatibility.

## CT-03: joined topology terminology

Target: `PUB-P2-007` and all uses of `J_atom`.

Outcome: `SURVIVES AS LOCALLY DEFINED SINGLETON-PARTITION TOPOLOGY / TERMINOLOGY CORRECTION CAT-RC-004`

The proof that

```text
J_A join J_R = J_Q
```

for `Q={{omega} | omega in Omega}` survives. The label `atomic topology` is not safe without qualification. In standard category-theory terminology, an atomic topology is generally described by nonempty sieves on a category satisfying an Ore condition. Here the locally defined topology also includes a null cover at `empty` and is generated by a particular singleton partition.

The exhaustive verifier found:

```text
all sieves over all 16 contexts: 298
covering sieves in J_Q:          168
```

Thus the execution itself does not support a statement that every nonempty sieve is covering. Use `J_singleton`, `J_Q`, or always say `topology generated by the singleton partition`.

## CT-04: transverse reflector commutation

Target: `PUB-P2-008`.

Outcome: `SURVIVES IN PSh(L,Set)`

For `V subset Omega`, transversality gives

```text
V intersect A_i intersect R_j
```

either empty or the singleton `{(i,j)}`. Therefore both nested products are indexed by exactly the atoms of `V`. Flattening and regrouping preserve all retained atom coordinates and drop the same absent coordinates under restriction. The comparison is natural in `V`, natural in `F`, invertible, and unit-compatible.

This check does not lift the isomorphism to `PSh(C x L)`, compose it with `J_obs`, or turn the natural isomorphisms into strict equalities.

## CT-05: pairwise-compatible probability non-gluing

Target: `PUB-P4-001`.

Outcome: `SURVIVES`

Let `X,Y,Z` be binary. Give each pair the uniform distribution on unequal outcomes. Every singleton marginal is uniform, so the pair distributions agree on overlaps. A global support point would have to satisfy

```text
X != Y,
Y != Z,
X != Z.
```

The first two imply `X=Z`, contradicting the third. No global joint distribution exists.

This is an existence failure. It does not rule out extension under a proved regular or acyclic profile.

## CT-06: unique gluing also fails when extension exists

Target: Paper 4 section 4.

Outcome: `SURVIVES`

Give two binary variables only their uniform singleton marginals. At least two global extensions exist:

```text
mu_corr(00)=mu_corr(11)=1/2,
mu_anti(01)=mu_anti(10)=1/2.
```

Both restrict to the same local marginals. Thus existence alone does not provide the uniqueness demanded by an ordinary Set-valued sheaf condition. A canonical optimizer would add a new selection theorem and hypotheses rather than recover uniqueness from compatibility.

## CT-07: Farkas certificate orientation

Target: `PUB-P4-003`.

Outcome: `SURVIVES AS A METHOD STATEMENT / IMPLEMENTATION OPEN`

Suppose the primal system is

```text
Aq=b,
q>=0.
```

If a proposed obstruction satisfies

```text
A^T y>=0,
b^T y<0,
```

then primal feasibility would imply

```text
b^T y = (Aq)^T y = q^T(A^T y) >= 0,
```

a contradiction. The sign orientation is correct. To become evidence, strict negativity must be verified in exact rational arithmetic or by an interval whose upper endpoint is negative. No such schema or verifier is present.

## CT-08: kernel restriction-square typing

Target: Paper 4 section 3.2.

Outcome: `SURVIVES AS A TYPED CANDIDATE / IMPLEMENTATION OPEN`

The proposed square

```text
r^Y_(V->U) o K_V = K_U o r^X_(V->U)
```

is well typed when

```text
K_V : X_V ~> Y_V,
r^X : X_V ~> X_U,
K_U : X_U ~> Y_U,
r^Y : Y_V ~> Y_U.
```

If any restriction is stochastic, both routes must be composed in the same Markov category. If equality is approximate, the metric, tolerance, estimator, and uncertainty become additional data. No Noetic kernel family currently instantiates the square.

## CT-09: finite-dimensional Laplacian necessity

Target: `PUB-P4-005`, Paper 4 sections 2 and 8, and `CIT-HANSEN-GHRIST-2019` use scope.

Outcome: `COUNTEREXAMPLE / BLOCKING CORRECTION CAT-RC-002`

Consider a graph with vertices `v,w` and one edge `e`. Let every stalk be the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space `ell^2`, with bounded identity restriction maps from both vertex stalks to the edge stalk. For an orientation of the edge,

```text
delta : ell^2 direct_sum ell^2 -> ell^2,
delta(x_v,x_w) = x_w-x_v
```

is bounded. Its adjoint exists, and

```text
Delta^0 = delta* delta
```

is a bounded cellular-sheaf Laplacian. Therefore finite-dimensional stalks are not universally necessary.

Correct profile statement:

> The proposed finite executable and matrix-certificate profile requires a finite cell complex, finite-dimensional real or complex stalks, linear structure maps, and declared inner products. More general Hilbert-valued profiles require analytic domain and boundedness conditions and are outside this paper.

The stronger and still valid type boundary is that a Set-valued Grothendieck sheaf or a Markov kernel does not acquire a cellular Laplacian without an explicit linear/Hilbert representation and adjoint data.

## CT-10: cellular-sheaf variance interface

Target: Paper 4 six-layer architecture.

Outcome: `OPEN INTERFACE REQUIREMENT CAT-RC-008`

In the Hansen-Ghrist convention, a cellular sheaf on the face poset is a covariant functor

```text
F : P_X -> Vect,
```

with structure maps from a face to a coface. A Grothendieck presheaf in Paper 4 is contravariant on its context category. Both may be informally called restriction maps, but their variance is not interchangeable.

Before implementation, each serialized map must state the category orientation and source/target stalks. A conversion between the marginal presheaf and a cellular sheaf must reverse or reorganize variance explicitly if their selected posets use opposite arrow conventions.

## CT-11: finite kernel versus induced affine channel

Target: `PUB-P4-002`.

Outcome: `SURVIVES WITH SERIALIZATION BOUNDARY`

For finite sets and fixed coordinate bases, an affine map between probability simplices is determined by its values on the Dirac vertices, hence by a stochastic kernel; conversely the kernel induces the affine map. This mathematical correspondence does not make their records identical:

- the kernel is indexed by conditional rows `K(y|x)`;
- the channel is a map between distribution objects;
- source/target identities, orientation, and normalization conventions still require distinct schemas.

The draft correctly treats the correspondence as a conversion rather than an alias.
