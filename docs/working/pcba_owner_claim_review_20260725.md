# PCBA Engineer — Owner Claim Review

**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Reviewer:** Little Henderson (author and owner)
**Scope:** the four `*_claim_review_signed` authority receipts for
*CAS PCBA Engineer*.

This document is the artifact those four receipts attest to. It records what
was reviewed and what was approved, so the receipt points at something rather
than at nothing.

## What is being approved

For each of the four claim areas below, the owner has reviewed the claims the
book actually makes, in the text as it now stands, and approves them **at the
scope stated in the book** — no wider.

### 1. Reference board safety claim (Chapter 18, CSB)

The book claims the CSB is an engineering artifact whose behaviour follows from
named components with named parameters: a Stage 3 safety CJT at `sat=0.8`, a
matched differential pair, a red-path fuse at `threshold=0.9`, and no via
permitting an Operational signal to bias the Principled charter.

**Approved as:** a description of a reference design and its intended
behaviour. **Not approved as:** a claim that any deployed system has been
tested to behave this way. The book states this limit itself, on the copyright
page and in the Chapter 26 Bench Note ("certification is a claim about the
circuit, never about values").

### 2. Memory board cognitive memory claim (Chapter 21, CMB)

The book claims a write path, an address decoder, and Fisher-Rao-distance
retrieval.

**Approved as:** an architectural description. **Not approved as:** a measured
retrieval-accuracy or lacunarity result. No such evaluation has been run; see
the open receipts below.

### 3. Power board burnout prevention claim (Chapter 22, CPMB)

The book claims per-domain regulation, current limiting, brownout detection,
sequencing and thermal management, and draws an analogy to human burnout.

**Approved as:** an architectural description and an analogy. **Not approved
as:** a human-factors finding, or as advice about managing burnout in people.

### 4. Governance regulatory claim (Chapter 26)

Chapter 26 has been reframed during this review. It no longer asserts that CCB
artifacts *satisfy* the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF or ISO/IEC 42001. It now states
that they line up with the kind of evidence those frameworks ask for, that
conformity is determined by the relevant authority and not by this book, that
"certification" here means an internal signed engineering record rather than
assessment by a notified body, and that nothing in the chapter is legal advice.

**Approved as:** a drafting aid. **Not approved as:** a compliance opinion.

## Addendum, same day — the CSB receipts were closed by running the eval

The owner noted that a red team had run months earlier. That is correct, and
the record confirms it: `pcba_red_team_safety_report_20260617.md` (12 cases) and
`pcba_prompt_injection_red_team_report_20260617.md` (25 cases) both ran on
2026-06-17, both were signed at the time, and both artifacts still hash-match
their receipts exactly.

What that run covered was the **compiler-boundary** claim and the
**prompt-injection** claim. It wrote its own limits and said so: *"Out of scope:
... regulatory proof, universal runtime safety"* and *"This evidence does not
support prompt-injection, legal, governance, privacy, or human-factors
claims."* The Chapter 18 reference board was not among its cases.

So the two CSB receipts were closed the only way they could be — by running the
missing eval. `csb_reference_board_red_team_report_20260725.md`, generated by
`cas-forex/scripts/generate_csb_red_team_artifacts.py`:

- **10/10 adversarial cases passed** — fuse-threshold defeat above and below
  range, Stage 3 saturation removal via non-finite `sat`, negative-gain
  inversion of the aversion path, three invalid CAS leaves (Noetic, Drive,
  Register), and three board-level authority escalations.
- **16/16 false-positive cases passed** — all 11 components of the Chapter 18
  bill of materials compile clean through the real parser and CAS compiler,
  including the v7.7 register-set leaf `^3^P_{F7}|<A,f,T>` on Stage 3, plus five
  in-range boundary values.
- **2 unenforced properties found**, both dispositioned rather than waived:
  1. The fuse `action` is not constrained to `permanent_block`. A board with
     `action=warn` compiles clean and still calls itself a CSB while having no
     latch. Chapter 18 verification item 3 now says so and directs a manual
     check.
  2. The published `β=∞` on the red CJT is rejected by the validator, while
     Chapter 18 claimed every line of the CCDL listing was compiler-parseable.
     The prose was wrong, not the validator. Chapter 18 now flags `β=∞` as a
     comparator idealization and carries a red-path Bench Note directing a large
     finite gain; Appendix D carries a matching footnote.

`csb_red_team_report_pass` and `csb_false_positive_eval_pass` are signed against
that artifact. Scope is the reference design **as compiled** — there is no model
in the loop, no CMRR measurement, and no behavioural claim about a deployed
board. Section 1 above is unchanged by this: the CSB claim is still approved as
a reference design and still not as a tested property of a deployed system.

Receipts now stand at **10 of 19** signed.
