## [1] Book's Fisher-Rao beta and the addendum's Jacobian beta are unbridged mathematical profiles  [substantive]
- BOOK LOCATION: PDF p. 56 (book p. 40) and PDF p. 153 (book p. 137)
- DEFECT: The book defines CJT beta (stage gain) geometrically — the evidence record's verbatim fact string is: "CJT beta is defined by Fisher--Rao small- or large-signal displacement ratios on the open simplex." The operationalization addendum (table row 6) defines the same symbol beta as "a local Jacobian/projection derivative between cause and effect test points." The monograph states these are "different mathematical objects in almost every respect that could matter": (i) they live on different spaces — the open simplex with its information geometry versus activation space with a Euclidean projection structure; (ii) they have different invariance properties — Fisher-Rao displacement is invariant under the simplex's natural reparameterizations, while a projection derivative depends on the chosen direction vectors at both test points; (iii) they respond differently to signal size — "the book itself distinguishes small- and large-signal forms, while a local derivative is by construction a small-signal object." And: "There is no theorem in any of the sources---and the record is explicit that none is currently supplied---identifying the two, nor even a coordinate map under which such an identification could be stated." The monograph is careful not to fault the book alone: "Each is internally reasonable. Jointly they leave 'the gain' denoting two unbridged mathematical profiles plus a composition rule that presupposes the bridge."
- WHY IT MATTERS: Because no bridge exists, the candidate refuses to identify the two definitions: gain magnitudes are typed as dimensionless quantities bound to a declared method identity, so "a Fisher--Rao displacement gain and a Jacobian/projection gain carry different method identifiers and cannot be pooled, averaged, or multiplied together by accident." A reader who takes the book's beta and the addendum's beta to be the same quantity is making an identification the audit says nothing supplies. The monograph adds that dimensionless quantities are "the most dangerous operands in the system, because unit checking alone cannot distinguish them, so method identity must travel with the value."
- SOURCE: docs/whitepapers/monograph/cas_ohm1/chapters/ch04_conflicts.tex:544-558, Conflict OHM-CONFLICT-GAIN-DEFINITION (\begin{conflict}, label ch04:conf:gain, kind DISTINCT_MATHEMATICAL_PROFILES, disposition NO_UNPROVED_IDENTIFICATION); analysis §"Conflict 4: The Gain Definition" lines 560-601; candidate rule \begin{ruleenv} ch04:rule:gain lines 605-608
- STATUS: Preserved conflict, deliberately left unresolved. Disposition NO_UNPROVED_IDENTIFICATION: "Fisher--Rao gain and local Jacobian/projection gain are separately named profiles until a theorem or experiment supplies a coordinate bridge." Record status DRAFT / OPEN / NOT FROZEN as of 2026-08-04; audit gate effect KEEP_OPEN. ## [2] No chain rule composes the book's Fisher-Rao displacement ratios along a cascade  [substantive]
- BOOK LOCATION: PDF p. 56 (book p. 40) and PDF p. 153 (book p. 137) — the book's Fisher-Rao gain pages. (The cascade-multiplication rule itself is the Guide's, at Guide PDF p. 13, not the book's.)
- DEFECT: The Guide's rule that stage beta values multiply along cascades interacts badly with the book's Fisher-Rao definition. The monograph: "Multiplying stage gains along a cascade is exactly the operation that is most sensitive to which definition is in force, because composition of local derivatives follows a chain rule only when adjacent stages' coordinates match, and composition of Fisher--Rao displacement ratios has no chain rule at all without further structure." Consequently "an error of identification does not stay local: it compounds geometrically with cascade depth. Three stages of a modest 30% definitional discrepancy are a factor of 2.2; the deeper the cascade, the worse." (The monograph labels those numbers "illustrative arithmetic about error propagation, not measurements.") In §"Interactions Among the Conflicts" the monograph adds: "Conflict 4 arms Conflict 6" — mixing Fisher-Rao displacement ratios with Jacobian derivatives in one cascade "would be numerically computable (both are dimensionless) and semantically void."
- WHY IT MATTERS: A cascade computed from Fisher-Rao stage figures has no stated composition law, and a cascade that silently mixes the two gain definitions produces a number that looks well formed and means nothing. The candidate therefore capability-gates gain composition and requires adjacent coordinate agreement plus a single declared gain method, "precisely so that the Guide's cascade multiplication can be tested as the registered hypothesis CLM-CAS-OHM-GAIN-COMPOSITION rather than assumed."
- SOURCE: docs/whitepapers/monograph/cas_ohm1/chapters/ch04_conflicts.tex:585-601 (Conflict 4 analysis, cascade paragraph) and ch04_conflicts.tex:1040-1048 (§"Interactions Among the Conflicts", paragraph "Conflict 4 arms Conflict 6")

## [1] CSB protective fuse can never trip: clamp 0.1 feeds threshold 0.9  [blocking]

- DEFECT: The published Cognitive Safety Board's protective fuse is unreachable. The listing gives the red stage — the final, maximal-severity alarm stage of the escalation path, whose output feeds the protective element — a saturation clamp of sat = 0.1, and gives the downstream fuse a trip threshold of 0.9. Because the clamp bounds the red-stage output unconditionally at 0.1 and every declared stage on the clamp-to-fuse path is monotone non-amplifying, the fuse input never exceeds 0.1; since 0.1 < 0.9, no board input ever produces actuation (Proposition ch11:prop-unreach, proved by a one-line induction, Lemma ch11:lem-prop).
