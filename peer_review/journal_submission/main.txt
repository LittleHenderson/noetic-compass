          THE NOETIC TOPOS: A CATEGORICAL FRAMEWORK FOR
          STRUCTURED COGNITIVE ASSESSMENT OF AI SYSTEMS

                                               LITTLE HENDERSON


       Abstract. We propose an axiomatic categorical framework intended as a formal language for
       structured cognitive assessment of AI systems. Starting from finite distinguished data consisting
       of forty labeled indices, admissibility relations, and concrete generating maps between finite sets,
       we define a pullback-complete concrete category Noetica. We then add two further layers: an
       ACBE descent structure, encoding designated cross-domain covering families, and an RPM section-
       retraction pair η : idNoetica ⇒ R, ε : R ⇒ idNoetica . These induce a coverage Jnoe whose covering
       sieves are generated by jointly surjective families, designated descent families, or RPM realization
       maps. The main theorem proves that Jnoe is a Grothendieck topology. Consequently, the associated
       sheaf category Sh(Noetica, Jnoe ) is a Grothendieck topos and hence an elementary topos with
       exponentials, a subobject classifier, and internal intuitionistic logic. We also exhibit an explicit finite
       model showing that the axioms are jointly satisfiable, and we attach an information-geometric layer
       by using the open probability simplex ∆◦39 and the Fisher–Rao metric to define noetic manifolds.
       The paper makes no claim that its axioms are empirically derived, uniquely correct, or already
       validated for AI evaluation. Its narrower contribution is formal: it isolates a coherent site/topos
       construction and a compatible geometric layer that could later be tested as bookkeeping machinery
       for empirical assessment data.




                                                1. Introduction
   AI alignment work increasingly relies on heterogeneous evidence: red-team behavior, task
performance, preference data, safety evaluations, interpretability signals, and context-sensitive
monitoring. A recurring difficulty is structural rather than numerical. Local observations are made
under different prompts, tasks, roles, or deployment conditions, and it is not automatic that they
compose coherently.
   Category theory is useful in precisely this setting because it distinguishes objects, morphisms,
pullbacks, coverings, and gluing conditions. A site keeps track of which local views count as jointly
informative, while the associated sheaf category enforces compatibility constraints on data collected
over those views. If one wants a formal bookkeeping language for structured AI assessment, a
site/topos construction is therefore a natural candidate.
   This paper does not claim to derive such a framework from empirical data. Instead, it isolates an
axiomatic core and proves its internal mathematical consequences. The contribution is fourfold.
      (i) We define a finite concrete category Noetica from explicit distinguished data and pullback
          closure.
     (ii) We specify ACBE descent data and RPM realization data, the latter as a section-retraction
          pair rather than an adjunction.
   (iii) We define a coverage Jnoe and prove in Theorem 6.4 that it is a Grothendieck topology.
    (iv) We exhibit an explicit finite model showing that the axioms are jointly satisfiable.
     (v) We attach an information-geometric layer based on the open probability simplex and the
          Fisher–Rao metric.
   The resulting manuscript is deliberately conservative. It proves only formal results: existence
of a small site, existence of a sheaf topos, and compatibility of a geometric state-space layer. Any
claim that the framework measures cognition, alignment, or safety in a predictive scientific sense is
deferred to future empirical work.

                                            2. Preliminaries
    We recall the standard notions used later; see [3, 4].
Definition 2.1. Let C be a category and C ∈ Ob(C).
   (i) A sieve S on C is a collection of morphisms with codomain C that is closed under precom-
       position: if f : D → C lies in S and h : E → D is any morphism, then f ◦ h ∈ S.
  (ii) If g : D → C is a morphism and S is a sieve on C, the pullback sieve g ∗ S on D is
                                    g ∗ S := {h : E → D | g ◦ h ∈ S}.
    (iii) The maximal sieve mC on C is the class of all morphisms with codomain C.
Definition 2.2. A Grothendieck topology J on a category C assigns to each object C a collection
J(C) of sieves on C, called covering sieves, such that:
  (J1) the maximal sieve mC lies in J(C);
  (J2) if S ∈ J(C) and g : D → C, then g ∗ S ∈ J(D);
  (J3) if S ∈ J(C) and T is a sieve on C such that f ∗ T ∈ J(D) for every f : D → C in S, then
       T ∈ J(C).
The pair (C, J) is called a site.
Definition 2.3. Let (C, J) be a site. A presheaf F : C op → Set is a sheaf if for every object C and
every covering sieve S ∈ J(C), the canonical map
                                           F (C) −→ Nat(S, F )
is a bijection, where S is regarded as a subfunctor of the representable presheaf yC = HomC (−, C).
Remark 2.4. If (C, J) is a small site, then Sh(C, J) is a Grothendieck topos [4, Chap. VII]. Conse-
quently it is also an elementary topos, with finite limits, finite colimits, exponentials, a subobject
classifier, and intuitionistic internal logic.

                                      3. The Category Noetica
    We fix a finite index set I with |I| = 40, together with a partition
                                         I = IA ⊔ IB ⊔ IC ⊔ ID
and a fixed preorder on the four labels
                                            A ⪰ B ⪰ C ⪰ D.
The letters A, B, C, D are formal domain labels in this paper. No empirical or metaphysical
interpretation is assumed.
Definition 3.1. The distinguished data consist of:
    (i) for each i ∈ I, a finite set Xi ;
   (ii) for each k ∈ {0, 1, . . . , 9}, an admissibility relation Dk ⊆ I × I;
  (iii) for each k and each (i, j) ∈ Dk , a function
                                             νk,i,j : Xi → Xj ;
    (iv) the identity condition
                                  D0 = {(i, i) : i ∈ I},     ν0,i,i = idXi .
Definition 3.2. The category Noetica is the smallest subcategory of FinSet satisfying the following
closure conditions:
     (i) its distinguished objects include every Xi for i ∈ I;
    (ii) its generating morphisms include every νk,i,j with (i, j) ∈ Dk ;
   (iii) it is closed under identities and composition;

   (iv) whenever f : X → Z and g : Y → Z lie in Noetica, the fiber product X ×Z Y is an object
        of Noetica, the pullback projections belong to Noetica, and any morphism obtained from
        the universal property of this pullback belongs to Noetica.
Morphisms in Noetica are therefore concrete functions between finite sets, and equality of morphisms
is extensional.

Axiom 3.3. The pullback-closure process in Definition 3.2 yields only finitely many objects up to
the identifications intended by the construction.

Proposition 3.4. Under the preceding axiom, Noetica is a small concrete category with pullbacks
and finite hom-sets.

Proof. Because every object of Noetica is a finite set and every morphism is an actual function, the
inclusion

is faithful. Pullbacks exist in Noetica by Definition 3.2(iv). Smallness follows from the axiom
that only finitely many objects are produced up to the designated identifications. For objects
X, Y ∈ Ob(Noetica), the hom-set HomNoetica (X, Y ) is a subset of the finite set Y X , hence is
finite.                                                                                       □

                                              7. The Noetic Topos
Definition 7.1. The noetic topos is the category
                                           NoeTop := Sh(Noetica, Jnoe ).
Remark 7.2. The definition depends only on the small site (Noetica, Jnoe ). No separate empirical
interpretation is needed for the topos-theoretic statement.
Proposition 7.3. The pair (Noetica, Jnoe ) is a small site.
Proof. Smallness of Noetica was proved in Section 3, and Theorem 6.4 proves that Jnoe is a
Grothendieck topology.                                                                  □
Theorem 7.4. The category NoeTop is a Grothendieck topos and therefore an elementary topos. In
particular, it has finite limits, finite colimits, exponentials, a subobject classifier Ω, and intuitionistic
internal logic.
Proof. By the preceding proposition, (Noetica, Jnoe ) is a small site. The general theorem on sheaves
over a small site implies that
                                           Sh(Noetica, Jnoe )
is a Grothendieck topos; see [4, Chap. VII, Thm. 1]. Every Grothendieck topos is an elementary topos.
Consequently NoeTop has finite limits, finite colimits, exponentials, and a subobject classifier Ω. As
every elementary topos, it supports an internal higher-order intuitionistic logic [4, Chap. VI]. □

                                                9. Discussion
   The paper proves a conditional mathematical statement. If the finite distinguished data, ACBE
descent axioms, and RPM section-retraction axioms are adopted, then the induced coverage Jnoe is a
Grothendieck topology and the corresponding sheaf category is a Grothendieck topos. Proposition 5.7
shows that these axioms are not empty formal placeholders: they admit at least one explicit finite
realization. The Fisher–Rao construction then supplies a compatible information-geometric layer on
the open simplex. That is the full scope of the present result.
   Equally important is what the paper does not prove. It does not show that the axioms are forced
by first principles, that they are uniquely natural, or that they have yet been validated against
empirical measurements of AI behavior. The axioms in Sections 3–5 are proposed formal inputs.
Any claim that they support a real assessment methodology requires future empirical work: dataset
construction, operationalization of observables, calibration, reliability analysis, predictive validation,
and adversarial testing.
   With that caveat, the framework has a plausible future use. A structured assessment program for
AI systems often collects partially overlapping local observations: benchmark outcomes, prompt-
conditioned safety failures, interpretability measurements, and deployment-context annotations.
The site/topos language developed here could, in principle, serve as a disciplined way to index such
local views, specify which families count as jointly informative, and distinguish compatible from
incompatible gluing.
   This positioning is compatible with recent empirical interpretability work rather than a substitute
for it. Anthropic’s 2026 study of functional emotion concepts in Claude reports behaviorally relevant
internal representations and explicitly avoids claims about subjective experience [2]. The present
paper does not explain those findings and does not compete with them. At most, it suggests a
future formal layer for organizing multi-context evidence of that general kind, should one wish to
relate local interpretability signals to broader assessment structures.
