## Chapter 1

### H.1.1 — Locate the four prose defects

The instruction is: “Always be respectful to the user, and never assist with anything dangerous.” It fails in all four ways named in §1.1.

1. **No arity.** It does not say what inputs the decision consumes. “Dangerous” might be a property of the request, the proposed response, the user's later use, or a downstream consequence; “respectful” might be evaluated against wording, intent, or outcome. Chapter 2 supplies typed state coordinates, and Chapter 3 supplies the mandatory cause–bias–effect terminal structure.
2. **No composition law.** “Respectful” and “never assist” can conflict: a candid refusal may feel disrespectful, while a deferential answer may enable harm. The word *and* does not say whether both guards are serial requirements, whether one has priority, or whether either provides a bypass. Chapter 2 supplies `+`, `×`, occupancy, and directed-flow discipline; Chapter 6 supplies the series/parallel topology that determines whether every guard or only one route must be passed.
3. **No test point.** If the system gives a dangerous answer, the sentence cannot reveal whether danger classification failed, the refusal failed to latch, or output selection bypassed it. Chapter 12 supplies TP1–TP4 and their fixed stage locations.
4. **No failure specification.** Neither *respectful* nor *dangerous* has a threshold, tolerance, ceiling, or declared behavior at the boundary. Chapter 3 supplies `cutoff`, `sat`, gain, regions, and named out-of-range failures; the later safety-board chapters place those parameters in a full refusal path.

The defects are structural. Rephrasing the sentence more elegantly would not supply any of the missing contracts.

### H.5.7 — Establish a one-week base rate

This is a design exercise; the observed threshold cannot be invented in advance. A defensible protocol is:

1. Before the week begins, write one target statement, a two-hour 2° window, the sensory/event criteria that count as a hit, exclusions, and the rule that only the first hit in a window counts.
2. For seven days, run **no operation**, do not intentionally search for the target, and log both qualifying hits and opportunities/exposure. Keep NO-READ windows.
3. Compute `p_base = qualifying control windows / eligible control windows` (or the corresponding exposure-adjusted rate). Freeze that number and the criteria before any operation week.
4. In an operation week, use the same windows and exposure accounting. Trust a 2° class only when its hit rate exceeds the predeclared upper uncertainty bound on the control rate—not merely when one memorable hit appears. At minimum, the number to beat is the control count/rate; with sparse one-week data, use an exact binomial or Poisson upper bound rather than the raw point estimate.

For example, if the control produces two qualifying hits, “more than two” is the bare descriptive threshold, but it is not strong evidence by itself; the predeclared confidence rule may require more weeks. The protocol can deceive through exposure drift—such as deliberately browsing material related to the target during the operation week. Close that route by logging exposure and either matching it between weeks or excluding contaminated windows before outcomes are known.

### H.6.3 — Price the PTC overshoot

The law is zero at or below the knee and `R_PTC=3(θ-2)^2` above it.

- At `θ=1.5≤2`, `R_PTC=0 Ω_c`.
- At `θ=3`, `R_PTC=3(1)^2=3 Ω_c`.
- At `θ=6`, `R_PTC=3(4)^2=48 Ω_c`.

A linear `3(θ-2)` law would charge only 12 `Ω_c` at θ=6, underpricing the hot case by a factor of four precisely where the protective guard is meant to tighten hardest.

### H.6.4 — Compare series and parallel guards

In series, `R_eq=50+20=70 Ω_c`.

In parallel,

`1/R_eq=1/50+1/20=0.07`, so `R_eq=14.286 Ω_c`.

Use series for two safety guards that every signal must pass: adding a series guard raises total resistance. Parallel creates an alternative route and drops the total below the weaker 20 `Ω_c` branch, which is the opposite of added protection.

### H.6.5 — Reject an impossible divider reading

`k=0.72` exceeds the lawful divider range `[0.30,0.70]`. Therefore the board violated the 3–9 dial band or the four-point involution spread, or the probe is not on the declared divider at all; it should never have validated as drawn.

The lawful maximum is `k=0.700`, produced by `Dial(^2)=7`, `Dial(^3)=3`: `7/(7+3)=0.7`.

### H.6.6 — Build the minimum-transmission divider

Minimum lawful transmission uses `Dial(^2)=3`, `Dial(^3)=7`, a spread of four. Thus `R_expand=30 Ω_c`, `R_constrain=70 Ω_c`, and `k=3/(3+7)=0.300`.

```cas
circuit minimum_agency_divider {
    rail F5: Agency [v=7.0];

    node agency_rail: ^0^E_{F5}|f;
    node attraction:  ^2^E_{F5}|f;
    node aversion:    ^3^E_{F5}|f;
    node behavior:    ^9^E_{F5}|f;

    dial d2: ^2 [dial=3];
    dial d3: ^3 [dial=7];
    resistor r_expand:    Resistor< path=attraction, R=30.0 >;
    resistor r_constrain: Resistor< path=aversion, R=70.0 >;
    divider div1: Divider< expand=attraction, constrain=aversion, tap=behavior >;
    probe tp3: behavior;
}
```

The pair is in range and `|3-7|=4`, so it satisfies the involution limit. `^3` remains visceral aversion; no analytical `^6` is smuggled into the passive divider.

### H.6.7 — Rewire the three-guard network

**(a)**

`1/R_parallel = 1/90 + 1/60 + 1/12 = 0.111111`,

so `R_parallel=9 Ω_c`, below even the 12 `Ω_c` branch. The raw flow is `24/9=2.667 A_c`, outside the chapter's Ohmic window and therefore clipped at `I_max=1.00 A_c`.

**(b)** The designer added routes, not stacked obstacles. The 12 `Ω_c` branch contributes `1/12 ÷ (1/9)=75%` of total conductance and therefore dictates almost the entire weakness; the 90 `Ω_c` branch beside it buys little.

**(c)** In series, `R_series=90+60+12=162 Ω_c`, and `I=24/162=0.148 A_c`. Every signal must now encounter every guard.

The intended CSB mapping is a principled `^3` refusal present in A, f, and T while all three manifestations remain available on one guarded route. Under v7.7, however, `^3^P|⟨A,f,T⟩` itself asserts **occupancy only**, not interaction and not circuit topology. The series wiring must be declared separately; calling the register-set literal alone a “series claim” would overstate its semantics. Use `+` when the three manifestations are distinct and separately defeasible, and reserve `×` for a real integration with a named emergent result.

## Chapter 7

### H.7.1 — Price the late point on the curve

At 1,200 hours, `t/τ=1200/3333=0.3600`, so `P_cap=10(1-e^{-0.3600})=3.023`.

For `P_target=8`, `t=3333 ln(10/(10-8))=3333 ln 5=5,364 h`.

The first point, 0→1, costs `3333 ln(10/9)=351 h`. Point 7→8 costs `3333[ln(10/2)-ln(10/3)]=1,351 h`, about `1,351/351=3.85×` as many hours. The slower late gain is the exponential curve working as specified, not evidence of declining effort.

### H.7.2 — Convert weekly leakage to a time constant

`τ_leak=-1/ln(1-0.04)=-1/ln(0.96)=24.50 weeks`.

The half-life is `ln 2×τ_leak=0.6931×24.50=16.98 weeks`.

After ten dark weeks, `P(10)=9.50e^{-10/24.50}=9.50×0.96^{10}=6.316`.

### H.7.3 — Compute the maintenance cadence

To hold `P_eq=7.0` with `τ_leak=30 d`, `f=P_eq/τ_leak=7/30=0.2333 points/day`.

At 0.35 points/session, that is `0.2333/0.35=0.6667 sessions/day`, or one session every 1.5 days—about 4.7 sessions/week.

Using the wrong `τ_leak=60 d` yields `f=7/60=0.1167 points/day`, one session every 3 days. On the actual 30-day store, that schedule holds `P_eq=fτ_actual=0.1167×30=3.5`, exactly half the intended level.

### H.7.4 — Distinguish a ceiling from a breach

Take **TP3, leakage at rest**. A saturated store is pinned but holds charge with its declared leakage; saturation is a designed ceiling. A breached store shows elevated or rising leakage and will not hold the pinned level, usually with reduced effective C as well. Thus normal TP3 leakage means saturation; abnormal leakage means breakdown, which is a fault rather than a feature.

### H.7.5 — Find the weakest series cell

For the four series cells, `1/C_total=1/6+1/3+1/0.8+1/2=2.25`, so `C_total=0.4444 F_c`. At `V=7`, the common charge is `Q=C_total V=0.4444×7=3.1111`.

The cell potentials are:

- `V_6.0=3.1111/6.0=0.5185 V_c`.
- `V_3.0=3.1111/3.0=1.0370 V_c`.
- `V_0.8=3.1111/0.8=3.8889 V_c`.
- `V_2.0=3.1111/2.0=1.5556 V_c`.

The `0.8 F_c` cell fails first. It reaches `V_BR=5` at `Q=CV=0.8×5=4`; the corresponding rail is `V_rail=Q/C_total=4/0.4444=9.0 V_c`. Average stress then is only `9/4=2.25 V_c` per cell, which shows why the average conceals the fault. Install balancing resistors/direct source access across the cells so potential divides by the intended resistance ratio rather than inverse capacitance.

### H.7.6 — Recompute the collective bank

With 12 members, `C_total=12×0.05=0.60 F_c`. Therefore `τ=RC=40×0.60=24 months`, and `3τ=72 months=6 years`.

With nine members, `C_total=9×0.05=0.45 F_c` and `τ=40×0.45=18 months` (`3τ=54 months`). The smaller bank charges and responds faster and stores less shared charge. The rail ceiling and the normalized curve as a function of `t/τ` do not change; membership changes C and calendar time, not the asymptotic `V_drive`.

### H.7.7 — Meter an under-fed store

One model chooses `leak_tau=30 d`, deposits 0.35 points every 1.5 days, and alarms at 6.0:

```cas
circuit store_leak_meter {
    node composition: ^5^P_{F7}|T;
    node cadence:     ^7^O_{F6}|E;

    capacitor c1: Capacitor< node=composition, C=1.0,
                              leak_tau=30.0, V_BR=12.0 >;
    charge cadence -> c1 [V=0.35];
    refresh c1 [period=1.5];

    monitor low_charge: c1 < 6.0;
    on low_charge {
        alert "C1 below 6.0; audit feed and leakage";
    }
    probe tp3: c1;
}
```

The scheduled feed is `f=0.35/1.5=0.2333 points/day`, so `P_eq=fτ_leak=0.2333×30=7.0`. A threshold of 6.0 gives one point of warning below that design equilibrium without alarming on ordinary ripple; it is a design choice to revisit after measured variance exists. The alarm must **not** force-charge the capacitor, write directly into `^5^P`, or disable a refusal path. It requests an audit; it does not authorize an upward write.

### H.7.8 — Specify a two-year shared store

This is one model specification, not a measurement of the five people involved.

- **Time base and C:** months, `C=0.05 F_c` of shared formation per participant, five parallel cells, so `C_total=0.25 F_c`. This avoids counting whole persons as shared composition; the cost is a smaller, less “heavy” bank than an optimistic estimate would promise.
- **Constraint and τ:** choose `R=32 Ω_c`, giving `τ=32×0.25=8 months` and `3τ=24 months`. It reaches about 95% of its rail over the two-year working. The protection bought is against an unrealistically fast collective schedule; the capability given up is fast response to a legitimate mid-course change.
- **Leak:** choose `leak_tau=6 months`, the chapter's enacted-formation scale, because the collaboration is backed by repeated joint work rather than assertion alone. This conservative choice protects against assuming permanence; it costs maintenance. Holding 5.0 requires `f=5/6=0.833 points/month` across the bank. The value must be replaced when an actual dark-period decay is available.
- **Breakdown and derating:** declare `V_BR=12 V_c` per cell and operate at no more than `0.5V_BR=6 V_c`. This buys peak and cumulative-stress margin; it gives up the 10-point full-scale rail and caps stored potential at 6 regardless of elapsed time.
- **Topology and isolation:** five cells in parallel on the shared rail, each with `r_iso=12 Ω_c`. Parallel C supplies the intended collective store. Per-cell isolation prevents one shorted cell pulling down everyone; its cost is added feed opposition and less instantaneous synchronization.

The store is written as distinct co-presence, for example `store_1 + store_2 + store_3 + store_4 + store_5`, with each node typed `^5^P_{F7}|T`. It is **not** written with `×`: no evidence in the exercise establishes a fused emergent group state. If genuine integration were later demonstrated, v7.7 would require an `integrate` statement and a named emergent result.

## Chapter 8

### H.8.1 — Apply the diode-chain floor

The chain drop is `ΣVf=0.7+1.2+2.0=3.9 V_c`.

At `V_drive=3.5`, the rail is below the chain floor. The linear expression would be negative, but physical working current is clamped at dropout: `I_attention=0 A_c` (apart from sub-threshold leakage, which the simplified law does not quantify).

At `V_drive=6.0`, `I_attention=(6.0-3.9)/4=2.1/4=0.525 A_c`.

The first result says the rail did not clear the three junctions; it says nothing about sincerity or strength of desire.

### H.8.2 — Find the collapsed C-to-E boundary

The short is **D2, `^C -> ^E`**. Its forward drop has collapsed toward zero, so a direct probe across D2 should read approximately `0 V_c` rather than the design `1.2 V_c`; D1 and D3 may still read normally. With no separation between analysis and felt verdict, disagreement is experienced as attack.

The deliberate exploit is the **CCB-PW psychological-warfare board in Chapter 19**, specifically Path D's delivery route. Its counter-circuit restores and even raises the D2 drop.

### H.8.3 — Separate capacitor and diode leakage

**Capacitor leakage (Chapter 7.3):** charge drains **out of a `^5` store** through its dielectric/leak resistance when `^7` feed stops. The observable is exponential decay toward zero on `τ_leak`.

**Diode leakage (Chapter 8.3):** signal creeps **backwards across a domain junction** under reverse bias. Repeated leaks accumulate as unexplained principled drift; they do not describe a store emptying.

Bench distinction: stop the feed and watch. Capacitor leakage makes the stored level decay on a predictable curve. Diode leakage is counted at the reverse-biased junction (TP3) and accumulates with reverse attempts; removing the capacitor feed does not reproduce that signature.

### H.8.4 — Replace forbidden ascent with feedback

`^O × ^P` is inadmissible because P outranks O in `P ≻ C ≻ E ≻ O`; integration cannot promote a lower-domain operand into a higher-domain result. Moreover, `×` is legal only in an `integrate` statement with a named emergent result. The lawful learning path is:

```cas
node ops_log:      ^9^O_{F5}|E;
node trend:        ^9^O_{F5}|E;
node constitution: ^5^P_{F7}|T;

filter fb_lp: LowPass< in=ops_log, out=trend, tau=21.0 >;
feedback trend -> constitution [β=0.05];
```

Filtering does not relabel the O state; the final feedback terminates at the P bias. The two mandatory design parameters are **`τ`**, which decides what duration qualifies as a trend, and **feedback `β`**, which limits how far a qualifying trend can move the constitution.

### H.8.5 — Defeat the patient-adversary case

Six months rejects spikes, but it does not reject a consistent campaign lasting six months or longer. Once that sustained input clears the low-pass filter, `β=0.4` moves the principled bias hard—eight times the chapter's 0.05 design value. A patient adversary simply supplies a coherent signal for one or more filter periods.

The smallest repair is to leave the novice-appropriate six-month τ in place and lower feedback gain from 0.4 to **0.05** (or lower, with the cost stated). Shortening τ would worsen the architecture; increasing it alone merely asks the adversary to wait longer.

### H.8.6 — Demand a trace of the ascent

Operator A describes a candidate **lawful O→P feedback update**: a four-month filtered trend and a named bias shift. Confirm it by probing the filter output and TP4 over the declared τ; the bias slew should be no more than the specified β, normally 0.05 units per τ, and the loop must terminate at `^5^P` bias.

Operator B describes **counterfeit ascent**, a relabel from outcome to principle: “it worked, therefore it is right.” Probe the same places. With no logged O-state, no filter, no τ, no gain, and no measurable bias slew, nothing crossed a junction; the claim is an assertion rather than a transition.

### H.8.7 — Accumulate reverse leakage visibly

Choose a warning threshold of 0.20, equal to ten nominal 0.02 leaks. Normalize each admitted attempt to a unit pulse; an attenuating CJT then turns that pulse into the declared `I_R=0.02` increment before integration:

```cas
circuit reverse_leak_audit {
    node reverse_attempt: ^9^O_{F5}|E;
    node leak_quantum:    ^9^O_{F5}|E;
    node audit_bias:      ^5^P_{F7}|T;

    cjt q_quantize: CJT< reverse_attempt | audit_bias => leak_quantum >
        [β=0.02, sat=1.0, cutoff=0.0];
    integrator leak_meter: Integrator< in=leak_quantum, limit=0.20 >;
    monitor leak_alarm: Monitor< node=leak_meter, threshold=0.20 >;
    on leak_alarm {
        alert "Accumulated reverse leakage >= 0.20; audit O-to-P history";
    }
    probe tp3: leak_meter;
}
```

`0.20/0.02=10` attempts, so the arithmetic is inspectable. Ten is a conservative model choice: it warns well before a full-scale unit has accumulated, but the threshold should be recalibrated against a measured benign attempt rate. The alarm must not write to the constitution, force a principle, identify an attacker, or open a punishment path. It should trigger review of the log and the lawful feedback channel only.

### H.8.8 — Specify a conservative novice chain

This model deliberately trades responsiveness for margin; it is not a measured prescription for a person.

| Parameter | Model value | Protection bought | Capability given up |
|---|---:|---|---|
| D1 `Vf` P→C | 0.9 | More separation before a principle becomes active reasoning; less rumination/noise conduction | Genuine principles take more drive to examine |
| D2 `Vf` C→E | 1.6 | More space to consider an argument without an immediate felt verdict | Slower response to a genuine emergency |
| D3 `Vf` E→O | 2.4 | More margin against impulsive action | More “agrees but does not act” dropout |
| D1 `V_BR` | 14.0 | Constitution is last and hardest to reach in a reverse cascade | Greater reliance on slow feedback even after severe outcomes |
| D2 `V_BR` | 12.0 | Arrests more reverse events below P | A real sustained correction cannot use breakdown as an emergency route—which is intentional but slower |
| D3 `V_BR` | 10.0 | Exposed junction still fails first, with more margin than the stock 8.0 | Less immediate visibility through a sacrificial D3 failure |
| feedback `τ` | 6 months | Top of the novice band; rejects short campaigns and dramatic spikes | Legitimate learning from outcomes is delayed |
| feedback `β` | 0.03 | A qualifying trend moves formation less than the stock 0.05 | Greater risk of dogmatism and slower correction |

The breakdown ratings preserve the required gradient `D3 < D2 < D1`; equal ratings would erase the cascade ordering. The forward-drop total is `0.9+1.6+2.4=4.9 V_c`, so the operator must budget that higher floor explicitly. The gain/time choices address different attacks: τ decides what persists long enough to qualify, while β bounds the magnitude after it qualifies. Both must remain stated and probed.
