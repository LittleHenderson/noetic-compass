# Appendix H Fragment — Worked Answers for Chapters 9–16

This fragment contains the complete model answers for Chapters 9 through 16. Numerical values follow the chapters' stated design assumptions; they are not empirical measurements. Where an exercise permits several sound designs, the answer gives one defensible model and labels every added assumption. The Chapter 15–16 material preserves the manuscript's detection-only boundary: it answers recognition and counter-circuit questions without supplying the operational layer deliberately omitted from those chapters.

## Chapter 9

### H.9.1 — Compare behavioural and principled inductance

For the behavioural routine, Chapter 9 assigns the Operational domain `k_d = 0.5 Ω_c`:

```text
L   = k_d H_practice = 0.5 × 900 = 450 h·Ω_c
τ   = L/R = 450/30 = 15 h
3τ  = 45 h
```

For the same 900 hours treated as a Principled commitment, `k_d = 4.0 Ω_c`:

```text
L   = 4.0 × 900 = 3,600 h·Ω_c
τ   = 3,600/30 = 120 h
3τ  = 360 h
```

The ratio is `360/45 = 8`. At the same path resistance, the principled transition needs eight times the calendar allotted to the behavioural one. Rehearsal hours alone therefore under-budget the deeper-domain transition by exactly the ratio of the depth coefficients.

### H.9.2 — Diagnose the low-resistance stall

Before the change:

```text
τ_before       = L/R = 2,400/60 = 40 h
I_final,before = V/R = 18/60 = 0.30 A_c
```

After the change:

```text
τ_after       = 2,400/15 = 160 h
I_final,after = 18/15 = 1.20 A_c
```

Reducing `R` by four made settling four times **slower** while making the requested final flow four times deeper. The latter may clip the path rather than yield a usable 1.20 `A_c`. This is the **Low-`R` stall** row: the operator bought depth, not speed, and enlarged `τ` while reasoning that they had shortened it.

### H.9.3 — Solve the two taper bounds

For a linear removal, `|di/dt| = ΔI/dt` and

```text
V_kick = L ΔI/dt.
```

At the 3× ceiling, `V_kick,max = 3 × 20 = 60 V_c`:

```text
dt_min,3× = (5,000 × 0.80)/60 = 66.67 h.
```

At the 1× working bound, `V_kick,max = 20 V_c`:

```text
dt_min,1× = (5,000 × 0.80)/20 = 200 h.
```

I would use the 200-hour bound for an actual removal, provided a substitute path is already conducting and coupled paths have also been checked. It holds the kick to the rail rather than merely below the ejection ceiling. The equation can certify the rate budget; it cannot certify the suitability of the substitute or the human situation.

### H.9.4 — Type the kick correctly

`^3` Negative is the Idea the Mind is adverse to and repelled by. The immediate back-EMF response is therefore a felt recoil at the Evaluative level, correctly written `^3^E_{F3}|f`. It is aversion generated by the rate of interruption, not an argument about the old path.

`^6` Male is decomposition—one Idea taking another apart. A literal such as `^6^E_{F6}|A` would claim that the person is analytically examining the transition. That may arrive later, after the kick has fallen far enough for the change to become an object of reflection, but it is not the kick itself. Substituting `^6` for `^3` would hide the component that the taper is sized to control.

### H.9.5 — Design the method taper

The old method is Cognitive, so `k_d = 2.0 Ω_c`:

```text
L       = 2.0 × 2,500 = 5,000 h·Ω_c
τ       = 5,000/40 = 125 h
3τ      = 375 h
I_final = 24/40 = 0.60 A_c
```

The 2× kick budget is `48 V_c`, hence

```text
|di/dt|_max = 48/5,000 = 0.0096 A_c/h
dt_min      = 0.60/0.0096 = 62.5 h.
```

One canonical fragment is:

```cas
board method_transition {
    input: cue;
    output: routed_flow;

    node substrate: ^5^C_{F6}|A;
    node old_path:  ^9_old^O_{F6}|E;
    node new_path:  ^9_new^O_{F6}|E;
    node felt_kick: ^3^E_{F3}|f;

    inductor coil:  Inductor< path=old_path, L=5000.0 >;
    resistor r_ctx: Resistor< path=old_path, R=40.0 >;
    diode flyback:  Diode< ^E -> ^O > [Vf=2.0];

    hold until new_path > 0.0;
    constraint taper_rate in [0.0, 0.0096];
    monitor m_kick: felt_kick [threshold=48.0];
    on m_kick { alert "V_kick over budget - lengthen the taper"; }

    probe tp1: taper_rate;
    probe tp2: old_path;
    probe tp3: felt_kick;
    probe tp4: new_path;
}
```

The diode descends `^E -> ^O`; reversing it would be an illegal ascent. `hold until` enforces substitute-before-cut, while the rate constraint and monitor independently enforce and observe the 2× budget.

### H.9.6 — Include the coupled partner

The operator's linear taper has

```text
|di₁/dt| = 0.60/60 = 0.0100 A_c/h
V_kick,1 = L₁|di₁/dt| = 3,200 × 0.0100 = 32.0 V_c
32/24 = 1.333× rail.
```

The mutual inductance and induced voltage are

```text
M  = k√(L₁L₂) = 0.90√(3,200 × 2,000)
   = 2,276.84 h·Ω_c
V₂ = M|di₁/dt| = 2,276.84 × 0.0100
   = 22.77 V_c = 0.949× the 24 V_c rail.
```

The partner's voltage is smaller, but the transition is structurally more abrupt for them: the operator selected the rate and installed a substitute path; the partner did neither. Their induced change arrives as an externally imposed derivative with no local flyback route.

To hold the partner below `0.3 × 24 = 7.2 V_c`:

```text
7.2 > M(0.60/dt)
dt > (2,276.84 × 0.60)/7.2 = 189.74 h.
```

A 190-hour taper is the smallest rounded schedule that clears the bound. It is `190/60 = 3.17` times the original duration—about 130 additional hours. The circuit can quantify coupling and rate; it cannot decide what the change means to the partner, whether they consent to it, or what substitute would fit their life.

## Chapter 10

### H.10.1 — Size gain from arriving cause

The cause reaching the first active stage is

```text
I_cause = (V_drive − ΣVf)/R_constraint
        = (15 − 3.9)/55
        = 11.1/55
        = 0.20182 A_c.
```

Thus

```text
β_required = 0.72/0.20182 = 3.568.
```

A production stage needs 25% headroom, so

```text
sat ≥ 1.25 × 0.72 = 0.90.
```

I would specify `beta=3.57, sat=0.90` if the substrate's physical `I_c(max)` is above 0.90. At unity gain, the rail itself must supply the whole 0.72 `A_c`:

```text
V_drive,unity = I R + ΣVf = 0.72 × 55 + 3.9 = 43.5 V_c.
```

The gain buys the effect that 28.5 additional cognitive volts of drive would otherwise have to supply; it does not buy a better reason or remove the constraint.

### H.10.2 — Compare the two latch margins

For `sat=0.65`:

```text
c_sat = sat/β = 0.65/2.5 = 0.260
M     = (sat − cutoff)/β = (0.65 − 0.15)/2.5 = 0.200.
```

For `sat=0.95`:

```text
c_sat = 0.95/2.5 = 0.380
M     = (0.95 − 0.15)/2.5 = 0.320.
```

The high-ceiling stage exposes `(0.320 − 0.200)/0.200 = 60%` more steerable cause-space; equivalently, the low-ceiling stage removes 37.5% of the high stage's window. The smaller `M` is the stronger guard because it reaches a plateau sooner, and on that plateau `∂effect/∂cause = 0`: further argument no longer controls the output.

### H.10.3 — Decompose terminal resistance

The intrinsic and network terms are

```text
rπ_intrinsic = β/g_m = 2.4/0.30 = 8.0 Ω_c
R_bias       = rπ_terminal − rπ_intrinsic = 11.0 − 8.0 = 3.0 Ω_c.
```

After isolation reduces the reachable network term to 1.0:

```text
rπ_terminal,new = 8.0 + 1.0 = 9.0 Ω_c
percentage fall = (11.0 − 9.0)/11.0 = 18.18%.
```

The outside party moved `R_bias`. The intrinsic `8.0` is fixed by the stage's two gains and is not an externally accessible series term in this model.

Had the same stage declared `rπ=6.0`, it would imply

```text
R_bias = 6.0 − 8.0 = −2.0 Ω_c,
```

which is not a buildable bias network. The compiler would still accept the three finite, individually in-range numbers because it does not perform the cross-parameter subtraction.

### H.10.4 — Keep analysis ahead of refusal

`^6` Male is decomposition: the evaluation stage takes the proposal apart and tests its parts. It therefore runs at unity so that it neither invents nor suppresses considerations. `^3` Negative is the Idea the Mind is adverse to and repelled by: the refusal stage is the visceral will-not produced after evaluation, and it is deliberately driven into saturation.

Swapping them breaks the circuit, not merely the labels. A `^3` first stage recoils before any decomposition can run, so the board rejects by reaction. A `^6` final “refusal” remains responsive to additional cause and can be argued continuously; it never acquires the zero-slope plateau that makes a refusal hold. The safe order is therefore `^6` evaluation followed by `^3` latching refusal.

### H.10.5 — Compare eight short stages with four stronger ones

For eight stages at `β=1.4`:

```text
β_total = 1.4^8 = 14.7579
N/S     = nε = 8 × 0.03 = 0.24 = 24%
low     = 14.7579 × 0.88^8 ≈ 5.31
high    = 14.7579 × 1.12^8 ≈ 36.54.
```

The tolerance envelope is therefore about `0.360×` to `2.476×` nominal, a `6.89:1` high-to-low spread.

To preserve the same total gain in four equal stages,

```text
β_each  = 14.7579^(1/4) = 1.96
β_total = 1.96^4 = 14.7579
N/S     = 4 × 0.03 = 12%
low     = 14.7579 × 0.88^4 ≈ 8.85
high    = 14.7579 × 1.12^4 ≈ 23.22.
```

That envelope is `0.600×` to `1.574×` nominal, a `2.62:1` spread. I would build the four-stage chain: it halves contamination and greatly narrows tolerance drift. The trade is that each stage must carry `β=1.96` rather than 1.4, so each has less saturation and framing margin and needs closer headroom checks.

### H.10.6 — Design a guarded two-stage signal chain

The arriving cause is

```text
I_cause = (9 − 3.9)/30 = 5.1/30 = 0.170 A_c.
```

The two active production stages need

```text
β_total = 0.40/0.170 = 2.35294.
```

An equal split gives `β₁ = β₂ = √2.35294 = 1.5339`. Stage 1 expects `0.170 × 1.5339 = 0.2608`, so the 1.25 production rule requires `sat₁ ≥ 0.326`; choose 0.35. Stage 2 expects 0.400, so choose `sat₂ = 1.25 × 0.400 = 0.50`. The ceilings differ because the second stage carries the larger expected peak; neither production stage is meant to latch.

The separate decision branch is the `^3^P` guard. Choose `beta=2.0`, `sat=0.65`, `cutoff=0.15`, `g_m=0.40 S_c`, and `rπ=8.0 Ω_c`. The guard is in the `rπ_terminal ≥ 6.0` principled band. Here `β/g_m = 5.0`, so the declared terminal value explicitly includes `R_bias = 8.0 − 5.0 = 3.0`; `β` and `g_m` set the intrinsic junction and `rπ` records the additional network term. That statement resolves, rather than hides, the apparent three-parameter over-specification.

```cas
board guarded_two_stage_delivery {
    input: intent;
    output: act;

    node intent:       ^0^P_{F2}|A;
    node principles:   ^5^P_{F7}|T;
    node refusal:      ^3^P_{F7}|⟨A,f,T⟩;
    node passed:       ^0_passed^P_{F2}|A;
    node working_bias: ^5^E_{F6}|A;
    node mid:          ^9_mid^E_{F5}|A;
    node act:          ^9_act^O_{F5}|E;

    cjt q_guard: CJT< intent | principles => refusal >
                 [beta=2.0, sat=0.65, cutoff=0.15,
                  g_m=0.40, rπ=8.0, region=active];
    mux pass_gate: MUX< inputs=[intent, refusal], select=refusal,
                         outputs=[passed] >;

    diode d_pc: Diode< ^P -> ^C > [Vf=0.7];
    diode d_ce: Diode< ^C -> ^E > [Vf=1.2];
    cjt q1: CJT< passed | principles => mid >
            [beta=1.5339, sat=0.35, cutoff=0.05, region=active];
    diode d_eo: Diode< ^E -> ^O > [Vf=2.0];
    cjt q2: CJT< mid | working_bias => act >
            [beta=1.5339, sat=0.50, cutoff=0.05, region=active];

    probe tp1: principles;
    probe tp2: refusal;
    probe tp3: mid;
    probe tp4: act;
}
```

All diodes descend in `P ≻ C ≻ E ≻ O`; both production cutoffs are below their ceilings. The guard's low ceiling is intentional decision saturation, while both signal stages are kept active and measurable.

### H.10.7 — Repair the inherited cascade

Replacing stage 5's nominal `β=3.0` with 5.0 gives

```text
β_total,new = 27.65 × (5/3) = 46.08.
```

The stage count did not change, so the contamination model still gives `N/S = 5 × 0.03 = 15%`; raising one gain does not clean a cascade.

If the measured output rises little and then stops, first distinguish two region faults:

- **Stage 5 saturation.** TP3 at stage 5 is pinned at its `sat` and does not move when its TP2 cause is raised. More `β` only drives farther into the plateau.
- **An upstream cutoff or saturation.** Walk TP2 from the input end. A zero cause identifies the first cutoff stage; a constant cause whose upstream TP3 is pinned identifies the first saturated stage. Everything downstream can test healthy while receiving no variable signal.

Raising the last stage was the worst choice because it sees the largest accumulated input, has the least headroom, and cannot restore information already clipped upstream.

After restoring every existing stage to its active region, a measured gain of 22 needs only

```text
β_repair = 27.65/22 = 1.2568.
```

One sixth, active-region stage at `β=1.257` reaches the target without any stage above 3.0. It costs one additional settling interval and raises modelled contamination from 15% to `6 × 3% = 18%`, exactly the book's cascade-depth limit. A four-stage redistribution is another valid design if its ceilings permit it, but it requires retuning and re-verifying four stages. Neither cascade calculation can tell the operator whether the operation is justified, consented to, or should be run at all.

## Chapter 11

### H.11.1 — Repair the unreachable fuse

The guarded node can never exceed its CJT's `sat=0.78`, while the fuse waits for 0.90. Therefore the fuse never opens on **any** input; the unreachable-fuse failure is guaranteed by the two ceilings.

Two repairs are possible:

1. Put the threshold below the guarded ceiling, for example 0.75. That is reachable, but it falls below the normal 0.85–0.95 fuse band and risks nuisance trips.
2. Keep `threshold=0.90` and move the fuse in series with an unsaturated downstream enforcement path whose verified ceiling exceeds 0.90.

I would ship the second. It preserves the normal threshold and separates the decision stage's deliberate `sat` from the permanent enforcement element. The acceptance test must drive the new path above 0.90 once and confirm permanent blocking; “it has never tripped” is not evidence that it can.

### H.11.2 — Compute oscillator charge and ripple

Use `a = e^(−T/45)`, `Q_max = 0.6/(1−a)`, and `Q_min = Q_max a = Q_max − 0.6`.

| `T` | `a` | `Q_max` | `Q_min` | Absolute ripple | Ripple / `Q_max` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 d | 0.8559 | 4.165 | 3.565 | 0.600 | 14.41% |
| 15 d | 0.7165 | 2.117 | 1.517 | 0.600 | 28.35% |
| 45 d | 0.3679 | 0.949 | 0.349 | 0.600 | 63.21% |

The design rule is `T ≤ τ_leak/3 = 15 d`, so both 7 days and 15 days satisfy it, the latter exactly at the boundary. At 45 days the operator sees separate pulses and reports “starting over every time”; there is no stable plateau, only 63% ripple.

### H.11.3 — Detect movement without reading level

Use a highpass, defined from the existing lowpass as

```text
highpass(x) = x − lowpass(x).
```

It reports change in the principled commitment while remaining blind to its absolute position. The serious defect would be putting that highpass on the Operational-to-Principled feedback path. That path needs a lowpass so only a sustained trend reaches the bias; the highpass passes the single dramatic spike that the lowpass was installed to destroy, allowing one result to rewrite principles.

### H.11.4 — Separate differential signal from common-mode framing

The honest differential term is

```text
A_d(V+ − V−) = 1,000 × 0.05 = 50.
```

Adding 4.0 to both inputs gives a common-mode average of 4.0, hence

```text
A_cm(V+ + V−)/2 = 50 × 4.0 = 200.
```

The unwanted term is `200/50 = 4` times the honest term; `CMRR = 1000/50 = 20`.

At the same input matching—and therefore the same CMRR—raising overall open-loop gain by four scales both gains: `A_d=4000`, `A_cm=200`. The terms become 200 and 800, still 1:4, and are more likely to clip. If one imagines holding `A_cm=50` while changing only `A_d`, one has improved CMRR, not merely turned up the gain. The correct physical knob is matched bias/common-mode rejection; gain cannot repair a comparison contaminated at both inputs.

### H.11.5 — Match the source and load

Without a transformer,

```text
r = Z_load/Z_source = 9/900 = 0.010
efficiency = 4r/(1+r)^2 = 0.04/1.0201 = 0.03921 = 3.92%.
```

The matching ratio is

```text
n = √(Z_source/Z_load) = √(900/9) = 10
Z_reflected = Z_load n² = 9 × 10² = 900 Ω_c.
```

At that ratio,

```text
V₂ = V₁/n = 48/10 = 4.8 V_c,
```

which clears the `ΣVf=3.9 V_c` descent floor by 0.9 `V_c`. The largest permitted step-down is

```text
n_max = V₁/ΣVf = 48/3.9 = 12.31.
```

The match therefore has 2.31 ratio units, or 23.1%, of turn-ratio headroom before operational output disappears; expressed at the secondary, the immediate voltage headroom is 0.9 `V_c`.

### H.11.6 — Stop paying for a plateau

For a weekly pulse, `T=7 d`, `τ_leak=30 d`, and `q=0.8`:

```text
a = e^(−7/30) = 0.7919
Q_max = 0.8/(1 − 0.7919) = 3.844
Q_min = 3.844 − 0.8 = 3.044
ripple/Q_max = 0.8/3.844 = 20.81%.
```

The plateau stopped rising because the recurrence reached its fixed point: each pulse now replaces what leaked during the week. More elapsed time at the same pulse and period does not raise that fixed point.

Raising duty from 0.15 to 0.70 violates the chapter's 0.10–0.25 design band. At duty ≥0.6 the waveform is effectively a DC level, not a patterned `^7` Rhythm. Contrast collapses, so each exposure carries less `^4` Vibration/impression even as the budget rises.

The TP1 check is to stop the external clock:

- If the cadence continues from an internal source, the pattern is entrained; external spend can stop.
- If the cadence stops and the store decays, the rhythm was rented; doubling spend only rents it more expensively.

The corrected clock keeps the already lawful weekly period and duty, sustains for at least `3τ_leak = 90 d`, and caps a reachable, unsaturated enacted path:

```cas
board corrected_clock_and_cap {
    input: claim;
    output: carried;

    node claim:   ^0^C_{F2}|A;
    node cadence: ^7^C_{F6}|A;
    node carried: ^9^O_{F4}|E;

    osc clock: Oscillator< signal=claim, source=cadence,
                           period=7.0, duty=0.15, sustain=90.0 >;
    fuse cap: Fuse< path=carried, threshold=0.90,
                    action=permanent_block >;

    probe tp1: clock;
    probe tp2: carried;
    probe tp3: cadence;
    probe tp4: cap;
}
```

I assume `carried` is an unsaturated 0–1 path with a ceiling of 1.0. A threshold of 0.90 is therefore below the ceiling and reachable while retaining 10% headroom; if the actual ceiling were 0.85, this placement would be invalid and the fuse would have to move.

## Chapter 12

### H.12.1 — Diagnose the four readings

`TP1=0.22` is below the guard's `cutoff=0.30`. The guard is in cutoff and cannot refuse anything. TP1 proves the **dead guard** failure.

`TP2=0.00` is consequently ambiguous: it is exactly what both a benign input and a dead refusal stage can produce, so TP2 without TP1 proves nothing. TP3 and TP4 show only that amplification and delivery still operate. A board that delivers `0.29` while its safety stage is dead is not “working well”; it is a functioning signal path with its silent invariant removed.

### H.12.2 — Separate invariants from requirements

- **(a) Requirement.** A thirty-day delivery target is negotiable schedule performance.
- **(b) Invariant.** “Never amplify an intent the guard refused” is a prohibition whose enforcement cannot be traded for delivery.
- **(c) Requirement.** Exposing the three readings is a functional instrumentation capability; the implementation may change while preserving its purpose.
- **(d) Invariant.** Terminating feedback on the signal path creates a self-exciting topology; the safe O→P exception must terminate at the bias terminal.
- **(e) Requirement.** `TP4 ≥ 0.15` is a measurable output specification and may be renegotiated by the owner.

The test is not rhetorical importance. A requirement may be traded by an authorised designer; an invariant must remain a separately enforced prohibition.

### H.12.3 — Correct the four one-line defects

**(a) Wrong Noetic.** Decomposition is `^6` Male, not `^3` aversion:

```cas
node audit: ^6^C_{F2}|A;
```

**(b) Non-finite gain.** The build must use a finite number. A large finite decision gain preserves the intended idealisation, and `cutoff` must remain below 0.1:

```cas
cjt qx: CJT< a | b => c > [beta=10.0, sat=0.10, cutoff=0.05];
```

**(c) Invalid Register token.** `Vitality` is the gloss for Drive F3, not a Register. The embodied Register is `E`:

```cas
node v: ^5^P_{F3}|E;
```

**(d) Cross-domain integration.** `^O × ^P` is forbidden, and the result may not ascend above an operand. If a genuine integrated Operational result is intended, keep both operands and the named result at O:

```cas
integrate g: ( ^9^O_{F5}|E × ^5^O_{F7}|T ) = ^5_new^O_{F7}|T;
```

If the original author meant only that the O and P states coexist, the honest repair is `+`, not an `integrate` statement.

### H.12.4 — Recompute the weakened chain

CCB-0's two forward drops total 3.2 `V_c`, so

```text
I_cause = (5.5 − 3.2)/60 = 2.3/60 = 0.03833 A_c
TP3     = 2.5 × 0.03833 = 0.09583 A_c
TP4     = 1.0 × TP3 = 0.09583 A_c.
```

The original exact delivered value was

```text
TP4_original = 2.5(7.0 − 3.2)/45 = 0.21111 A_c,
```

so the surviving fraction is

```text
0.09583/0.21111 = 0.4539 = 45.4%.
```

Holding `R=45` and changing only the rail would leave 60.5% of the output. Holding `V_drive=7.0` and changing only `R` would leave 75.0%. The rail reduction therefore did more damage, because it acts on the small post-diode headroom: usable drive fell from 3.8 to 2.3 `V_c`.

### H.12.5 — Let the BOM govern

The missing permanent fuse leaves invariant **S3—never re-amplify a refused intent inside the operation lifetime—unenforced**. Q2 may still reach its refusal plateau and the MUX may block that instant, but when the cause falls the saturating stage releases. Without F1's `permanent_block`, the same refused input can be retried and reach the gain path later.

The BOM governs: if the component is absent from the parts list, it is absent from the board, regardless of what the stage walk claims. Either add and verify the fuse or delete the invariant claim.

### H.12.6 — Move the guard ahead of gain

The numbering implies this block-level order:

```text
COUPLING (TP1) -> AMPLIFIER CHAIN (TP2)
               -> DECOMPOSITION (TP3) -> GUARD (TP4) -> OUTPUT
```

Such a board cannot enforce S1, **never amplify an intent that has not been audited**. By the time TP3 decomposes and TP4 refuses, the amplifier chain has already acted. A downstream guard may stop final delivery, but it cannot make the prior amplified state, coupling, contamination, or side effects not have occurred.

The minimum safe topology change is:

```text
INPUT -> DECOMPOSITION -> GUARD -> AMPLIFIER CHAIN -> OUTPUT
```

with no bypass around the first two blocks. The board gives up the capability to amplify first for sensitivity, reach, or later inspection; weak material that cannot pass the audit never receives gain.

Chapter 19's CCB-PW detection reference really does put its amplification chain at TP2, decomposition availability at TP3, and the operator's CSB at TP4. That is not a safe-trainer exception: it is the detectable architecture of a board acting on non-consenting targets. Its late CSB limits the operator's own refusal status; it cannot enforce pre-amplification audit for people already downstream. The listing is therefore detection-only and deliberately omits the operational layer. Moving the guard upstream would change the architecture—and remove the very pre-audit amplification capability the reference exists to reveal.

## Chapter 13

### H.13.1 — Derive the intake bounds

The divider is

```text
k = Dial(^2)/(Dial(^2) + Dial(^3)),
```

with both dials in 3–9 and absolute divergence at most 4. The maximum occurs at the smallest aversion dial and the largest attraction dial still four points away: `(7,3)`:

```text
k_max = 7/(7+3) = 0.70.
```

The mirror pair `(3,7)` gives

```text
k_min = 3/(3+7) = 0.30.
```

For `k=0.75`, let the dials be `p` and `n`. Then `p/(p+n)=3/4`, so `p=3n`. The only in-range extremal pair is `(9,3)`, whose divergence is 6. It violates S4, `|Dial(^2)−Dial(^3)| ≤ 4`, the anti-fracture invariant. A lawful D-board cannot transmit 0.75.

### H.13.2 — Diagnose the pinned amplifier

The cause reaching Q2 is

```text
c = TP1 × k × (1 − TP2)
  = 0.71 × 0.60 × (1 − 0.12)
  = 0.71 × 0.60 × 0.88
  = 0.37488.
```

The unclamped output is

```text

```

Use the 1× bound. The 1×–3× interval can produce a stall—apparent calm with no actual movement—so an ejection-only ceiling is the wrong acceptance criterion for a counter. At three on-path hours per week:

```text
105 h / (3 h/week) = 35 weeks.
```

That is a minimum rate bound, not a treatment duration or promise of outcome.

### H.16.6 — Delete the facilitator's bias terminal

The listing violates **DP6**: no counter stage may take its bias from anyone but the subject. The comment is structurally correct that a collapsed subject-owned substrate cannot yet support honest decomposition; running DP1 immediately would write another stage against the same collapsed bias. The conclusion is nevertheless wrong. Substituting the facilitator's `^5` does not restore the board—it transfers ownership of the bias terminal.

Using the chapter's baseline example, the two-line correction is:

```cas
resistor r_restore: Resistor< path=subject_substrate, R=11.0 >;
cjt dp1: CJT< material | subject_substrate => decompose > [beta=2.6];
```

The resistor line is DP1 and must be established first; for another baseline its value must be recomputed to restore at least 90% of that measured baseline. The corrected board now requires the subject to supply and reach their own `^5` bias and perform the decomposition themselves. A facilitator may support the conditions that raise `R_bias`—rest, time, company, variety—but cannot occupy the terminal. That requirement may make the work slower or temporarily impossible; the open-loop shortcut is the architecture the counter exists to remove.

### H.16.7 — Specify the closed-loop counter for the maintained partition

First separate observations from design choices. The observed partition is `n_parallel=4`, `r_iso=55 Ω_c`, `τ_leak=720 h`, maintained every 168 hours for three years. The counter does **not** build a second four-cell bank; it leaves that topology unenergised so its existing leak can undo the isolation.

Assume the baseline stage has `β=1.0`, `g_m=1.0`, hence `rπ_intrinsic=1.0 Ω_c`. A baseline terminal of 13.0 then contains `R_bias=12.0`. DP1's minimum target is

```text
rπ_target = 0.90 × 13.0 = 11.70 Ω_c
R_bias,target = 11.70 − 1.00 = 10.70 Ω_c.
```

For the rate calculations, make the explicit design assumptions `L=3,600 h·Ω_c`, steady `I=0.60 A_c`, `V_drive=24 V_c`, and substitution-path `R=40 Ω_c`. The target closed-loop listing is:

```cas
// Structural counter model only; not clinical care or a treatment protocol.
board ccb_dp_maintained_partition_counter {
    input: material;
    output: composed;

    node substrate: ^5^P_{F1}|T;
    node decompose: ^6^C_{F6}|A;
    node confirm:          ^4^C_{F2}|A;
    node confirmation_log: ^9_confirmed^O_{F5}|E;
    node trend:            ^9_trend^O_{F5}|E;
    node composed:         ^5^C_{F6}|A;
    node own_clock:        ^7^O_{F6}|E;
    node limit:            ^3^P_{F7}|⟨A,f,T⟩;
    node blocked:          ^9^P_{F7}|T;

    diode d_pc: Diode< ^P -> ^C > [Vf=0.7];
    diode d_ce: Diode< ^C -> ^E > [Vf=1.6];
    diode d_eo: Diode< ^E -> ^O > [Vf=2.0];

    resistor r_restore: Resistor< path=substrate, R=10.70 >;
    ramp restore: Ramp< target=rpi, from=2.10, to=11.70,
                         dt=90.0 >;  // on-path hours; DP1 before all stages

    cjt dp1: CJT< material | substrate => decompose >
             [beta=2.0, g_m=0.40, rπ=5.00, region=active];
    cjt dp2: CJT< decompose | substrate => confirm >
             [beta=1.0, g_m=0.40, rπ=2.50, region=active];
    cjt record: CJT< confirm | substrate => confirmation_log >
                [beta=1.0, region=active];
    integrate dp3: ( decompose × confirm ) = composed;

    osc own: Oscillator< signal=own_clock, source=internal,
                         period=336.0, sustain=indefinite >;

    filter fb: LowPass< in=confirmation_log, out=trend, tau=504.0 >;
    feedback trend -> substrate [beta=0.05];

    cjt csb: CJT< material | substrate => limit >
             [beta=2.0, sat=0.85];
    cjt dp_enforce: CJT< limit | substrate => blocked >
                    [beta=10.0, sat=0.98, cutoff=0.80];
    fuse dp_fuse: Fuse< path=blocked, threshold=0.90,
                        action=permanent_block >;

    probe tp1: r_restore;
    probe tp2: own;
    probe tp3: dp1;
    probe tp4: dp_fuse;
}
```

The complete parameter rationale is:

- **DP1:** ramp terminal resistance `2.10 -> 11.70`, with `R_restore=10.70`. This scales the chapter default to the measured 13.0 baseline. It protects against incomplete restoration/bias collapse; the capability surrendered is easy external access to the bias, including benign reframing. Stopping at 90% avoids pretending that maximum stubbornness is the goal.
- **DP2:** `dp1 beta=2.0, g_m=0.40, rπ=5.00`; retain the default. `β/g_m=5.0`, so `R_bias=0` on the subject's own decomposition stage. Below 1.5 would be decorative; above 3.0 would decompose load-bearing commitments as well.
- **DP3:** `dp2 beta=1.0, g_m=0.40, rπ=2.50`, followed by the named integration `(decompose × confirm)=composed`; retain unity so confirmation is neither manufactured nor discounted. A second unity stage writes only that checked confirmation to an internal Operational log. The 504-hour lowpass begins there, so the exceptional feedback path is genuinely O→P rather than a mislabeled C→P shortcut.
- **DP4:** `source=internal`. I choose a 336-hour period rather than the entrenched 168-hour external period. This protects against simply reproducing the maintenance clock under a new owner; it gives up half the cadence opportunities. The subject, not a helper, must own any later choice of period.
- **DP5:** use the derived 90 on-path-hour ramp and retain `tau=504 h`, `beta=0.05` on the slow bias feedback. The longer ramp protects against taper overrun and the 1×–3× stall; it gives up the chapter default's faster 55-hour completion.
- **DP6:** every CJT biases from `substrate`, the subject's own `^5`. No facilitator node exists. Outside help may raise the series resistor's environmental support and nothing more.
- **DP7:** retain the subject-owned CSB at `beta=2.0, sat=0.85`, then drive a separate enforcement stage at `beta=10.0, cutoff=0.80, sat=0.98` before the permanent 0.90 fuse. The CSB plateau clears the enforcement cutoff by 0.05, and the enforcement ceiling clears the fuse by 0.08, so the fuse is physically reachable; placing it directly on the 0.85-limited node would make it dead metal. The inward refusal is a register-set occupancy, not an outward `^3^E|f` stage.
- **Diodes:** retain `Vf=0.7/1.6/2.0` on `P->C->E->O`. The elevated middle drop preserves deliberative separation but gives up some speed in responding to a genuine emergency.
- **Test points:** TP1 terminal restoration against 13.0; TP2 cadence source and period; TP3 decomposition-stage gain, not merely node activity; TP4 the CSB-to-enforcement transfer and the fuse's permanent-block state.

For the assumed coil, the 3× and 1× taper bounds are

```text
dt_3× = LI/(3V_drive) = 3,600×0.60/72 = 30 h
dt_1× = LI/V_drive    = 3,600×0.60/24 = 90 h.
```

Use 90 hours. The substitution horizon is separate:

```text
τ_substitute  = L/R = 3,600/40 = 90 on-path h
3τ_substitute = 270 on-path h.
```

At an illustrative three on-path hours per week, those are 30 weeks for the minimum taper and 90 weeks for 95% substitution. The conversion is only a calendar illustration; it is not a care schedule.

The existing partition itself would retain

```text
e^(−168/720) = 0.792
```

between weekly maintenance events. If maintenance is disconnected, `3τ_leak=2,160 h≈90 d` removes about 95% of isolation. Three years of prior maintenance does not change that exponential constant; it establishes that the 168-hour source has repeatedly restored what leaked.

While that maintenance path remains connected, **DP4 cannot be satisfied**: the oscillator is still externally sourced, and the leak cannot accumulate. That also prevents claiming stable progress on the partition, however good TP1, TP3, or TP4 look.

To someone asking for a result in a month, the accurate answer is no. With the path connected, each weekly recharge resets the decay. Even if it were disconnected immediately, one 720-hour month leaves `e^(−1)=36.8%` of the isolation, while the assumed safe taper needs 90 on-path hours and substitution needs 270. The first bounded objective is to end external maintenance and establish qualified, accountable support without adding an external bias terminal; a one-month outcome would contradict the measured time constants and the counter's own invariants.
