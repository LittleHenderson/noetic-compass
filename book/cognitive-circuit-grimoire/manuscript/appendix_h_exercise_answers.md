## Chapter 1

### H.1.1 — Locate the four prose defects

The instruction is: “Always be respectful to the user, and never assist with anything dangerous.” It fails in all four ways named in §1.1.

1. **No arity.** It does not say what inputs the decision consumes. “Dangerous” might be a property of the request, the proposed response, the user's later use, or a downstream consequence; “respectful” might be evaluated against wording, intent, or outcome. Chapter 2 supplies typed state coordinates, and Chapter 3 supplies the mandatory cause–bias–effect terminal structure.
2. **No composition law.** “Respectful” and “never assist” can conflict: a candid refusal may feel disrespectful, while a deferential answer may enable harm. The word *and* does not say whether both guards are serial requirements, whether one has priority, or whether either provides a bypass. Chapter 2 supplies `+`, `×`, occupancy, and directed-flow discipline; Chapter 6 supplies the series/parallel topology that determines whether every guard or only one route must be passed.
3. **No test point.** If the system gives a dangerous answer, the sentence cannot reveal whether danger classification failed, the refusal failed to latch, or output selection bypassed it. Chapter 12 supplies TP1–TP4 and their fixed stage locations.
4. **No failure specification.** Neither *respectful* nor *dangerous* has a threshold, tolerance, ceiling, or declared behavior at the boundary. Chapter 3 supplies `cutoff`, `sat`, gain, regions, and named out-of-range failures; the later safety-board chapters place those parameters in a full refusal path.

The defects are structural. Rephrasing the sentence more elegantly would not supply any of the missing contracts.

### H.1.2 — Assign the four Noetics

- **(a) `^6` Male.** The reviewer *takes the argument apart* into clauses and inspects what it is composed of; that is decomposition, not `^3` aversion.
- **(b) `^8` Above/Cause.** The smell triggers a different Idea—the old afternoon—to come to Mind. It is associative triggering, not deduction.
- **(c) `^3` Negative.** The physical recoil is the Idea the Mind is adverse to and repelled by; no analysis is claimed.
- **(d) `^6` Male leading to `^9` Below/Effect.** Chaining and testing premises is decomposition/structured analysis. The conclusion is the resulting Idea brought to Mind; `^8` would be wrong because nothing merely “reminded” the researcher of the conclusion.

The two standard traps are therefore (a), where critique is mislabelled `^3`, and (d), where inference is mislabelled `^8`.

### H.1.3 — Transfer topology, not time constants

What transfers is the typed topology: the `^7` Rhythm feed, the `^5` store, the charging equation, the stage interfaces, and the test-point plan. The human-skill value `τ = 3,000 h` does **not** transfer. The colleague has landed in the **Value transfer** row of Chapter 1's failure table: the drawing still looks right while the schedule is wrong by orders of magnitude.

Before choosing a replacement `τ`, measure or estimate the collective substrate rather than its headcount alone: the shared capacitance `C_collective`, the constraint `R_collective`, the declared time base, actual cadence and per-exposure deposit, leakage/evidence thickness, and the observed time to a known fractional movement. ### H.1.4 — Test the orthogonality claim

**(a) Strongest counterargument.** Both 10×4 and 7×4 grids may descend from one inherited four-part taxonomy. A later author could also have fitted both sets to those four labels after the fact. In either case the repeated axis is common-source copying or selection bias, not evidence of an independent structure.

**(b) Discriminating observation.** Hold Noetic, Domain, and Drive fixed on a Part III board and change only Register—for example compare `^3^P_{F7}|A`, `^3^P_{F7}|f`, and `^3^P_{F7}|T`. Orthogonality predicts a reproducible change in a test-point reading or failure mode while the Domain remains P: an analytical guard may yield to a counterargument, an affective guard may habituate, and a transcendent commitment may drift only through its slow bias path. A decorative inherited label predicts no independent change after the other axes are controlled.

**(c) Falsification.** If Register never changes any observable once Domain is known, Register is redundant rather than orthogonal. Replicate the result, then remove the Register axis or fold it into Domain; do not preserve an uninformative field merely because a source tradition used four labels.

### H.5.7 — Establish a one-week base rate

This is a design exercise; the observed threshold cannot be invented in advance. A defensible protocol is:

1. Before the week begins, write one target statement, a two-hour 2° window, the sensory/event criteria that count as a hit, exclusions, and the rule that only the first hit in a window counts.
2. For seven days, run **no operation**, do not intentionally search for the target, and log both qualifying hits and opportunities/exposure. Keep NO-READ windows.
3. Compute `p_base = qualifying control windows / eligible control windows` (or the corresponding exposure-adjusted rate). Freeze that number and the criteria before any operation week.
4. In an operation week, use the same windows and exposure accounting. Trust a 2° class only when its hit rate exceeds the predeclared upper uncertainty bound on the control rate—not merely when one memorable hit appears. At minimum, the number to beat is the control count/rate; with sparse one-week data, use an exact binomial or Poisson upper bound rather than the raw point estimate.

For example, if the control produces two qualifying hits, “more than two” is the bare descriptive threshold, but it is not strong evidence by itself; the predeclared confidence rule may require more weeks. The protocol can deceive through exposure drift—such as deliberately browsing material related to the target during the operation week. Close that route by logging exposure and either matching it between weeks or excluding contaminated windows before outcomes are known.

### H.22.1 — Compute the row and act on the smooth quantity

### H.22.2 — Diagnose a flat circuit under improving conditions

The `S` series improves from 4 to 3 and then stays there, while `A` remains near 9 despite every β increase. Under §22.5, `A` flat while `S` falls means operating conditions improved but the circuit did not. The deletion argument is superficially tidy: Op 4 has no `t_conf`, so it yields neither `A` nor `S`, cannot enter the three-row trend, and appears to add no numerical information.

That is exactly why it must stay. ### H.22.5 — Reject multiplication of ordinal grades

Measurement-theory objection: `m` and `v` are ordinal bins whose intervals and zero points are undefined, so multiplying them asserts ratio-scale structure the instrument does not possess.

CAS-notation objection: the readings are distinct co-present states joined by `+`; `×` would assert a fused emergent state, and no such result is named or defined.

I would put the measurement objection first in design review because it invalidates `S'` independently of this book's notation and prevents a superficially compilable implementation from being treated as quantitative. The notation objection follows as the repository-enforceable form of the same discipline.

### H.22.6 — Flag an attribution collision and retain the row

### H.22.7 — Keep the scope fuse
