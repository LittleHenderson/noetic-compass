# CHAPTER 5
## St. John's Equation — The Mathematics of Manifestation
*Two clock readings, one constant, and exactly what you may conclude from them*

A wristwatch, a notebook, and a car radio.

The operation ran twenty minutes. The operator wrote `20` in the notebook, went out on an errand, and an hour later a song came on the radio — the one about the little red Corvette, unbidden, in a car whose driver had spent twenty minutes that evening holding an image of exactly that object. They wrote `60`.

Everything in this chapter is built from those two numbers.

Stare at that before going further. The entire calibration apparatus of this tradition — the constant, the ratio, the amplitude requirement, the decision to cast again or to stop calibrating and go act — rests on two readings taken by a person with a watch, about events whose boundaries that person chose. When did the operation start: when the candle was lit, or when the image locked? Did the song count from the first bar or from the moment it registered? Two soft numbers, fed into a formula that will cheerfully turn a two-minute disagreement into a thirty-fold change in one of its outputs.

The formula itself is not the hard part. The formula is one multiplication.

### 5.1 The Revelation Time Codes — 42 months, 1,260 days, 3½ years

The Book of Revelation is read here as a **calibration document** — not prophecy, not allegory, but a text that states one measurement four different ways across eight passages. That is what engineering documents do. A datasheet gives you the same current limit in milliamps, in watts at a stated voltage, and in a derating curve, because different parts of the design need it in different forms.

| Reference | As written | In days | In years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rev 11:2 | forty and two months | 1,260 | 3½ |
| Rev 11:3 | a thousand two hundred and threescore days | 1,260 | 3½ |
| Rev 11:9, 11:11 | three days and an half | 1,260 (day-for-year) | 3½ |
| Rev 12:6 | a thousand two hundred and threescore days | 1,260 | 3½ |
| Rev 12:14 | a time, and times, and half a time | 1,260 | 3½ |
| Rev 13:5 | forty and two months | 1,260 | 3½ |
| Daniel 7:25 | a time and times and the dividing of time | 1,260 | 3½ |
| Daniel 12:7 | a time, times, and an half | 1,260 | 3½ |

Three conversions do the work, and all three are **conventions**, not discoveries. Name them out loud, because an engineer who cannot name their unit conventions cannot check anyone else's arithmetic.

1. **The prophetic year is 360 days.** So 42 months × 30 days = 1,260 days, and 1,260 ÷ 360 = 3½ years. Under a 365-day year the same 1,260 days is 3.45 years — the same number to within one and a half percent, which is why the convention survives being wrong about the calendar.
2. **A "time" is one year.** So "a time, and times, and half a time" is 1 + 2 + ½ = 3½. The plural "times" reading as two is the traditional convention and there is no arithmetic that forces it.
3. **A day stands for a year** in the 3½-day passages. This is the oldest of the three conventions and the one doing the most lifting.

Apply all three and every row collapses to the same value. That convergence is real and it is also **not eight independent measurements**. It is one figure restated eight times inside one interpretive tradition, and a reader who counts the rows as corroboration has counted the same evidence eight times. What the codes actually supply is narrower and more useful: **a unit convention and a span.** Three and a half of a named unit. Nothing else in the text says what happens *inside* that span.

[[FIGURE 5-B: Eight stacked horizontal bars of identical length, one per row of the time-codes table, each labelled at its left with its reference (Rev 11:2, Rev 11:3, Rev 11:9/11, Rev 12:6, Rev 12:14, Rev 13:5, Dan 7:25, Dan 12:7) and at its right with its as-written unit (42 months, 1260 days, 3.5 days, 1260 days, time-times-half, 42 months, time-times-dividing, time-times-half). Three conversion arrows enter from the left margin, labelled "360-day year", "a time = 1 year", "day-for-year", each pointing at the rows it governs. All eight bars terminate at a single vertical rule on the right labelled "3.5". Beneath, a caption box: "One figure, eight statements, one tradition — not eight measurements."]]

A span is still worth having. It sets a **pacing expectation**, and pacing is the parameter operators get wrong most often and most expensively. An operator who believes a complete cognitive restructuring takes six weeks will read an ordinary week-four plateau as failure and tear down a working circuit. An operator who has internalized a 3½-year figure will read the same plateau as week four of a hundred and eighty-two and leave the circuit alone. Chapter 15.4 makes the same point about Overton progression with numbers attached: the collective time constants there are measured in months, and an operator who runs in-group values on a national substrate under-budgets the calendar by an order of magnitude.

But a span alone calibrates nothing. To get an instrument you need a **ratio** — a partition of the span into the part the operator supplies and the part the system supplies on its own. The codes give the denominator. The next section finds the numerator.

### 5.2 The 10,000 Hour Constant — Derivation from the Adept's Training Arc

> *"10,000 hrs of practice has become widely popular in contemporary culture due to psychology and self-help books. However, this bit of knowledge is not new to the Adepts of Majik nor was it new to the Ancient Maji or the Priesthood of Kemet."*
> — *Majik and the True Kabbalah*

> *"This discovery was unearthed as a result of the many students of the Royal Art reaching Adeptship in a span of 3 years, 5 months and 5 days or 3½ years. Manifestation of the Student's desire (to become adept at Majik) took place at the end of the 3½ years / 30,000 hours, after 10,000 hours of practice or 1/3 of the time it took the desire to manifest."*
> — *Majik and the True Kabbalah*

Two figures: a **span** of roughly 3½ years, and an **active practice load** of roughly 10,000 hours inside it. Convert the span to hours under each of the three available conventions and see how much the answer actually moves.

| Convention | Span (days) | Span (hours) | One third |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prophetic year, 360 days: 3½ × 360 | 1,260 | 30,240 | 10,080 |
| The stated arc: 3 y, 5 m, 5 d | ≈ 1,253 | ≈ 30,072 | ≈ 10,024 |
| Calendar year, 365.25 days: 3½ × 365.25 | ≈ 1,278 | ≈ 30,682 | ≈ 10,227 |

All three land within 2.3% of 30,000 hours, and their thirds within 2.3% of 10,000. **The round numbers are roundings**, chosen at the convenient end of a narrow spread. That is an ordinary and honest engineering move — a 4.7 kΩ resistor is not 4,700.0 Ω either — but it must be stated, because a reader who believes 10,000 was counted will over-trust every number downstream of it.

What survives the rounding is the thing worth having:

```
tau = t_practice / t_total_manifestation
    = active ^7 Rhythm input / total elapsed ^7 Rhythm
    = 10,000 / 30,000
    = 1/3
```

One part active work, two parts gestation, three parts total. **This is the constant, and its value is a ratio, not an hour count.** If the true active load were 8,000 hours inside a 24,000-hour span, τ would be unchanged. That robustness is the whole reason the constant is stated as a fraction rather than as a duration, and it is why nothing in this chapter depends on winning an argument about the popular ten-thousand-hour figure.

That argument is worth one honest paragraph anyway, since a reader will bring it. The number entered general currency through Malcolm Gladwell's *Outliers*, drawing on the deliberate-practice research of Anders Ericsson; Ericsson subsequently objected in print — in *Peak*, with Robert Pool — to the way the round figure gets used (Appendix J). **This book does not rest τ on that literature and does not need to.** It rests τ on the partition. Take the partition away and there is no equation; take the specific hour count away and every result in this chapter is unchanged.

#### What the constant looks like as a component

The 1:3 split is not only a ratio. Read it against the `^5` Female capacitor of Chapter 7.1 — the Ideas a Mind is composed of, held as stored charge — and it fixes a time constant. The charging curve is Chapter 7.2's:

```
P_cap(t) = 10 × (1 − e^(−t/tau_mastery))
```

Choose `tau_mastery = 3,333 h`, so that **3τ = 10,000 h**. Then:

| Practice hours | Multiple of τ | `P_cap` (of 10) | Charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 0.30 | 2.59 | 26% |
| 3,333 | 1τ | 6.32 | 63% |
| 6,666 | 2τ | 8.65 | 86% |
| 10,000 | 3τ | 9.50 | 95% |

Ten thousand hours is **three time constants** — the point at which an RC network is conventionally called charged. The constant and the component agree, which is the kind of agreement worth checking and not the kind worth celebrating: the value 3,333 was *chosen* to make them agree. At `τ_mastery = 10,000 h` the same practice leaves the rail at 63%, contradicting the tradition's own claim that 10,000 hours completes the arc; at 1,000 h the rail saturates inside a year, contradicting everything observable about acquiring a craft. Chapter 13 wires this rail as `C1`, and Chapter 20.6 runs it out to a decade-scale schedule.

> **Bench Note — the daily rate is the part nobody quotes, and it is the part that decides.** Ten thousand hours inside 3½ calendar years is `10,000 / 1,278 = 7.8 hours a day, every day, with no days off`. That is not a hobby; it is a full-time apprenticeship, which is what the training arc describes. Change the schedule and the span changes with it, because τ fixes the *ratio* and not the calendar: at one hour a day, 10,000 hours takes 27.4 years; at three hours a day, 9.1 years; at six hours a day five days a week, 6.4 years. When someone quotes 3½ years at you, ask what daily rate they are assuming. If they have not got one, they have quoted a span with no numerator.

#### On π, φ, and the temptation to derive

> *"Occurrences in time happen in accordance to 0.3 or 1/3 and just as 3.14 shows that no circle is truly a circle, 0.3 shows that no occurrence is truly an occurrence, thus susceptible to manipulation by way of consciousness."*
> — *Majik and the True Kabbalah*

The analogy is that π ≈ 3.14159… and 1/3 = 0.333… are both non-terminating, and that a domain governed by a non-terminating constant is a domain that never quite closes — never a perfect circle, never a settled event. As a **mnemonic** for which constant governs which axis, it is serviceable. As mathematics it is nothing: 1/3 is rational and terminates in base three, π is transcendental and proven so, and **no derivation produces 1/3 from π or from φ.** This book does not offer one and Chapter 23.1 declines it again at greater length. The only derivation of τ in these pages is the partition above.

### 5.3 The Formula: t_op / t_conf × 1/3 → a/b → β_next, n_ops, T_manifest

Two clocks in, three readouts out.

```
Step 1 — the realized ratio
    eta = t_op / t_conf

Step 2 — scale by the constant
    result = eta × tau        where tau = 1/3
           = a / b            reduced to lowest terms

Step 3 — read the parameters
    beta_next    = b        amplitude each further operation must deliver
    n_operations = a        how many further operations
    T_manifest   = a × t_op additional operation minutes committed

(beta_next and beta_required are the same readout under two names;
 this book uses beta_next, and the amplitude ratio A below in preference to both)
```

**What Step 2 actually computes.** `result` is a *sufficiency number*. It equals 1.0 when the operation is calibrated and falls below 1.0 when it is under-driven, and the calibration point sits at `η = 3` — three minutes of held operation for every minute the confirmation took to arrive. That placement is what τ is *for*. **τ is a scale factor, a unit convention**, chosen so the readout is expressed against the same 1:3 work-to-total split the training arc exhibits. Change τ and every entry in every log rescales by the same factor while every comparison between operations stays exactly where it was. The equation carries an **ordering**, not a physical quantity, and Chapter 23.1 makes that the first thing it says.

**The number to act on.** Define the amplitude requirement:

```
A = b / a = 1 / result = 3 / eta = 3 × t_conf / t_op
```

`A` is the reciprocal of the sufficiency number, and it is a smooth function of the two clock readings. The pair `(a, b)` is not. Lowest-terms denominators are not continuous in their inputs, and the whole fragility of this instrument lives in that one fact.

> **Bench Note — do this arithmetic by hand before you trust anything downstream of it.** Take `t_op = 20 min`. At `t_conf = 20`: `η = 1`, `result = 1/3`, so `a = 1`, `b = 3`. Now be wrong about the confirmation by four minutes in either direction. At `t_conf = 16`: `η = 5/4`, `result = 5/12`, **`b = 12`**. At `t_conf = 24`: `η = 5/6`, `result = 5/18`, **`b = 18`**. A twenty-percent timing error moved the "required gain" from 3 to 12 in one direction and from 3 to 18 in the other — a quantity that jumps four- and sixfold under an error the operator cannot even detect. Now compute `A` for the same three readings, in the order 16, 20, 24: **2.4, 3.0, 3.6** — smooth, monotone, exactly `3/η` every time. Report `a` and `b` in the log because the tradition does; **decide on `A`.** If `a > 3` after you have reduced, the pair is an artifact of arithmetic and you should read `A` alone. Chapter 23.1 fixes this properly with a five-minute quantizing grid.

**`A` is not a β setting.** This is the correction that costs operators the most. `A` multiplies the *delivered current* — the flow at the far end of the board, not the gain of one stage. Chapter 4.2 gives the relation the current obeys: `V_drive = I_attention × R_constraint`. Five things set delivered current on a desire board (Chapter 13.5): the clarity of the formed `^0` Idea, the transmission through the dial divider, the residual `^3` aversion, the amplifier, and `R_op`. The CJT's design range tops out near **β = 4.0** (Chapter 3.3). So an operator who reads `A = 9` and writes `β = 9` into a listing has specified a device that does not exist, and has also skipped the four levers that were actually available.

**And "nine times stronger" is a distance, not an effort.** Chapter 4.2's warning applies directly here: `A` is a **Fisher-Rao ratio** on the open probability simplex, not a linear multiple of exertion (Appendix L §L.3). `A = 9` means nine times the geodesic shift between the current cognitive state distribution and the target one. Two operations that feel equally strenuous can sit at wildly different Fisher-Rao distances from their targets. Effort is not the measured quantity and never was.

**The bands.** The edges of `A` land on clean values of `η`, which is a good sign that the scale is doing something rather than nothing.

| `A` | `η` | Reading | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A ≤ 1` | `η ≥ 3` | Sufficient | Stop calibrating. Go take the practical action |
| `1 < A < 3` | `1 < η < 3` | Close | Finish the resistance work; leave the amplifier alone |
| `3 ≤ A < 9` | `1/3 < η ≤ 1` | Under-driven | One lever at a time |
| `A ≥ 9` | `η ≤ 1/3` | Weak coupling | The target is mis-specified. Chapter 23.3 |

**The fourth readout, recorded and not acted on.** The source text reads `b` a second way, as a multiplier on the confirmation delay giving an expected arrival: `T_arrive = b × t_conf`. For the Red Corvette that is `9 × 60 = 540` minutes — the nine hours the text names. Log it. Do not schedule around it, and do not tell anyone it is a prediction: it is built on `b`, and the Bench Note above just watched `b` swing sixfold under a four-minute error.

### 5.4 Worked Examples: The Red Corvette, Career Change, Romantic Target

Four cases. Every number is carried through; nothing is asserted that is not computed. Three of the four are the cases the tradition names. The fourth is the one it does not, and it is the one you will meet most often.

#### Example 1 — The Red Corvette

> *"I operate for 20 minutes for a red corvette, 60 minutes later I receive a confirmation in the form of a song that just happens to come on the radio called 'Little Red Corvette.'"*

```
t_op = 20      t_conf = 60
eta    = 20 / 60 = 1/3
result = 1/3 × 1/3 = 1/9          a = 1,  b = 9
A      = 9/1 = 9.0                (check: 3/eta = 3 / (1/3) = 9)
n_ops  = 1     T_manifest = 1 × 20 = 20 min     T_arrive = 9 × 60 = 540 min
```

Degrees recorded at the time: `^7` Rhythm **20 min**, `^1` Mind **2°** (the image went in and out of focus), `^4` Vibration **2°** (60 minutes, clear but rationalizable).

**Reading.** `A = 9.0` sits exactly on the re-specification boundary. The canonical worked example of this entire tradition is a **borderline cast**, and that is worth knowing before you copy its numbers as a model of success. The source's own conclusion — *"I must perform an operation that's 9 times stronger"* — is the `β` error named in 5.3: nine times the delivered current, from five levers, on a device whose amplifier stage cannot supply more than about four. Chapter 13.6 carries this same operator through three operations with full board instrumentation and finds that **98% of the eventual improvement came from the resistance terms and under 2% from the amplifier.**

#### Example 2 — Career Change

An operator casts for a position in a field they have been circling for two years. Thirty minutes of held operation; forty-five minutes later an unsolicited message from a recruiter.

```
t_op = 30      t_conf = 45
eta    = 30 / 45 = 2/3
result = 2/3 × 1/3 = 2/9          a = 2,  b = 9
A      = 9/2 = 4.5                (check: 3 / (2/3) = 4.5)
n_ops  = 2     T_manifest = 2 × 30 = 60 min     T_arrive = 9 × 45 = 405 min
```

Degrees: `^7` Rhythm **30 min** (2°, at the very top of its band), `^1` Mind **2°**, `^4` Vibration **2°**.

**Reading.** `A = 4.5` is squarely in the under-driven band. This is the good case — the instrument is on-scale, the requirement is finite and specific, and the standing instruction applies: **one lever at a time.** Move clarity or move a resistance term, never both, and re-read. An operator who improves the statement *and* clears the calendar *and* re-tunes the amplifier between two operations has bought a better number and learned nothing about which change bought it; the Parameter-Change Log in Appendix G exists to make that discipline hard to skip. Note also what `n_ops = 2` costs: sixty minutes of further operation, against a resistance term — a live job market — that will not move in sixty minutes. The number is real and it is small compared to the work it is pointing at.

#### Example 3 — Romantic Target, and what the instrument does with it

An operator casts at another person's affections. Fifteen minutes, rushed, poor concentration; three hours later a vague social-media post that might be about anything.

```
t_op = 15      t_conf = 180
eta    = 15 / 180 = 1/12
result = 1/12 × 1/3 = 1/36        a = 1,  b = 36
A      = 36/1 = 36.0              (check: 3 / (1/12) = 36)
```

Degrees: `^7` Rhythm **15 min** (2°, barely inside its middle band), `^1` Mind **3°**, `^4` Vibration **3°** — both graded readings at the weak end and the duration close behind them, which is itself the finding.

**Reading.** `A = 36` is four times past the re-specification boundary. The tempting response is to brute-force it, and 5.3 already ruled that out: there is no device with β = 36, and cascading stages does not rescue it because each stage clamps at its own ceiling (Chapter 10.5). But the structural reason this reading came back off-scale is more useful than the arithmetic.

`A` is a shortfall in **delivered current**, and current is set by `V_drive / R_constraint` (Chapter 4.2). Decompose the resistance on a cast aimed at another person's decision (Chapter 4.4): `R_cap` and `R_resource` are the operator's and can be worked. `R_context` is partly shared. But the dominant term is another mind's `^5` composition and its own `^2`/`^3` polarity toward the operator — **a resistance the operator cannot measure, cannot decompose, and has no lawful path to modify.** An instrument reading off-scale against an unmeasurable resistance is not a weak instrument. It is a correctly specified one, telling you the target you named is not the target that is blocked.

**This book does not supply a circuit aimed at a non-consenting person's choices.** The analysis above is here so you can recognize the architecture — in this chapter's arithmetic, and in Chapters 16, 18 and 19 where the same shape appears at scale. The operational version is deliberately absent, and consent architectures are the subject of Chapter 26.2. What the instrument *does* license is the self-directed route: run the `^6^C_{F6}|A` decomposition of Chapter 23.3 on your own stated desire, find what the wanting is actually about, and cast on that. Sub-desires sit in the operator's own `^5`, at resistance the operator can genuinely lower, and they routinely read inside the driveable band while the prime target reads past 9.

> A plain line, not a hedge. A cast that will not read, run against a wanting that will not let go, is a recognizable and unpleasant place to be. Everything in this chapter is arithmetic on a logbook. None of it is a psychological or medical instrument, and it does not describe, diagnose, or treat distress, compulsion, or attachment of any kind. If the operations have stopped being something you choose, that is a matter for a clinician, and nothing in this book substitutes for that care.

#### Example 4 — The Silent Cast

Twenty-five minutes of good operation. Nothing arrives. Not that evening, not the next morning.

```
t_op = 25      t_conf = (none)
eta    -> 0
A      -> infinity
```

`A → ∞` is a limit, not a value, and a validator will reject a non-finite parameter for exactly the reason it rejects `β=∞` on the CSB's red path (Chapter 24): parameter fields are range-checked. Elsewhere in the book an infinite gain can be replaced by a large finite one. **Here it cannot**, because the input is not large — it is *missing*. The bench returns **NO-READ**, and NO-READ is a result. Log it.

The discipline that makes NO-READ possible is declaring the window **before** the cast. Chapter 23.1 computes it as three times the rolling median confirmation time with a 24-hour floor; at this stage of your practice, use the floor. Then do the base-rate arithmetic that the floor is protecting you from — with your own number, not one this book supplies:

```
Suppose confirmation-shaped events for a loosely specified desire occur,
by ordinary chance, once every 8 waking hours = every 480 min.
Over a 24-hour window (1,440 min):   1,440 / 480 = 3 expected events.
```

Three. If your window is a day wide, something will land in it, and what you have measured is your own patience. **Get the rate honestly**: pick a desire, do not cast on it, and tally confirmation-shaped events for one week. That control count is the cheapest and most valuable measurement in this entire practice, and almost nobody takes it.

So the Silent Cast produces no `A` and three usable readings anyway: `^7` Rhythm 25 min, `^1` Mind whatever it honestly was, and `^4` Vibration **absent** — which is the row that will matter when you have ten of them.

### 5.5 The Quantitative Table — Mind (^1), Vibration (^4), Rhythm (^7)

Ten Noetics, and at the bench only three of them yield a reading you can take with a watch and an honest self-report. This is a statement about **instrumentation**, not about the topos: every one of the 40 Cognitive States carries an activation on the open simplex (Appendix L), and all of them are quantitative in principle. But `^5` Female tells you *what the Mind is composed of* and `^8` Above/Cause tells you *that one Idea triggered another* — they identify **which** operation fired, not **how much**. Three of the ten answer "how much" without instrumentation:

| Noetic | What it reads | How you take it | 3° (weak) | 2° (moderate) | 1° (strong) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`^1` Mind** | depth of gnosis — undistracted awareness on the formed Idea | self-report immediately after, before anything else is written | frequent wandering; the image will not hold | wanders and returns; intermittently clear | complete absorption; the Idea is the only content present |
| **`^4` Vibration** | strength of the confirmation's impression | clock the delay, then grade the impression | > 2 h; "was that a confirmation?" | 30 min – 2 h; clear, rationalizable | < 30 min; unmistakable, demands attention |
| **`^7` Rhythm** | duration of the operation | wristwatch, start to finish | < 10 min | 10 – 30 min | > 30 min |

The bin edges are **design choices** and each has a stated failure at other values. `^4` Vibration's 30-minute and 2-hour edges are set so that the 1° bin is short enough to sit well inside any plausible base rate while the 3° bin is wide enough to catch a genuinely slow circuit; push the 1° edge out to two hours and every operator grades every operation 1°, and the column stops discriminating. `^7` Rhythm's 10- and 30-minute edges are set at the two places operators actually break: below ten minutes the gnosis has not had time to establish, and above thirty the `^1` Mind reading almost always falls. Chapter 23.1 quantizes both clocks onto a five-minute grid, deliberately finer than these 30-minute bins and deliberately coarser than the moment a person *notices* a confirmation, which is not a one-minute-accurate event.

Two of the three readings come off a wristwatch and one does not, and the asymmetry is worth naming rather than papering over. `^1` Mind is **self-reported by the same awareness whose depth is being reported**, which is a measurement problem no logbook design can fully solve. An operator absorbed at 1° was, by definition, not monitoring their absorption; an operator who can tell you precisely how absorbed they were has told you something about the answer. Three partial defences are available and all three are procedural. Grade immediately, before any other line is written, so the reading is a fresh impression rather than a reconstruction. Grade against the **behavioural** descriptions in the table — did the image hold, did it return — not against how the operation felt. And accept that `^1` Mind is the noisiest of the three columns and should never be the sole basis for a decision; it earns its place by moving *together with* `^4` Vibration across many rows, which is a pattern hindsight cannot manufacture as easily as it manufactures a single grade.

In CAS terms the three readings are three distinct states, and the register does real work in separating them:

- `^1^C_{F2}|T` at 1° — the Mind in the Cognitive domain under Wisdom, transcendent register: absorbed, purpose-oriented, not tracking itself. The **same state** at 3° reads `^1^C_{F2}|A` — still Mind, still Cognitive, but analytical: checking the clock, noticing the wandering, narrating the operation. The Noetic did not change. The register did, and that is exactly what registers are for (Chapter 2.5).
- `^7^C_{F6}|E` — Rhythm under Structure, embodied register. It is a clocked bodily act and reads as one.
- `^4^C_{F2}|f` — Vibration under Wisdom, affective register: the impression the returning event makes. The delay is measured; the impression is graded.

> **Bench Note — start the clock at the same event every time, and write down which event it is.** `t_op` has two defensible start points: the moment you begin the procedure, and the moment concentration actually establishes. They differ by five to ten minutes for most operators, and 5.3's Bench Note just showed what a four-minute difference does to `b`. Either convention is fine. **Mixing them across a logbook makes the log worthless**, because every comparison between two operations is then a comparison between two different measurements. Write your convention on the first page of the log, in ink, before the first entry, and do not revise it because a later operation would read better under the other one. The same applies at the other end: `t_conf` runs from the **end of the operation**, not from its start, and not from the moment you got around to writing it down.

### 5.6 The Confirmation Board — Tracking Progress Across Operations

A **confirmation** is a `^4` Vibration return: an event that arrives after the operation and brings the desire back to Mind. Structurally it is the canonical `^8` → `^9` pair and nothing more exotic than that. The song is `^8^C_{F2}|E` — Above/Cause, an Idea that triggers a *different* Idea than itself to come to Mind. The desire surfacing is `^9^C_{F2}|A` — Below/Effect, the Idea brought to Mind by an Above/Cause trigger. Associative triggering, exactly as Chapter 2.2 defines it. Not inference, not evidence, not a message.

Grade every confirmation when it arrives:

| Grade | Delay | Impression | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1°** | < 30 min | startling, undeniable | the circuit is coupled; the reading is worth trusting |
| **2°** | 30 min – 2 h | clear, but you can talk yourself out of it | operational, needs refinement |
| **3°** | > 2 h | subtle, ambiguous | weak coupling; re-examine gnosis, the formed Idea, the RPM gate |

**Only the first confirmation enters the equation.** `t_conf` runs from the end of the operation to the first one, in minutes. Later confirmations stay on the sheet as `^4` Vibration evidence and never re-enter the ratio. The Confirmation Log in Appendix G enforces this with a "first confirmation for this operation? Y/N" field, and that field exists because the rule is broken constantly — an operator who admits a stronger confirmation that arrived later has improved their number by choosing their data.

> **Bench Note — grade before you know the outcome, or do not grade.** A 3° confirmation graded a week later, once the desire has actually manifested, grades 1° every time. This is not dishonesty; it is ordinary hindsight and it is unavoidable once the outcome is known. The only defence is procedural: grade within minutes of noticing, write the grade in ink, and never revise it. The Confirmation Log has a "recorded within ___ min of noticing it" line for the same reason. If that line ever reads more than about thirty minutes, the grade above it is a memory of an impression, not the impression.

**And the error that costs more than all the others together: a confirmation is not a manifestation.** A confirmation says the signal reached the Ether. It says nothing about whether the signal reached the target with enough amplitude to overcome `R_constraint`. Chapter 13 puts a number on the gap — a delivered current between the board's cutoff and its manifestation threshold, a band in which confirmations arrive reliably and nothing else does. That band is where most operators conclude the practice does not work, and they are half right: the board is working and the delivery is short. **St. John's Equation exists to measure exactly that shortfall**, which is why `A` is defined as a ratio of delivered current and not as a verdict on the operation.

The board itself is twelve columns wide and one row per operation. Keep it in one place, and keep every operation on it — including the ones that did not read.

```
Op #  Date        Desire (one line)              t_op  t_conf  ^1  ^4   eta    result   (a,b)   A     Band
----  ----------  -----------------------------  ----  ------  --  --  -----  -------  ------  ----  --------
 1    03/14/26    I own a red Corvette             20      60  2°  2°  0.333    1/9     (1,9)   9.0   RE-SPEC
 2    03/17/26    I own a red Corvette             20      20  1°  1°  1.000    1/3     (1,3)   3.0   DRIVE
 3    03/21/26    I own a red Corvette             24       8  1°  1°  3.000    1/1     (1,1)   1.0   ACT
 4    03/24/26    I hold a paid position doing X   30      45  2°  2°  0.667    2/9     (2,9)   4.5   DRIVE
 5    03/26/26    (silent cast, same desire)       25       —  2°   —    —        —        —     —    NO-READ
```

Read the `A` column down, not across. Three things are visible there and nowhere else. **A falling `A`** — 9.0 → 3.0 → 1.0 — is a converging campaign, and Chapter 23.2 gives it a ratio and a threshold. **A flat `A`** across three or more operations means nothing you are changing is reaching the delivered current, and the response is to stop casting and go decompose. **A NO-READ row is not a gap in the log**; it is the row that tells you whether your rate of confirmation is above your base rate, and a logbook with no NO-READ rows in it is a logbook whose window is too wide.

The printable forms are Appendix F (the St. John's Equation worksheet) and Appendix G (the Quantitative Table template, which carries the Operation, Confirmation and Parameter-Change logs as Forms 1, 2 and 3). Chapter 22 is the full treatment of the three measurements; Chapter 23 builds the bench that automates the arithmetic, quantizes the clocks, and refuses to return a number when it has not got one.

### Parameter Table

Every value below is a design choice made in this book. None is a measurement, and each has a stated failure at other settings.

| Parameter | Value | Why this value | Failure at other values |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tau` | 1/3 | The training-arc partition: one part active work, two parts gestation | Any other value rescales every log entry identically and changes no comparison — it is a unit convention, not a finding |
| Calibration point | `A = 1` at `η = 3` | Places sufficiency where τ puts it | Moving it re-labels the bands without changing their order |
| Re-spec boundary | `A = 9` | Head of a multiplicative campaign, not one hard cast (Chapter 23.2) | Higher: campaigns that cannot converge stay open indefinitely |
| Discard rule | discard `(a,b)` if `a > 3` | Above 3 the pair is a coprime artifact of two soft clocks | Kept: "nine operations at 64× strength" gets written into a log as an instruction |
| `tau_mastery` | 3,333 h | Makes `3τ = 10,000 h` at 95% charge on the `^5` rail | 10,000 h leaves the rail at 63% at 10,000 hours, contradicting the constant; 1,000 h saturates inside a year |
| `^4` Vibration edges | 30 min, 2 h | 1° bin short enough to sit inside plausible base rate | 1° edge at 2 h: every operation grades 1° and the column stops discriminating |
| `^7` Rhythm edges | 10 min, 30 min | The two points where operators actually break | Wider bins merge a failed operation with a held one |
| Confirmation window | 24 h floor | Below the point where base rate dominates (Chapter 23.1 computes it properly) | Wider: everything confirms. Narrower: genuinely slow circuits read as dead |
| Confirmation admitted | first only | The ratio needs one arrival, not the best one | Best-of admitted: the operator chooses their data and `A` improves without cause |
| Grading latency | < 30 min from noticing | Before hindsight reaches the grade | Graded after the outcome: every 3° becomes 1° |
| Amplifier ceiling | `β ≤ 4.0` | The CJT's device range (Chapter 3.3) | `A` written as β specifies hardware that does not exist |

### Failure Modes

| Mode | Mechanism | What you observe | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reading `b` instead of `A`** | Lowest-terms denominators are discontinuous in their inputs | `b` swinging from 3 to 18 between adjacent operations that felt identical | Act on `A = 3/η`; discard the pair when `a > 3` |
| **`A` written as β** | The amplitude requirement mistaken for a stage gain | A listing specifying β = 9 on a device whose range ends near 4.0 | Five levers, one at a time (Chapter 13.5); recruit rather than over-specify |
| **Window creep** | The confirmation window widened until something lands | Every operation confirms; no NO-READ rows anywhere in the log | Declare the window before the cast; run the one-week control count |
| **Best-of admission** | A later, stronger confirmation entered instead of the first | `A` improving while nothing about the operation changed | First confirmation only; the Y/N field on Appendix G's Form 2 |
| **Retrospective grading** | Confirmation graded after the outcome is known | A log in which no operation was ever graded 3° | Grade within 30 minutes, in ink, and never revise |
| **Boundary drift on `t_op`** | Start-of-operation defined differently in different entries | Two operations that felt the same reading five minutes apart | Write the convention on page one of the log and hold it |
| **Confirmation read as manifestation** | The `^8` → `^9` return mistaken for arrival | Confirmations arriving reliably, outcomes never | Chapter 13's delivered-current band; `A` is what measures the gap |
| **τ treated as physical** | The scale factor mistaken for a measured constant | `A = 9` trusted the way a 470 Ω resistor is trusted | It is a convention; trust the ordering it produces, not the magnitude |

### Test Points

Chapter 5 has no board — it has a **measurement chain**, and a measurement chain has test points like anything else. Chapter 23 builds the bench (CCB-CAL) that automates these four; until then you are the instrument.

| TP | Reads | Expected | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TP1** | `t_op` against your written start-of-operation convention | Both boundaries taken by the same rule as every prior entry | Boundary drift — the entry is not comparable to the rest of the log |
| **TP2** | `^1` Mind degree, self-reported before anything else is written | 1° or 2°. A 3° operation is a measurement of the operator, not the circuit | At 3°, do not compute `A`; fix the operating conditions and re-run |
| **TP3** | `t_conf` admissibility: first confirmation, inside the declared window | Inside the window, graded within 30 min of noticing | Outside the window: NO-READ, and that is a logged result |
| **TP4** | `A = 3 × t_conf / t_op`, and the pair only if `a ≤ 3` | 1 – 9. Below 1, stop calibrating; at or above 9, re-specify | `a > 3`: discard the pair, read `A` alone |

Read them in that order. TP1 and TP2 decide whether the measurement is admissible at all; TP3 decides whether there is a reading; TP4 is the only one that produces a number you act on. Three of the four gates can send you home without an `A`, and that ratio is deliberate.

### What This Chapter Established

- **The Revelation time codes give a span, not an instrument.** All eight passages reduce to 3½ under three named conventions — the 360-day prophetic year, "a time" as one year, and day-for-year — and they are one figure restated eight times inside one tradition, not eight independent measurements. A span calibrates nothing until it is partitioned.
- **The constant is a ratio and not an hour count.** Three calendar conventions put the training-arc span between 30,072 and 30,682 hours — a spread of 2.0%, sitting no more than 2.3% above 30,000 — and 30,000 and 10,000 are roundings taken at the convenient end of it. `τ = 1/3` survives being wrong about the hours, because changing numerator and denominator together leaves the partition alone.
- **Ten thousand hours is three time constants.** With `τ_mastery = 3,333 h`, the `^5` capacitor of Chapter 7.2 stands at 95% charge at 10,000 hours — the conventional definition of charged. The agreement is by construction, since 3,333 was chosen to produce it, and it is stated here so nobody mistakes it for a second confirmation.
- **`result = η × τ` is a sufficiency number, equal to 1.0 at calibration**, and `A = 1/result = 3/η` is its reciprocal. `A` is smooth in the clock readings; the reduced pair `(a, b)` is not, because lowest-terms denominators are discontinuous. Report the pair, decide on `A`, discard the pair when `a > 3`.
- **`A` is a Fisher-Rao ratio on delivered current, not a stage gain and not an effort multiple.** `A = 9` asks for nine times the geodesic shift toward the target and can be paid from five levers, of which the amplifier — capped near β = 4.0 — is the least available.
- **Only three Noetics take a bench reading without instrumentation**: `^1` Mind (depth of gnosis), `^4` Vibration (confirmation strength), `^7` Rhythm (duration). This is a fact about instrumentation, not about the topos. Register carries real information here: the same `^1^C_{F2}` reads `|T` at 1° and `|A` at 3°.
- **A confirmation is `^8` Above/Cause triggering `^9` Below/Effect — associative surfacing and nothing more.** It says the signal reached the Ether. It does not say the signal reached the target, and the gap between those two is what `A` measures.
- **NO-READ is a result.** Declare the window before the cast, admit only the first confirmation, and run the one-week control count so you know your own base rate. A logbook with no NO-READ rows has a window too wide to measure anything.
