# The Four Kinds of Human Experience

*Chapter 1.*

Before any symbol: what this book is actually about.

You already describe your own life in four different ways, and you switch between them without noticing. Ask someone how their week was and watch which answer comes out.

> "I finally worked out why the deal fell through." — **mental**
>
> "I've been miserable since Tuesday." — **emotional**
>
> "My shoulders have been up around my ears." — **physical**
>
> "I don't know what any of it is *for* any more." — **spiritual**

Same week. Four true answers. They are not four ways of saying one thing, and they are not interchangeable — a person can be intellectually clear, emotionally wrecked, physically fine, and utterly without purpose, all at once.

This book is organised around those four. They have a precise place in the framework behind it, and that is the first thing to see.

## The four are an axis, not a list

The Cognitive Architecture System describes any mental event on four axes at once:

| Axis | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Noetic** | *what* is happening |
| **Domain** | *where* — principled, cognitive, evaluative, or operational |
| **Drive** | *why* — which motive is powering it |
| **Register** | *how* — in what manner it is experienced |

Register is the fourth. It has exactly four values, and they are the four answers above:

| Register | Human word | What it is like from the inside |
|---|---|---|
| `\|A` **Analytical** | **mental** | words, reasons, sequences, arguments you could write down |
| `\|f` **Affective** | **emotional** | felt valence — drawn toward, pushed away, heavy, light |
| `\|E` **Embodied** | **physical** | sensation, posture, breath, gut, the body's own knowing |
| `\|T` **Transcendent** | **spiritual** | meaning, purpose, what it is all for; the sense of being oriented |

**That is the 1:1 this book is built on.** Mental is `|A`. Emotional is `|f`. Physical is `|E`. Spiritual is `|T`. Not a metaphor, not an analogy, not a decorative parallel — the framework's own axis, read in ordinary human language.

Two consequences follow immediately, and both matter.

**First: a single event is usually present in several registers at once, and the registers disagree.** That is not confusion; it is the normal condition. Someone can know perfectly well, in `|A`, that a relationship is over, and still reach for their phone in `|E` every evening at six. The analysis finished; the body had not been told. Naming the registers turns a puzzling contradiction into a description: *this is resolved analytically and unresolved in the body.*

**Second: a register you cannot name is a register you cannot work on.** Most people who say "I'm fine" are reporting `|A` and not noticing `|E`. Most people who say "I can't think straight" are flooded in `|f` and blaming `|A`. The four words give you somewhere to point.

## The same hour, in four registers

Here is one ordinary hour, written four times. Nothing about the events changes.

A woman is clearing out her late father's flat. She finds his coat.

**`|A` — mental.** *"The tenancy ends on the 30th. I need to decide about the furniture, and I should call the council about the meter reading."* Plans, sequences, administrative facts. The mind doing what a mind does when it wants a job it can finish.

**`|f` — emotional.** *"I miss him so much it makes me angry."* Valence. Not a thought about missing him — the thing itself, heavy and warm and unbearable in turns.

**`|E` — physical.** *"My throat closed when I opened the wardrobe. I've been standing in the same spot for ten minutes."* Sensation and posture, arriving before any of it has been put into words. This register often knows first.

**`|T` — spiritual.** *"He kept this coat for forty years. What was he holding on to? What am I?"* Meaning and orientation. Not a feeling and not a plan: the question of what a life is for, asked from inside one.

The registers are not stages and not a hierarchy. They are concurrent. A book that only ever says "she felt sad" has told you one quarter of that hour, and it has left out the quarter that usually decides what happens next — the body, which was already standing still in front of the wardrobe.

## Why a component library needs this

Because the components behave differently depending on which register they are working in.

A capacitor is retention. In `|A` it is a memorised fact you can still recall. In `|f` it is a grudge that has not discharged. In `|E` it is a flinch your hands do before you know why. In `|T` it is a vow. Same component, same job — retain what arrived — and four completely different human situations.

A resistor limits flow. In `|A` it is counting to ten. In `|f` it is refusing to let someone see how much they hurt you. In `|E` it is the breath you slow on purpose. In `|T` it is a discipline you keep because of who you intend to be.

So every entry in this book is written the same way, and it starts where you live:

- **The experience** — a real situation, told plainly.
- **What is happening** — the same situation read in CAS terms.
- **The part that matches** — the electrical component, and why the correspondence holds rather than merely rhymes.
- **Where it breaks** — the line past which the analogy stops being true.

And where a mapping genuinely holds across all four registers, all four are given, because that is the strongest evidence a mapping is structural rather than decorative.

## How strong a claim each mapping makes

Not every pairing is equally deep, and pretending otherwise would make the whole book useless. Each carries exactly one badge.

- **ISO — exact structural isomorphism.** The roles correspond one-for-one and the relation has the same form. These are the mappings that survive being pushed hard.
- **ENG — engineered analog.** The roles match, but only once you stipulate a quantity, a threshold, or a control input. The number is a design choice you make, not a fact you discover. Most mappings are this, and saying so is what keeps the ISO ones credible.
- **PED — pedagogical analogy.** The resemblance teaches and stops there. Nothing quantitative transfers.

One badge per entry. Never a hybrid. If a mapping is *almost* ISO, it is ENG, and the badge says so.

## The one-sentence version

**Your mental life is `|A`, your emotional life is `|f`, your physical life is `|E`, and your spiritual life is `|T` — and a circuit that handles one of them can be read as handling that whole quarter of a person.**

Everything that follows is that sentence, made specific forty-two times, and then walked through eight real situations from beginning to end.
