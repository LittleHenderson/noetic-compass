# Grief That Will Not Discharge

*Chapter 9. Case study 1 of 8 — the capacitor, the leak, and the reference.*

Margaret is sixty-eight. Her husband Alan died eighteen months ago, after forty-one years. She is not unwell. She is not "not coping" — she runs the house, sees friends, has taken up swimming. And every evening at about six o'clock, when Alan used to come in from the garden, she finds herself standing at the kitchen window with no memory of having walked there.

She has been told this is normal. It is. She has also been told it will pass. That part is not quite right, and the reason it is not quite right is a component.

## The circuit

Margaret is holding a charge. Something arrived — forty-one years of a particular person arriving at a particular time — and it has persisted, and the persistence is doing work. That is the **capacitor**, and this is the clearest ISO mapping in the whole library: capacitance is retention across time, and it resists a change in the voltage across it. She is not remembering Alan at six o'clock. She is being *moved* by a stored quantity, and the movement happens before the remembering does.

The retention is not a decision, a weakness, or a failure to move on. It is a stored quantity doing exactly what stored quantities do.

**In `|E`**, the body knows first. Her feet take her to the window. Thirty years of walking to that window when she heard the gate means the path is not a thought any more, it is a groove, and the groove runs without her.

**In `|f`**, the six o'clock window does not feel like sadness exactly. It feels like *waiting*. That is the honest report, and it is more informative than "grief," because waiting is a state with a missing arrival in it.

**In `|A`**, she can say all of this clearly. *"I know he is not coming back."* She is not confused and she is not in denial. The analytical register is resolved. This is the contradiction that other people misread — they hear a clear sentence and conclude that the rest should have followed.

**In `|T`**, the question is not *where is he* but *what were those forty-one years*. That question has no answer that fits in a sentence, which is why it is the register that takes longest.

## What the parts say

**The capacitor** explains why it is still happening. Charge persists. The question is not *why has she not discharged it* — a capacitor does not discharge itself, that is the entire point of a capacitor. The question is whether anything in the circuit provides a path.

**The leakage resistor** is why it is gentler than it was. Every retention has a leak, and the leak rate is a parameter, not a moral quality. At eighteen months the charge is a little lower than it was at six. Somebody whose grief is exactly as sharp at year five as at week five does not have better love; they have a stuck leak, and that is a fault worth naming as a fault.

**The reference node** is the part almost nobody thinks about, and it is doing a great deal here. Margaret's zero was set by a house that had a six o'clock in it. Her baseline for *a normal evening* is a room with another person arriving. Measured against that zero, every evening reads as low. Nobody recalibrated it, because recalibrating a reference is not something you do by deciding to — it happens or it does not, and it is slow.

**The dashed arrow** is what she has not yet noticed. The feedback path is: *feeling low at six → going to the window → feeling worse at the window → the window becoming the place where the feeling lives.* The window is now a stage set for the state. That is a returning signal, and it is the one place in this circuit where a small change is available.

## What actually helps, in circuit terms

Not "moving on," which asks the capacitor to stop being a capacitor. Three engineering moves do apply.

**Provide a path.** A capacitor discharges through something. Crying is a path. So is telling somebody the true sentence — *"I'm not sad, I'm waiting"* — out loud, because saying it moves the state from a charge into `|A`, where it can be handled.

**Change the leak deliberately.** Grief-work is not about lowering the charge; it is about making sure the leak*age* exists. Walking, swimming, sleeping, being in rooms with living people: all of these are the resistor. Nothing dramatic. Leak rates are small numbers applied over a long time.

**Move the reference, slowly, by exposure.** The zero point updates by spending time in evenings that are not that evening. It cannot be argued into place. Six months of Thursdays somewhere else does more than any amount of insight.

## Where the analogy stops

A capacitor has one job and no interior life. Margaret has a life, a history, and a stake in what the forty-one years meant — and the meaning question, `|T`, is not in the capacitor at all. The component explains the *persistence* and the *shape of the curve*. It does not explain the love that produced the charge, and it must never be used to explain it away.

That is the boundary. The circuit tells you why the window still works on her. It says nothing about whether she should stop going there.

## What to take from it

- Grief persisting is retention doing its job, not a failure of character or of acceptance.
- The registers disagree, and the disagreement is information: `|A` resolved, `|E` not yet told.
- The leak is a parameter. Ask whether it exists, not whether it is fast enough.
- Somebody's zero was set by the life they had. That is the part that takes longest to move.
- **The same expression, two different lives.** Margaret's sister lost her own husband the same year, and on the page the two events are identical — `^8` trigger, `^5` substrate, `^9` arrival, retention, leak. What differs is the board: one of them has a return path and a boundary that can actually fire and one person she tells things to; the other has none of those. Same expression. Two completely different years.
