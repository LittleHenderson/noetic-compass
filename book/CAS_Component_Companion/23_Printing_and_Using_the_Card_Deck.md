# Printing and Using the Card Deck

*Fifty-six cards, seven sheets, one US Letter page per eight cards.*

The companion deck is a separate PDF: `CAS_Component_Companion_Cards.pdf`. It carries the whole guide in a form you
can hold in one hand at the bench, cut out, shuffle, and lay next to a schematic.

**Print it one-sided, at Actual Size, on US Letter.** Not "fit to page". The cards are sized so that the body text
lands at a readable size on paper, and every sheet carries crop marks — sixteen ticks around the border plus one
between each card — so a guillotine or a ruler and a craft knife gives clean edges. Two columns by four rows, eight
cards per sheet, 3.61 by 2.34 inches each.

Scale the page and three things break at once: the text drops below the size it was set at, the cut marks stop
lining up, and the cards stop being the same size as each other. If your printer insists on fitting the page, turn
that setting off rather than accepting it.

### What is in the deck

| Cards | What they are |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Five guides: the 1:1 rule, the Domain legend, reading a CAS state, operators and firing, and the four drift stops. |
| 6–34 | Twenty-nine component cards, one per part, with symbol, role, example and guardrail. |
| 35–42 | Eight annotation cards: the Drive rail, the reference node, the probe, the switch, the meter, the typed net label, and the two arrow kinds. |
| 43–52 | Ten circuit cards, each a compact signal chain for one cognitive schematic. |
| 53–56 | Four use cards: how to study the deck, the claim boundary, printing, and building your own circuit. |

**Five entries are taught on their parent cards rather than printed separately**, because they are narrow or
derived and a card of their own would teach less than the sentence it replaced: **Varactor** on the capacitor and
LC-tank cards, **Current mirror** on the differential-pair card, and **NAND**, **NOR** and **XNOR** on the XOR card.
All forty-two entries keep their own plate and their own row in the memory deck — the deck of cards is a study aid,
not the reference.

### Sorting the deck

Cards carry a number, so keep them in number order the first time through. After that, two other orders earn their
keep:

- **By job.** Group the limiters (resistor, capacitor, inductor), the one-way parts (diode, Zener), the amplifiers
  (CJT, op-amp, differential pair, active load), the timing parts (oscillator, crystal, PLL), the logic parts, and
  the annotations. Building a circuit from a shuffled deck is a good test of whether you know what each part is
  *for*.
- **By badge.** Sort into ISO, ENG and PED. The ENG pile is the one worth re-reading: those are the mappings that
  need a number you have to choose, and choosing it badly is the usual failure.

### Studying from the deck

Three passes, in this order, and each one is a different question:

1. **Name the job from the symbol alone.** No text. If you cannot say what the part does before you read the card,
   the shape has not stuck yet.
2. **Recite the cognitive role and the badge.** Saying "resistor, limits activation flow, ENG" out loud is the
   whole exercise. The badge is not decoration — it tells you how far the mapping carries.
3. **Explain the example and the guardrail without looking.** This is the pass that matters. The guardrail is the
   line that tells you where the analogy stops, and knowing it is the difference between using this library and
   abusing it.

A memorised metaphor is not enough. The role correspondence and the boundary are the content; the metaphor is only
how you reach them.
