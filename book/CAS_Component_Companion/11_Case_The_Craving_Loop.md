# The Craving Loop

*Chapter 10. Case study 2 of 8 — the oscillator, the inductor, and the missing leak.*

David is fifty-one. He stopped drinking four years ago and has not had a drink since. He is clear about that, and it is true. What he cannot explain to anyone is why, at 5:40 every evening, something in him starts looking for the off-licence — four years on, with no intention of going, and no desire he would describe as desire.

He is not craving a drink. He is running a loop that used to end in a drink, and the loop has not been told.

## The circuit

**The oscillator** is the loop itself. Positive feedback has taken over: the output has become the input, and the cycle sustains without any fresh reason. This is why David cannot argue himself out of it at 5:40. There is no argument to win, because nothing external is driving the signal. An oscillator does not need an input. That is what an oscillator is.

**The inductor** is the forty years of established pattern. Inductance opposes a *change in rate* — and David asked for the largest possible rate of change on the day he stopped. The stored energy in that pattern did not vanish; it found another path. The walk to the shop became a walk around the block; the drink became a glass of something else at the same time; the ritual reassembled itself around a new object. Inductors do that. Interrupt the current and the energy goes somewhere, and if you have not provided a route it will find one.

**The missing leak** is the part nobody plans for. A capacitor with no discharge path holds its charge indefinitely. David's evening state has been kept intact by the fact that he never allowed the 5:40 feeling to complete and fade. He interrupted it, every time, with a substitute — which is not the same as letting it discharge, and is why it has not diminished in four years.

**The reference node** is the part that keeps him honest about time. His zero is *an evening with a drink in it*, because that is what forty years of evenings built. Measured against that zero, every sober evening reads as a departure rather than a norm. Four years is not long enough to move a zero set by forty.

## The same moment, in all four registers

- `|A` **Mental** — *"I notice I am planning a route past the shop. I am not going to go in. I know what this is."* The analytical register is doing the work of recognition, and it is working — he has not gone in, four years running.
- `|f` **Emotional** — *"It feels like something is missing and I want it filled."* Not a desire for alcohol, which is why "just don't drink" is filed as unhelpful: the feeling is a shape, not a beverage.
- `|E` **Physical** — *"My hands are restless and I am looking out of the window."* The body is running the approach sequence it has run ten thousand times, and it starts before he notices it started.
- `|T` **Spiritual** — *"What is the evening actually for now?"* This is the register that nobody warns you about in early recovery: the loop was never only chemical. It was a way of marking the end of the working day and of having something to be. Remove it and the question of what the evening is *for* is left standing in an empty room.

That last line is the one that matters most and is addressed least. A person can be physically sober, emotionally stable, and analytically clear, and still be running an evening with no purpose in it — which is a `|T` fault, and it will pull toward anything that supplies one.

## What actually helps, in circuit terms

**Add loss, not resistance.** Resistance limits flow through the loop; loss takes energy out of it. David's walk around the block is loss. So is company at 5:40, and so is anything that makes the loop cost something to run.

**Provide a discharge path.** Let the feeling complete. Sit with the 5:40 state for twenty minutes without substituting anything, and let it rise and fall. It is unpleasant, it is finite, and — unlike the substitute — it *ends*, which is the entire difference between discharging and muffling.

**Change the reference slowly.** Not by deciding that evenings are fine. By accumulating evenings that do not have the shape of the old ones, until the zero moves. This is the slowest part and the one that most reliably works.

**Feed the `|T` rail.** The evening needs a purpose that is actually his. This is not a metaphor and not a self-help slogan: an oscillator with no load will keep oscillating about something, and the question is only what.

## Where the analogy stops

An oscillator has no stake in its output and no memory of what it used to be. David has both, and the meaning of the loop to him — what it once did for him — is not in the circuit. The components explain why the pattern persists and where to break it. They do not explain the man, and they must not be used to.

There is also a claim boundary worth stating plainly: this is a description of one pattern, not a treatment. Anything involving dependence belongs with people qualified to handle it.

## What to take from it

- A repeating pattern that needs no fresh input is an oscillator, and arguing with it is the wrong instrument.
- Abrupt change meets the largest opposition. Move the rate, not just the value.
- A substitute that muffles is not a discharge path. Only one of them reduces the charge.
- Four registers, four different interventions. Working on one and calling the others solved is how people stay stuck while doing everything right.
