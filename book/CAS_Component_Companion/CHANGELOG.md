# The Component Companion — Changelog

## 2026-09-09 — v1.3 (one expression, many boards; 172 pages)

The consequence of v1.2, followed to its end — and it is the most hopeful claim in the book.

**Two completely different boards can implement the same expression and produce outcomes with
almost nothing in common.** If the notation reports and the board commits, then an identical
expression admits radically different designs. Two people can have the *same* Noetic, Domain, Drive
and Register, the same direction of transition — and live completely different versions of it.

**What the expression fixes:** what kind of event it is, where it sits, what powers it, how it is
experienced, and the direction of the transition. That is the *shape*, and it is a real constraint.

**What the board fixes, and the expression cannot:** gain, bias and operating point, thresholds,
clamps and ceilings, the return path, time constants, isolation, defaults and failure states, and
whether the protective element is reachable. Chapter 2 now carries these as a table. None of them
is in the expression, and all of them change the outcome more than the expression does.

**The worked comparison.** One expression — `^8^C_{F4}|A + ^5^P_{F7}|T -> ^9^C_{F4}|f`, a sound
meeting accumulated composition and landing as a felt arrival — run on two boards. Board A: gain
high and unclamped, no dead-band, no return path, no stated default; the sound lands as a certainty
about a whole relationship, in a second. Board B: gain limited, a clamp at 0.85, a dead-band on the
decision, a return path with τ in hours, a reachable fuse, a stated fail-closed default; the same
sound is a pang that passes and gets thought about in the evening.

**Three consequences now written in:**

- **"We have the same problem" is usually false.** Two people compare *expressions* and conclude
  they share a condition. They share a shape and differ on every parameter that decides how bad it
  gets — so the conversation becomes an argument about whose experience is real.
- **Reframing alone often does nothing.** Renaming the event changes the description. If the gain is
  still high and the return path is still missing, the board runs exactly as before. Reframing works
  when it moves a parameter.
- **Board changes work when nothing else does.** Sleep is a rail; a stated boundary is a fuse, if it
  is reachable; leaving the room at the start rather than the end is a dead-band; telling one other
  person is a return path. None of them changes the expression, and all of them change the outcome.
  **You can change your life without changing what happened to you, because most of what decides the
  outcome was never in the event.**

**The guardrail on the claim**, stated in the chapter so it cannot be over-read: the shape constrains
the *class* of outcome. No board turns an `^8` associative landing into a reasoned conclusion — that
needs a `^6` operation, a different expression. No board turns `^3` repulsion into `^2` attraction;
that conversion does not exist. You can change magnitude, speed, reversibility and failure mode.
You cannot change what kind of event it is.

**Also added:** the comparison carried into the grief case study — Margaret's sister lost her husband
the same year, and on the page the two events are identical; what differs is the board — and a
fourth closing warning in Chapter 19.

**Verification.** Guide 172 pages, 0 errors, 0 overfull boxes, 0 missing glyphs; deck unchanged at 56
cards on 7 sheets; cover regenerated at the 172-page spine (0.3873 in); **41/41** cross-artifact
checks pass, including a new assertion that both halves of the claim survive typesetting.

## 2026-09-09 — v1.2 (the schematic states intent; 170 pages)

A precision added to Chapter 2, and it changes how the two languages should be read.

**The notation reports. The board commits.** An expression states what a state *is*, and it is true
or false about the world. A schematic states what a designer *intends to happen*, and every symbol
on it was placed and every parameter chosen. `sat=0.8` is not a measurement; it is a decision about
how high the output may go. `threshold=0.05` is a decision about where the boundary sits. **CAS
PCBA is how you write down how the notation is being used, and what effect the architect intends.**

The notation has nowhere to put intent. The board is nothing but intent. The chapter now carries a
side-by-side table, then the six places intent shows up on a page — which parts are present at all,
where they sit relative to one another, the numbers, the default and failure state, what is
isolated from what, and what is labelled. A fuse upstream of a clamp and a fuse downstream of it are
different designs with the same part list; the expression cannot tell them apart and the schematic
cannot avoid doing so.

**The three badges, re-read as claim strength.** ISO: the designer claims the correspondence is
structural. ENG: the designer is stipulating, and the number is theirs. PED: the designer claims
nothing quantitative. This is why a hybrid badge is not a nuance — it is a designer declining to
state the strength of their own claim.

**Guardrails are the operating envelope.** *Where it breaks* is not hedging. It is the
absolute-maximum section of a datasheet: the range within which the designer warrants the
behaviour, and the specific way it fails outside. A datasheet without one is not a more confident
datasheet.

**Whose board are you running?** The reason the distinction is not academic. Some of a person's
circuit they designed; much of it they did not. A rule inherited from a parent is a component
somebody else placed. A reference set in a house where the atmosphere had to be monitored is a zero
point you did not choose. A fuse whose trigger condition was named by somebody else will not fire
when you need it. **You can be running a board designed by a person who is not you, for a purpose
that was not yours — and the board runs perfectly.** That is what makes it hard to notice. Reading
your own behaviour as a schematic is how you find out which parts you chose, so that the inherited
ones can be kept deliberately rather than merely never examined.

**Also added:** theatre, fiction and ritual to the domains map in Chapter 19, and a third closing
warning there — a map is not a design. Description is what the notation does; turning description
into a design means placing components on purpose and taking responsibility for having chosen them.

**Verification.** Guide 170 pages, 0 errors, 0 overfull boxes, 0 missing glyphs; gutter 0.9003 in;
deck 56 cards on 7 sheets; cover regenerated at the 170-page spine (0.3828 in); **40/40**
cross-artifact checks pass, including a new assertion that the intent section is present in the
typeset guide.

## 2026-09-09 — v1.1 (re-aimed at human experience: 166 pages, nine case studies)

The v1.0 guide taught the component library and treated human experience as an aside. That was
backwards, and this revision turns it around. The book is now **166 pages in five parts**, and it
opens with what it is actually about.

### The claim the book now makes on every page

The four Registers of CAS v7.7 are the four kinds of human experience, in the framework's own
terms:

| Register | Human word |
|---|---|
| `\|A` Analytical | **mental** — reasons, words, sequences |
| `\|f` Affective | **emotional** — felt valence |
| `\|E` Embodied | **physical** — sensation, posture, breath |
| `\|T` Transcendent | **spiritual** — meaning, purpose, what it is all for |

Chapters 4–9 state **all four registers for every one of the 42 entries** — not as a claim made
once in an introduction, but 42 times on the page, next to the symbol each one belongs to. A
mapping that only ever mentions one register has not made the claim at all, which is why the
register block is generated into every entry rather than left to the writer's mood.

### New in this revision

- **Chapter 1 — The Four Kinds of Human Experience.** The register axis in ordinary language, with
  one hour of a life written four times: a woman clearing out her late father's flat, in `|A`,
  `|f`, `|E` and `|T`. Nothing about the events changes between the four readings.
- **Chapter 2 — The Notation and the Symbols Enforce Each Other.** The two languages, and the
  crucial nuance: **they are not translations of each other.** They overlap where both can state
  the same thing — one expression, one transistor — and each holds expressions the other cannot.
  The notation carries admissibility relations, the involution pairs, the open simplex, the
  `|⟨…⟩` / `+` / `×` distinction, and recipe arithmetic; the drawing carries ordering, isolation,
  protection placement, multiplicity at a glance, and the reference. A worked example shows both
  directions of enforcement on one man's afternoon in a shop.
- **Part III — nine case studies**, each read in all four registers: grief that will not discharge
  (retention and leak), the craving loop (oscillator, inductor, missing discharge path), the panic
  that fires too easily (a threshold with no dead-band), the argument that resonates (an LC tank at
  a fixed frequency), the trigger that is not a reason (`^8` is associative triggering, **not**
  reasoning, and no amount of reasoning changes its timing), muscle memory (capture range versus
  lock range), the purpose that holds (regulation carried by the `|T` rail), the session that
  browned out (a supply fault invisible from the output), and **social engineering read two ways**
  — the same components that describe a teacher describing a fraud, with the notation naming the
  substrate the attack uses and the drawing naming the wire the defence goes into.
- **Chapter 19 — What Else This Pair Illustrates.** Thirteen more domains — love and attachment,
  fear, anger, habit, learning, grief, purpose and spiritual life, burnout, manipulation and
  propaganda, institutions and groups, teaching, self-knowledge — each with the parts that do the
  work.
- **Every schematic gains an "In a life" reading**, so the ten circuit drawings are pointed at a
  person rather than left as topology.
- **The 56-card deck was re-aimed to match.** Body labels are now **THE PART / IN LIFE /
  REGISTERS / GUARD** on component cards and **WHAT IT IS / IN A LIFE / THE CHAIN / GUARD** on
  circuit cards, and every component card carries a compact four-register line. If the deck had
  kept the old labels the two artifacts would have taught different things.

### What did not change

The drawings. Every plate and schematic is byte-identical to v1.0, which passed the geometry and
symbol audits; this revision is copy and structure. The audits were re-run and still pass, and the
freeze on the electrical definitions is unchanged — including the two rules that matter most:
a bridge rectifier never converts `^3` Negative into `^2` Positive, and a fuse whose threshold
sits above every upstream ceiling can never fire.

## 2026-09-09 — v1.0 (first edition: the component library as its own book)

*The Component Companion* is published as a **standalone 58-page 6×9 guide**, with its own front
matter, its own contents, its own cover and its own 56-card printable deck. It is not an appendix
to anything.

The material began life as Appendix J inside *CAS PCBA Engineer*. That framing was wrong: a
reference you keep open beside the main volume is a book, not an appendix, and calling it one put
a 40-page illustrated component library at the back of an 800-page design text. It has been moved
out, restructured as eleven numbered chapters plus one appendix, and given its own print run.

**The guide.** Front matter (Preface, How to Use This Guide, How to Read These Plates), then
Chapters 1–7 covering the seven symbol plates one component family at a time; Chapters 8–9 the ten
cognitive schematics; Chapter 10 the complete 42-entry memory deck; Chapter 11 printing and using
the card deck; and Appendix A, the frozen inventory and design notes. Every entry carries exactly
one mapping badge — **ISO** (exact structural role correspondence), **ENG** (engineered analog
needing a stipulated quantity or threshold), or **PED** (teaching analogy, no quantitative
transfer). There is no fourth label; a mapping that is *almost* ISO is ENG, and the badge says so.

**The deck.** `CAS_Component_Companion_Cards.pdf` — 56 cards, 7 US-Letter sheets, 8 per sheet in a
fixed 2×4 grid, each exactly 3.61 × 2.34 in with crop marks. One badge per card, enforced at
compile time.

### The electrical drawings were wrong, and the old check could not see it

Four defects were found and fixed, each with a negative control that now fails if it returns.

- **The bridge rectifier had two of its four diodes reversed** — the upper-right and lower-left
  diodes pointed away from the node they feed, so the "bridge" shorted its own AC inputs on the
  positive half-cycle instead of rectifying. The rotation list is corrected, and
  `audit_symbol_invariants.py` now *derives* the required direction from the declared AC / DC+ /
  DC− corners rather than comparing against a literal.
- **The current mirror was not a mirror.** The left device's base was wired to the shared emitter
  rail and the right device's base had no wire at all — a base–emitter short plus a floating base.
  It is now diode-connected with the bias node driving the second base, and the audit checks that
  the reference collector and both bases are one node set using wire segments only (the device
  circles are bodies, not conductors).
- **Every arrowhead in all 17 figures stopped 0.410 mm short** of its target and rendered 26% small,
  because the tip inherited the wire's line width.
- Smaller glyph repairs: the Schmitt hysteresis mark no longer pokes outside its triangle, the relay
  linkage spans coil to armature, the breaker's trip square is carried on the operating arm rather
  than floating, switch and breaker contacts are drawn as open circles rather than junction dots,
  the ADC/DAC trapezoids put the wide side on the multi-bit port with bit stubs, and the photodiode
  arrows aim at the junction.

### Six schematics had wires ending on bare canvas

- **Circuit 2** — the four Domain blocks were too wide for their pitch, so each diode straddled both
  blocks, its white fill erased the block borders, and every inter-stage arrowhead pointed
  *backwards*. Blocks are now 19 mm wide on a 29.6 mm pitch, each diode sits at the computed gap
  midpoint, and the Zener guard lands on the middle diode's anode lead instead of inside its body.
- **Circuit 4** — the source wire ran straight through the feedback resistor; it now stops at both
  terminals.
- **Circuit 6** — the result was taken from the multiplexer's **select** stub while the real output
  lead dangled. The result now comes from the output lead, and the select input is driven by the
  refusal-sense line it belongs to.
- **Circuit 7** — the mirror's tail connection started on bare canvas and the bias wire stopped
  1.5 mm short of the base terminal.
- **Circuit 8** — the tank-to-PLL wire ended 2.9 mm above the PLL box.
- **Circuit 9** — the refresh line floated at both ends, the probe sat on the CJT base bar and
  erased its middle, and two three-way junctions carried no dot.
- **Circuit 10** — the seven Drive rails ended 8.5 mm above the regulator block with no bus between
  them; the brownout sense line started 1.5 mm short.

### Claim states

Every parameter value in this guide is an engineering design choice, not a measurement from a
person, an AI model, or a biological system. The `ISO` badge claims role-level structural
correspondence only. No plate is validated against IEC 60617 or IEEE 91 text; the ADC/DAC trapezoid
orientation and the breaker trip mark follow common databook practice and remain flagged for
house-standard confirmation in Appendix A.
