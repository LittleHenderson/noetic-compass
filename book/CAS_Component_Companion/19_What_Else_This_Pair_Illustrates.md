# What Else This Pair Illustrates

*Chapter 19.*

Nine situations have been worked through in full. The library is not limited to those nine, and this chapter is a map rather than an argument: for each domain, the parts that do the work and the thing the pair lets you say that ordinary language struggles to.
None of these is a claim of clinical or scientific validity. They are the domains where these components have earned their keep in this book, and where a reader can go next.

## Love and attachment

**Parts:** capacitor, current mirror, LC tank, reference, regulator.

Attachment is retention with a long time constant, and much of what people find mysterious about it becomes ordinary once it is described that way — why a person can be gone for a decade and still shape an evening; why a familiar tone lands before any thought; why two people who love each other can still resonate on one frequency and nowhere else. The reference node is the part that explains why leaving a long relationship is disorienting in a way that has nothing to do with missing anybody: the zero point moved.

## Fear, anxiety, and threat

**Parts:** Schmitt trigger, oscillator, zener, fuse, rail.

A threat system is a comparator with a threshold, and almost every intervention that works is a change to the threshold, the dead-band, or the supply — not to the fear. The `|E` register is the fastest one and therefore the first place to work. Naming the trigger condition correctly is what lets the protective rule fire at all.

## Anger and conflict

**Parts:** resistor, differential pair, LC tank, relay, circuit breaker.

Anger is usually a signal doing a job, and the useful question is what it is a difference *between*. Conflict that repeats with the same shape is a resonance, and resonance is changed by changing `L` or `C`, never by resolving to do better. The resistor is the whole of the "count to ten" advice, and it is more precise than the advice: pressure unchanged, rate constrained, cost paid.

## Habit, discipline, and change

**Parts:** inductor, oscillator, crystal, PLL, DAC.

The single most useful fact here is that inductance opposes a change in *rate*. Nearly all failed self-improvement asks for the largest possible rate of change and therefore meets the largest possible opposition. The crystal explains why a fixed daily practice holds better than an enthusiastic irregular one, and the PLL explains why you cannot entrain yourself or anyone else by jumping straight to a distant rhythm.

## Learning and skill

**Parts:** PLL, crystal, ADC, DAC, active load.

Capture range and lock range are the two numbers that make deliberate practice deliberate. Repetition outside the capture range is not practice. Naming a continuous bodily difference in words is what converts practice into something you can steer.

## Grief and loss

**Parts:** capacitor, leakage resistor, reference, transformer.

Retention persists, leaks, and can be retuned. The reference moved, and the reference is the part that takes longest to move back. The distinction between discharging a feeling and muffling it is the difference between two completely different outcomes from the same behaviour.

## Purpose, meaning, and spiritual life

**Parts:** rail, regulator, reference, solid arrow, crystal.

This is the `|T` register, and in this framework it is not decoration. Purpose is a *supply*. A board with an unsupplied `|T` rail runs every other stage at reduced margin — which is the mechanism behind a great many things that get called depression, burnout, or a crisis of faith. A practice that keeps you oriented is a reference; integrity is a reference other people set their clocks by; a vow is a relay. Religious and contemplative traditions have been describing these structures for a very long time in their own vocabulary, and the components here are one attempt at a vocabulary that does not require you to accept any of them.

## Burnout, exhaustion, and collapse

**Parts:** rails, brownout detector, thermal cutoff, sequencer, regulator dropout.

Supply faults are invisible from the output until they are not. The warning tier is an instruction. Restore in sequence — body, connection, plan, output — or the board browns out again.

## Manipulation, propaganda, and coercive control

**Parts:** current mirror, low-pass, relay, reference, mislabelled fuse.

Covered in Chapter 18. The general form is worth remembering: an attack on a person's reasoning is much harder than an attack on their *classification* and their *isolation*. Rename the situation and the rules attached to the old name will not fire. Keep them alone and hurried and the loop closes. And the reference is the prize — move a person's zero and everything they do afterwards is measured against a world the operator supplied.

## Institutions and groups

**Parts:** rails, sequencer, reference, ground, crystal, XOR, majority logic.

Everything in this book scales. A team has supply rails (what work can actually start), a reference (what counts as a normal week), a crystal (the person whose steadiness others lock to), and failure modes that look exactly like an individual's: brownout, resonance, a threshold nobody can cross, a rule whose trigger condition was quietly renamed. An organisation's fuse often cannot fire for the same reason a person's cannot — the threshold is above every level the system can reach.

## Teaching and being taught

**Parts:** transformer, ADC, DAC, PLL, probe.

A transformer changes level without changing content and isolates the two sides — which is the precise statement of why explaining something to a beginner is not a diluted version of the real thing. The ADC and the DAC are the two halves of learning anything: naming the continuous difference, then rendering the name back into practice.

## Self-knowledge

**Parts:** probe, net label, meter, reference, differential pair.

The probe observes without joining in; the net label turns a bundle of observations into one named wire; the meter insists that a number carry its method; the differential pair removes everything you share with the situation and shows you what is left, which is you; and the reference asks the question that does more work than any other single question in this book — *what am I measuring this against, and who set that zero?*

And the question underneath all of them, from Chapter 2: **whose board are you running?** Which parts of your behaviour did you place, deliberately, and which were installed by somebody else for a purpose that was never yours? The inherited ones are not faults. They are components somebody else chose, still running, and the only way to tell the difference is to read the schematic and ask who designed each stage.

## Theatre, fiction, and ritual

**Parts:** transformer, oscillator, crystal, PLL, reference, relay.

A play is a deliberately built circuit that runs on an audience. A ritual is a fixed sequence with a crystal at its centre — the same words, the same hour, the same order — and the point of the fixity is that it holds when the participants cannot. Fiction is a transformer: it carries content across a gulf between one person's life and another's without either side touching the other directly, which is precisely why it can say things that a direct statement cannot.

## The map is not the territory

Two warnings, both of which the framework itself insists on.

**First: no part of this is a diagnosis.** These are structural descriptions. A structure that fits is not a condition you have, and fitting several of them is not a syndrome.

**Second: the components are neutral and the description is not a cure.** Knowing that a repeated argument is a resonance does not lower its Q. Knowing that the fuse is mislabelled does not relabel it. The value is that the right lever becomes visible — and then somebody still has to pull it, usually slowly, usually with help.

**Third: a map is not a design.** Everything in this chapter is description, and description is what the notation does. Turning any of it into a design means placing components on purpose — choosing a threshold, stating a default, deciding what fails safe — and taking responsibility for the fact that you chose them. That is the difference Chapter 2 draws between reporting a state and committing to one, and it is the difference between understanding your life and authoring part of it.

**Fourth, and the most hopeful thing in the book: the same expression can be run on radically different boards.** Two people with an identical description of what happens to them — same Noetic, same Domain, same Drive, same Register — can live completely different versions of it, because gain, thresholds, clamps, return paths, time constants and reachable boundaries are all decisions, and none of them is in the expression. You cannot change what kind of event you are dealing with. You can change its magnitude, its speed, its reversibility, and whether it fails safe. Most of what determines how a life goes was never in the event.
