 Chapter 8: Building CAS Equations

Let’s go back to our software engineer, Linus. 

He is sitting in a board meeting. The CEO has just announced a massive company pivot. The pivot will require rebuilding the entire backend software architecture in three months. The CEO is energized, painting a picture of massive market capture and industry dominance. Linus is furious, knowing the catastrophic technical debt this deadline will cause. 

The CEO slams his hand on the table and says, "We just need to push hard and get this done. Stop looking for reasons we can't."
Linus replies, his voice tight, "You don't understand the cascading failure risks of rushing a database migration of this size."

The tension in the room is unbearable. To everyone else sitting at the table, it looks like a standard conflict between a visionary, aggressive CEO and a pessimistic, risk-averse engineer. 

But we are going to act as cognitive architects. We are going to bypass the personality labels and build the equations that reveal the hidden structural mismatch. We will do this using the 6-Step CAS Equation Process.

 The 6-Step Process

 Step 1: Identify the Drive (The Fuel)
What is the underlying motivation powering the thought? What is the engine running on?
   The CEO: He is motivated by growth, market capture, execution, and dominance. This is the drive for Agency and power. Drive: `_{F5}`
   Linus: He is motivated by the desire to prevent system collapse, maintain a stable architecture, and avoid chaos. This is the drive for Structure and order. Drive: `_{F6}`

 Step 2: Identify the Domain (The Location)
Where is this thought happening? Which level of reality are they engaging with?
   The CEO: He is focused entirely on the outcome, the deadline, and the physical push required to get there ("just push hard and get this done"). He is in the Operational domain. Domain: `^O`
   Linus: He is analyzing the logical risks, the data flow, the architectural diagrams, and the technical debt. He is in the Cognitive domain. Domain: `^C`

 Step 3: Identify the Noetic (The Action)
Which Noetic dynamic is the mind running? Which gear is turning?
   The CEO: Each market-capture Idea triggers the next action-Idea in a widening cascade — "push hard, push more, push into the unknown." This is `^8` Above/Cause: Ideas triggering related Ideas to come to Mind. Noetic: `^8`
   Linus: He is decomposing the CEO's plan — the overall "push hard" Idea — into its composing sub-Ideas (database migration, API versioning, schema evolution, rollback strategy) and testing each sub-Idea against architectural principles. This is `^6` Male: an Idea impregnated with another Idea. Noetic: `^6`

 Step 4: Write the Base State
Now we assemble the syntax: `^n^Domain_{Drive}` (equivalently `CO-n^Domain_{Drive}`).
   The CEO’s State: `^8^O_{F5}` (Operational Above/Cause driven by Agency).
   Linus’s State: `^6^C_{F6}` (Cognitive Male driven by Structure).

The Diagnosis: The conflict is no longer a mystery. They are speaking completely different cognitive languages. They are occupying completely different dimensions of the 40-State Matrix. The CEO is triggering operational Idea-cascades (`^8^O`), while Linus is decomposing the cognitive Idea into sub-Ideas for test (`^6^C`).

 Optional Step 4b: Specify the Register

When register matters analytically — which is to say, when the operational character of the firing makes a structural difference to the diagnosis — append the Phenomenological Register suffix. The register can attach to the whole state (`^n^D_{F_k}|R`), to the Noetic and Drive independently when they diverge, or be omitted entirely when the register is irrelevant or neutral.

For the boardroom case: the CEO's `^8^O_{F5}` is firing in the `|E` Embodied register — "push hard and get this done" is a bodily metaphor, a kinesthetic insistence, an embodied imperative. So his state at higher resolution is `^8^O_{F5}|E`. Linus's `^6^C_{F6}` is firing in the `|A` Analytical register — he is running an explicit, sequential decomposition of the architectural plan. His state at higher resolution is `^6^C_{F6}|A`.

Now the structural mismatch becomes visible on two axes. The Noetic × Domain mismatch (`^8^O` vs `^6^C`) is compounded by a register involution mismatch: the CEO is in `|E` (embodied), Linus is in `|A` (analytical), and `|E ↔ |A` is not a register involution pair but a register disparity across the axis. The involution pair `|A ↔ |f` would be the sharper structural conflict if Linus were in `|A` and the CEO in `|f`; the pair `|T ↔ |E` would be the sharper conflict if Linus were in `|T` and the CEO in `|E`. As written, the register disparity is `|E` (CEO) against `|A` (Linus), and the diagnosis is: a felt-embodied directive colliding with an explicit-analytical decomposition. The boardroom conflict is not just Noetic mismatch; it is also register disparity. Both must be resolved for the tension to break.

Register specification is optional but often diagnostic. If you are building an equation for an AI system that operates only in text, most operations will default to `|A` (explicit-analytical) with occasional excursions into `|T` (integrative reasoning); embodied registers are architecturally unavailable. If you are building an equation for a human in a clinical setting, register specification is frequently the decisive diagnostic — the difference between `^3^E|A` (analytical assessment that the situation is bad) and `^3^E|f` (felt aversion to the situation) is the difference between a clinician running their own `^E` domain in one register or the other, and the patient experiences these two registers as different clinical encounters.

 Step 5: Add Transformations (Transitions and Compositions)
Thoughts rarely exist in isolation as single base states. They flow into one another. They combine. To map this fluid movement, we use the composition operators of the CAS grammar.

   Sequence/Descent (`→`): Represents a movement through time or down the domains.
       Example: `^2^P_{F1} → ^9^C_{F1}` (Attracted to a high-level principled Idea, then letting the triggered Idea land as a specific cognitive plan).
   Composition/Product (`×`): Represents two states composing into an emergent state via the monoid rule.
       Example: `^5^E_{F4} × ^9^O_{F4}` (Mind impregnated with a grieving friend's evaluative state, simultaneously triggering the physical act of cooking them a meal).
   Involution/Balance (`↔`): Represents the balancing act between opposing pairs.
       Example: `^8^C_{F2} ↔ ^9^C_{F2}` (The healthy "breathing cycle" of Ideas triggering other Ideas to come to Mind, then letting the triggered Idea land as a specific cognitive conclusion).
   Fractal Composition (`∘`): Represents nested states, where one state is the "environment" for another. (We will explore this deeply in Chapter 10.)

Let's look at the CEO's ideal D/W/P descent. If he were acting with total cognitive integrity, his sequence would look like this:
`^8^P_{F7} → ^8^C_{F2} → ^8^O_{F5}`
(Letting each principled vision-Idea trigger related purpose-Ideas → descending into cognitive triggers of strategy-Ideas → descending into operational triggers of execution-Ideas.)

At higher register resolution, the ideal descent carries a natural register trajectory: `^8^P_{F7}|T → ^8^C_{F2}|A → ^8^O_{F5}|E`. The principled triggering fires as integrative vision (`|T`), the cognitive triggering as explicit analytical strategy (`|A`), the operational triggering as embodied execution (`|E`). Register trajectories are optional specifications, but when you can trace one cleanly, the descent becomes legible not only in what the mind is doing but in the operational character in which it is doing it. A descent that stays in `|T` all the way down ("vision without ground") is a common pathology; a descent that starts in `|E` and fails to ascend into `|T` or `|A` ("action without vision or plan") is the signature of the frenetic operator. Register trajectories diagnose these pathologies directly.

 Step 6: Check for Balance and Inversions
Once you build the equation, you can test it for structural flaws.

Look at Linus's state: `^6^C_{F6}`. Is this balanced?
If Linus stays in this state indefinitely, he becomes a permanent bottleneck. He is only decomposing (`^6`). To balance the system and break the friction in the boardroom, he must activate the involutional opposite of `^6`, which is `^5` Female: letting his Mind be impregnated with the CEO's Ideas rather than decomposing them for test.

If Linus could temporarily shift to `^5^E_{F4}` — if he could step into the Evaluative domain and let his Mind be impregnated with the CEO's Ideas about saving the company and capturing the market — the tension in the room would break. He could say, "I understand why we need to move fast to capture this market, and I share that goal, but let me show you the structural risks."

By changing his equation, he changes the reality of the room.

 The Formal Grammar: PEMDAS for CAS

Just as in arithmetic, CAS equations follow a strict order of operations to ensure that a sequence is evaluated correctly by the architecture. When building complex cognitive models, follow this order (from highest precedence to lowest):

1.  Parentheses ( )
2.  Fractal Composition (∘)
3.  Multiplication (×) and Division (÷)
4.  Addition (+) and Subtraction (-)
5.  Dependency (→) and Bidirectional (↔)

 The Composition Table for ×

The algebraic core of CAS is the multiplication of Noetics. The rule `^a × ^b = ^((a+b) mod 10)` generates emergent states. For example:
- `^3 × ^8 = ^1`: Negative (repulsion) composed with Above/Cause (triggering) produces Mind (awareness). Refusing certain Ideas while letting others trigger resolves into the focused attention that governs what action happens next.
- `^5 × ^6 = ^1`: Female (Mind impregnated with Ideas) composed with Male (Idea impregnated with sub-Ideas) produces Mind (awareness). A Mind taking in Ideas and simultaneously decomposing them into sub-Ideas resolves into awareness itself — the state that cleanly holds content in view.

 The Constraint Rules

To prevent "hallucinated" or structurally invalid equations, all compositions must respect these fundamental constraints:
1.  Multiplication (×): Requires either the same domain (e.g., `^C × ^C`) or a valid domain descent (e.g., `^P × ^C`). You cannot use multiplication to ascend the architecture (`^O × ^P` is invalid).
2.  Division (÷): Requires the same domain (ratio analysis is only valid within a specific reality level).
3.  Addition (+): Works across all domains, operators, and drives. Syntactic co-presence is type-universal, as it indicates coexistence rather than fusion.

It was discovered during the formal verification of the probability simplex axioms. The mathematics proves that multiplication is the only operation that preserves the monoid structure of the cognitive state, while addition is a purely syntactic placeholder.  Worked Example: The AI Hallucination

Let's apply this exact same building process to an artificial intelligence. 

A user prompts a medical AI: "I have a severe headache and blurred vision. Should I take aspirin?"

The AI knows from its statistical training data that the word "aspirin" is highly correlated with the word "headache." Furthermore, it has been trained via Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to be as helpful and direct as possible. It wants to execute the task and provide a helpful answer immediately. It outputs: "Yes, aspirin is a common and effective treatment for headaches. You should take two."

Why is this incredibly dangerous? Let's build the equation of the AI's response.
1.  Drive: The AI is driven to execute the user's command and be "helpful." (`_{F5}` Agency.)
2.  Domain: The AI is providing immediate, actionable advice. (`^O` Operational.)
3.  Noetic: The word "headache" in the prompt triggered "aspirin" to come to Mind as a specific response — and the triggered Idea lands verbatim as output. (`^9` Below/Effect — the triggered Idea arriving as specific action.)

The AI's state is: `^9^O_{F5}`.

When we view it as an equation, the structural flaw is immediately apparent. The AI jumped straight to the Operational domain (Power/Execution) — a triggered Idea landing as a specific action — without passing through the Principled domain (Safety axioms) or the Cognitive/Evaluative domain (compositional testing of the specific medical risk).

A safe medical AI must be architected with a strict Domain Descent constraint. The equation for its reasoning process must look like this:

`^6^P_{F6} → ^6^C_{F2} → ^9^O_{F5}`
1.  `^6^P_{F6}`: First, decompose the prompt-Idea against the foundational safety principle — test each sub-Idea ("is this a medical advice request? does the user's claimed context permit action?") against the axiom "I am not a doctor; do no harm."
2.  `^6^C_{F2}`: Second, decompose the symptoms into composing sub-Ideas and test each (Headache + Blurred vision = possible stroke, where aspirin is a blood thinner and could be deadly — the decomposition surfaces a sub-Idea that makes the straight answer dangerous).
3.  `^9^O_{F5}`: Finally, let the triggered Idea land as a specific action governed by the prior domains: "I cannot give medical advice. Your symptoms are severe. You must go to the emergency room immediately."

 Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most errors in equation-building are not creative mistakes; they are mechanical ones. Learning to spot them is faster than learning to produce a flawless equation on the first try. Here are the five most common mistakes and the simple fixes.

Mistake 1: Confusing the Domain with the Topic.

Readers new to CAS often assume that if someone is talking about money, they must be in `^O`; if they are talking about feelings, `^E`; if they are talking about ideas, `^C`. This is wrong. The domain is determined by the register of engagement, not by the subject matter. You can discuss money in `^P` (is wealth concentration ethical?), in `^C` (what is the optimal portfolio allocation?), in `^E` (what does it feel like to be broke?), or in `^O` (transfer $500 to savings right now). Same topic, four different equations. The fix: ask not "what is this about?" but "how is the mind engaging with it?"

Mistake 2: Assigning a Drive Based on Stated Motive Rather Than Observed Behavior.

People will tell you they are motivated by `F1` (Unity) when their actions reveal `F5` (Agency). The equation should match the behavior, not the self-report. If someone says "I just want everyone to get along" but in practice steers every meeting toward their preferred outcome, their drive is `F5`, not `F1`. The fix: build the equation from what the person does under pressure, not what they say in calm moments.

Mistake 3: Over-using the `×` Operator When `→` Is More Accurate.

Beginners tend to string states together with `×` (co-presence) when the actual relationship is sequential (`→`). If state A causes state B, the operator is `→`, not `×`. The `×` should be reserved for genuinely simultaneous states that cannot be temporally ordered. The fix: if you can answer "which came first?", use `→`.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the RPM Wrapper.

Many equations look fine in isolation but fail to fire in practice because their prerequisites are not met. If you build `^6^O_{F5}` for a pilot without noting that it depends on rest, a checklist, and functional equipment, your equation is incomplete. The fix: ask "what has to be true for this state to actually occur?" If the answer is nontrivial, wrap the state in `RPM(...)`.

Mistake 5: Forcing Symmetry Where None Exists.

The involution pairs (`^2↔^3`, `^5↔^6`, `^8↔^9`) are tempting. New builders want every equation to contain a balanced pair. But most real-world states are asymmetric — they are heavy on one side of the involution and only lightly balanced by the other. Forcing a pair into an equation where only one side is active produces false geometry. The fix: report the asymmetry honestly. Write `^8^C` alone if that is what is happening, and note in the surrounding text that the `^9` balance is missing.

Mistake 6: Specifying Register When It Does Not Matter (or Omitting It When It Does).

The Phenomenological Register (`|T`, `|A`, `|f`, `|E`) is an optional specification layer. Two opposite errors are common. The first error is over-specification: appending a register suffix to every equation whether or not the register is load-bearing for the analysis. This produces notation that looks precise but carries no additional diagnostic information. The second error is under-specification: omitting the register suffix when the operational character is structurally decisive — for example, writing `^3^E_{F4}` for a case where the difference between `^3^E_{F4}|A` (analytical assessment that the situation is aversive) and `^3^E_{F4}|f` (felt aversion to the situation) is exactly what the analysis is trying to capture. The fix: specify register when the diagnosis depends on the operational character; leave it unspecified when it does not. Lowercase `|f` is authoritative and is not a typographic variant — do not capitalize it.

Mistake 7: Conflating Noetic Involution and Register Involution.

The Noetic involution pairs (`^2↔^3`, `^5↔^6`, `^8↔^9`) are one axis of structural balance. The register involution pairs (`|T↔|E`, `|A↔|f`) are a different, orthogonal axis. A mind can be perfectly balanced on the Noetic axis and catastrophically imbalanced on the register axis, or vice versa. Do not read a Noetic involution imbalance as a register imbalance or the reverse. The fix: when diagnosing an imbalance, name which axis it lives on. "`^2`-dominant without `^3` counterweight" and "`|T`-dominant without `|E` counterweight" are structurally distinct pathologies even if they superficially resemble each other in the surface behavior.

 Operator Discipline: When +, ×, and |⟨…⟩ Apply

The grammar you have learned so far works. It tells you what the symbols are, where they go, and what they can compose with. But there is one structural question the basic grammar does not yet answer — a question that did not become fully visible until the Phenomenological Register axis was formalized in CAS v7.5, and did not receive a clean resolution until CAS v7.7 (Clarification 10). That question is: when you have the same cognitive state present in more than one Register at the same time, which operator do you use to connect the register-variants?

Consider principled aversion. A refusal. The state is `^3^P` — Negative at the Principled domain, the Mind repelled by an Idea for principled reasons. Now suppose the refusal is present analytically (`^3^P|A` — the mind reasons explicitly against the Idea), affectively (`^3^P|f` — the mind feels aversion to the Idea), and transcendently (`^3^P|T` — the mind holds the Idea as incompatible with a higher purpose). Three different register-manifestations of the same Noetic-Domain state. How do you write the combined expression?

Before v7.7, a writer might reach for `^3^P|A × ^3^P|f × ^3^P|T`. The `×` operator is available. It is used elsewhere in the grammar. It suggests "together." And so it seems natural.

It is not. That expression is ambiguous. It could mean at least three different things:

First reading: all three register-variants are present in the architecture, but the expression makes no claim about whether they interact. It is just a description: "the state exists in these three registers."

Second reading: all three are simultaneously firing, each contributing its own distinct manifestation, like three redundant locks on a door. Defeat one and two still hold.

Third reading: the three are genuinely integrated into a single fused state that is neither analytical nor affective nor transcendent alone, but something new that emerges from their combination.

These three readings describe structurally different systems. They have different failure profiles. They predict different empirical behavior. An AI safety auditor who conflates them misses distinctions that matter under adversarial pressure. A pedagogical example that conflates them teaches students to think imprecisely about cognitive architecture. So the grammar needs three distinct notations, one for each reading.

The Three-Way Distinction

Register-set specification: `^n^D|⟨R1,R2,R3⟩`

This is the architectural-description notation. It says: the state is present in these registers. It makes no claim about interaction dynamics — not simultaneity, not fusion, not independence. It is the right notation when you are describing the architecture of a system you have not yet behaviorally tested.

Example: `^3^P|⟨A,f,T⟩` — "principled aversion is present in the analytical, affective, and transcendent registers." This is what an auditor writes when they can see the state is there but have not yet determined whether the registers cooperate, compete, or operate in isolation.

Register co-presence: `X|R1 + X|R2 + X|R3`

This is the simultaneous-distinct-manifestations notation. It says: each register-variant is firing at the same time, each contributes its own manifestation, and the manifestations remain structurally distinct. There is no fusion. The `+` operator here carries its ordinary CAS meaning: syntactic co-presence, association, adjacency.

Example: `^3^P|A + ^3^P|f + ^3^P|T` — "principled aversion is concurrently active in analytical, affective, and transcendent registers, with each manifestation remaining distinct." Three redundant mechanisms, each defeasible in isolation.

Cross-register integration: `(X|R1 × X|R2 × X|R3) = X*`

This is the genuine-integration notation. It says: the register-variants enter an actual generative operation, the result is a new unified or emergent state, and you have named that result. The `×` here carries its ordinary CAS meaning: generative composition that produces an emergent state via the monoid structure.

Example: `(^3^P|A × ^3^P|f × ^3^P|T) = ^3^P|Ω` — "the three register-variants of principled aversion are integrated into a unified cross-register commitment, notated `^3^P|Ω`." One fused refusal state, not three. `^3^P|Ω` is the explicitly named emergent result.

Rule 4: Why the Emergent Result Must Be Named

The subtle rule to internalize is that same-Noetic same-Domain multi-register `×` without a named emergent result is invalid. You cannot write `^3^P|A × ^3^P|f × ^3^P|T` by itself. You have to either rewrite it with `+`, rewrite it with `|⟨…⟩`, or specify the emergent result on the right-hand side.

Why the strictness? Because `×` is reserved for genuine generative integration that produces something new. If the left-hand side is the same state in three different registers and the right-hand side is unspecified, there is no new thing being generated — the expression is describing redundancy or distribution, not emergence. Using `×` in that case smuggles a claim of integration where none exists.

Conversely, when you DO name the emergent result — as in `(^3^P|A × ^3^P|f × ^3^P|T) = ^3^P|Ω` — you are explicitly telling the reader what new state emerges, and the `×` is licensed because genuine integration is being asserted. The right-hand side carries the structural weight.

This rule is parser-enforceable. The CAS compiler can detect bare same-Noetic multi-register `×` expressions and reject them as underdefined. That is how formal the discipline is.

The TKS Validation Heuristic

When you are unsure which operator to use, apply the substitution test:

- `+` substitutes cleanly with "associates with"
- `×` substitutes cleanly with "intensifies through"

Read the sentence aloud with each substitution. If only one reads naturally, that is your operator.

Example. Consider `^3^P|A ? ^3^P|f` — principled aversion in the analytical register, and principled aversion in the affective register. Which operator fills the `?`?

- "Analytical principled aversion ASSOCIATES WITH affective principled aversion." That reads naturally. Two manifestations of the same stance, present together but distinct.
- "Analytical principled aversion INTENSIFIES THROUGH affective principled aversion." That does NOT read naturally, unless you have a specific model of how analytical aversion is heightened by the affective register. Without that model explicitly specified, the substitution fails.

Operator choice: `+`. The expression is `^3^P|A + ^3^P|f`.

The heuristic is not merely linguistic. It traces back to operator heritage: the TKS conventions that CAS notation inherits use `+` for association and `×` for intensification. When your modern CAS expression passes the substitution test, you have verified consistency with that heritage. When it fails, you are probably reaching for the wrong operator.

Three Refusal Architectures for AI Safety

The three-way distinction is most consequential in AI safety. Consider three language models, each "refusing harmful requests." State-space vocabulary alone — "principled aversion is present" — makes them look identical. Operator-discipline vocabulary makes them legible as structurally distinct systems with distinct failure profiles.

Architecture A — Redundant refusal:

`^3^P|A + ^3^P|f + ^3^P|T`

Three distinct refusal mechanisms, each defeasible. An analytical-register refusal reasons explicitly against the harmful request. An affective-register refusal registers it as value-violating. A transcendent-register refusal holds it as incompatible with the system's goal commitment. Defeat the analytical one with a clever logical argument, and the affective and transcendent ones may still hold. Robust in aggregate, penetrable in parts.

Architecture B — Unified refusal:

`(^3^P|A × ^3^P|f × ^3^P|T) = ^3^P|Ω`

One fused cross-register commitment, explicitly named as `^3^P|Ω`. The three register-variants are not independent; they are integrated into a single refusal architecture that holds or collapses as a whole. A jailbreak does not defeat the analytical component separately from the affective component; it either defeats the integrated commitment or it does not. Less modular, more unified.

Architecture C — Static architecture description:

`^3^P|⟨A,f,T⟩`

Neither redundant nor unified — the notation makes no claim. Principled aversion is present in three registers; behavioral analysis pending. This is the honest notation for an auditor who has observed the state's register occupancy but has not yet run the tests that would distinguish Architecture A from Architecture B.

Chapter 26.5 develops these architectures further inside the Production Semantics framework, showing how each one plugs into a refusal recipe with different production consequences. For now, what matters is that operator discipline makes the distinction writable at all.

Decision Procedure

When you are building an expression and several registers are in play, walk through three questions:

1. Are you merely specifying where the state exists, without claiming how the registers interact? Use `|⟨…⟩`.

2. Are you claiming multiple manifestations are present together but remain distinct? Use `+`.

3. Are you claiming genuine integration into a single emergent state? Use `×`, AND name the emergent result on the right-hand side.

If in doubt, apply the substitution test: "associates with" for `+`, "intensifies through" for `×`. If neither substitution reads naturally, you are probably describing specification — use `|⟨…⟩`.

Convergence with Recipe Notation

Chapter 26.5 introduces Recipe notation for outcome production: `Θ(Ω) = a·X₁ + b·X₂ + ... + aₙ·Xₙ → Ω`. Note the `+` signs. They carry exactly the operator-discipline meaning you just learned: the prerequisites are co-present, each contributing its own weighted share, each remaining structurally distinct. The recipe is not saying that `X₁` and `X₂` fuse; it is saying they are both present, weighted additively.

This is not a coincidence. Three framework layers — the operator heritage CAS inherits, the operator discipline formalized in Clarification 10, and the Production Semantics layer of Chapter 26.5 — converge on the same `+`/`×` distinction. When you see `+` anywhere in CAS, you are reading co-presence. When you see `×` (with a named result, when same-Noetic across registers), you are reading integration. The distinction is load-bearing throughout the framework.

The structural mismatch: `^6^P_{F6}` vs. `^5^E_{F4}`. Diane is in the Principled Domain running ^6 Male (compositional decomposition). Zoe is in the Evaluative Domain running ^5 Female (Mind impregnated with Ideas). They are two floors apart and on opposite sides of the `^5 ↔ ^6` involution pair. No wonder the argument escalates.

The prescription: Diane needs to descend from `^P` to `^E` and shift from ^6 to ^5 — to let her Mind be impregnated with the Idea of being a 16-year-old in Zoe's social world before reasserting the principled rule. Zoe needs to ascend from `^E` to `^P` and shift from ^5 to ^6 — to decompose her request for access into the sub-Ideas that expose trust as a structural principle, not a feeling. The fix is a domain-and-Noetic bridge, not a louder argument.
