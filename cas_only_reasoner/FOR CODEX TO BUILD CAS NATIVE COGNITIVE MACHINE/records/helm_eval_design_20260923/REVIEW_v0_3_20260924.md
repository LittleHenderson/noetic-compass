## BLOCKER

### B1. Too few real decoy problems, and the cluster unit can hide it

**The rule.** Section 3.2: each analogy MATCH item needs at least 3 decoys with the focus's degree multiset but
different wiring. Each NO_MATCH item needs at least 3 such worlds too. So every analogy focus needs **at least 3
same-degree, non-isomorphic classes** in TEST ∪ WITHHELD.

**Measured at 5 nodes (all 267 classes).**
- Only **30 of 267** classes have 3 or more same-degree partners anywhere. They form just **6** degree groups, of sizes
  4, 4, 5, 5, 6 and 6.
- With the real `split_five` over 300 throwaway salts, TEST_NEW_5 foci with at least 3 same-degree partners inside
  TEST ∪ WITHHELD: **mean 5.12, minimum 0, maximum 11** (10th percentile 3, 90th percentile 8).

**Measured at 6 nodes.**
- If WITHHELD_6 is drawn blindly by the sampler (120 more shapes, as written), TEST foci with at least 3 same-degree
  partners inside the 180 TEST ∪ WITHHELD shapes: **2 to 9, mean 4.83** (12 throwaway salts).
- If the decoys are instead taken from the **full** 5,074-class list (minus DEV), **20 to 28** of 60 TEST foci
  qualify, in **16 to 23** distinct degree groups (4 throwaway salts).

**The attack.** Section 4 defines an analogy cluster as (focus class, multiset of bank classes, status). Two banks
for the same focus with one decoy swapped are two "clusters". At 5 nodes a builder can make, say, 10 banks for each of
about 5 foci and print 50 or more clusters and a large W. But the real evidence is about 5 shapes in at most 6 degree
groups. This is the v0.1 failure ("80 items" were about 7 clusters) in a new form. The 10-cluster floor would pass
while the evidence would not.

Items that share a degree group are not independent, even across foci. Focus F1 against decoys {F2, F3, F4} and
focus F2 against {F1, F3, F4} are the same discrimination problem.

**Fix.**
1. Analogy cluster = **focus class**. All items with that focus form one cluster, right only if every item and every
   presentation is right. Print also the number of **distinct degree groups**. Apply the 10-floor to both numbers.
2. Correspondence NO_MATCH cluster = the **unordered** pair {focus class, target class}, with at most one NO_MATCH
   cluster per focus. Or cluster NO_MATCH items by focus.
3. At 6 nodes, build WITHHELD_6 on purpose from the **degree groups of the TEST foci**, taken from the full class
   list. Enumeration takes under 1 second. Better still, split 6-node classes **by whole degree group**, so that a
   group belongs wholly to the DEV side or wholly to the TEST side (this also fixes M7).
4. Fix now which TEST foci enter analogy: all TEST classes with at least 3 eligible partners. Print how many and how
   they differ from the rest of TEST (they lean to shapes with common degree lists).
5. At 5 nodes, declare `ANALOGY_STRUCTURE_TRANSFER_5` **`NOT_TESTABLE` now**, before any build. The whole 5-node
   universe has only 6 degree groups with 4 or more members, so it can never reach 10 independent units. Keep 5-node
   analogy as a diagnostic, or change its recipe and say so.

## MAJOR

### M1. Correspondence: the bar misses "guess by degree, then check"

The registered `degree-sorted matching` answers NO_MATCH only when the degree multisets differ. By construction they
never differ in this test. So it always answers MAPPING, and is always wrong on the 30% NO_MATCH items.

A rule that is just as cheap does better. Guess the map by sorting nodes by degree, check that it keeps every edge
(one pass over the edges, no search), and answer NO_MATCH if the check fails.
- On a NO_MATCH item no map keeps the edges, so this rule is **always right**.
- On a MAPPING item it is right when its one guess is valid in both presentations.

**Measured over all classes** (chance a random degree-respecting guess is valid = |automorphisms| / product of the
factorials of the degree-class sizes, squared for 2 presentations):

| Level | One degree-sorted guess valid in both presentations | The same with 1-round neighbour-degree sorting |
|---|---|---|
| 5 nodes | 0.7439 | 0.9803 |
| 6 nodes | 0.6086 | 0.9810 (stable refinement 0.9898) |

At a 70/30 mix, the guess-and-check bar is about 0.3 + 0.7 x 0.7439 = **0.8207** at 5 nodes and **0.7260** at 6 nodes.
The registered degree-sorted bar is about **0.5207** and **0.4260**. So W falls by half or more: at 6 nodes from
about 0.57 to about 0.27 of the clusters, and at 5 nodes from about 0.48 to about 0.18. (These use the uniform class
mix; the real TEST mix will differ a little.)

**Fix.** Register `DEGREE_GUESS_THEN_CHECK`. Also register a flag-anchored version: map the declared whole and the
participants first, then guess by degree, then check (see M2). Re-print W and power. State the line the design draws:
a bar may make **one** guess and check it, but may not search.

### M2. Flags leak structure, and the trained course taught the leak

Helm reads `is_declared_whole` and the participant rank for **every** world in an analogy packet
(`pointer_inputs.py`, world-choice kinds), and for the target in correspondence.

**What the trained course did.** I measured the 64 training families: 64 distinct (domain, kind) pairs, 6 archetypes.
Inside each archetype, **all families share one labelled edge list** (6 of 6). So in every trained analogy and
correspondence pair, the whole and both participants sit on **matching nodes**. "Match the flagged nodes" was always
right in training.

**The natural build repeats it.** HSET-1A builds a second presentation by relabelling the same spec, and the `primary`
nodes follow the structure. A partner or target built the same way keeps its flags on the image nodes.

**Measured** (MATCH items; the partner plus 3 same-degree decoys, all of other domains; flags of each decoy drawn at
random; one presentation):

| Level (items) | Partner flags follow the nodes: flag rule | ...: degree rule | Partner flags redrawn: flag rule | ...: degree rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 nodes (8,688) | **0.903** | 0.247 | 0.060 | 0.257 |
| 5 nodes (90) | **0.822** | 0.167 | 0.111 | 0.256 |

The flag rule: pick a same-degree candidate whose whole and two participants have the same (in, out) degrees as the
focus's.

**Fix.**
1. For **every** world in a bank (focus, partner, distractor, decoys) and for every correspondence target, draw the
   declared whole and the participants **fresh for that world, by one rule**: whole = a uniformly random root;
   participants = uniformly random other nodes. Never copy them from the focus.
2. Register `FLAG_ANCHOR` comparators for analogy and correspondence.
3. Print, per status, how often the partner's flags land on images of the focus's flags.
4. Also draw each world's **kind** word at random from its domain, never "the training partner's kind". Every trained
   kind word belonged to exactly one family, so to exactly one shape.

### M3. MATCH and NO_MATCH banks can give the status away

Section 3.2 fixes the NO_MATCH bank ("every non-focus world has the focus's node and edge counts, and at least 3 have
its degree multiset"). It does not fix the MATCH bank the same way. Three leaks follow:
- **Count leak.** MATCH = partner + 3 same-degree decoys gives 4 same-degree candidates. NO_MATCH with exactly 3 gives
  3. Counting them tells the status. MATCH fillers with other edge counts also differ from NO_MATCH banks.
- **Domain leak.** Decoy domains are not fixed. A decoy in the focus's own domain is removed for free by the required
  domain check. So "3 decoys" can mean fewer real ones.
- **Distractor leak.** The same-domain isomorphic distractor appears only in MATCH items. Its presence then signals
  MATCH.

**Fix.**
1. Build each NO_MATCH bank as the **twin** of a MATCH bank, with the partner swapped for one more same-degree,
   non-isomorphic, other-domain decoy. (At 5 nodes this needs degree groups of 5 or more; see B1.)
2. Require **at least 3 same-degree decoys of other domains** than the focus.
3. Put the same-domain isomorphic distractor in **both** statuses at the same rate. In NO_MATCH it is a genuine
   near-miss: the same wiring in the same domain, so still no match.
4. Assert at build that the per-bank profile has the same distribution in both statuses. The profile is the counts of
   worlds with the focus's node count, edge count, degree multiset, and domain.
5. Register a status comparator: "NO_MATCH if fewer than m same-degree other-domain candidates", with m chosen as its
   best value on the cell.

### M4. Reach at 6 nodes: two-hop is beaten, but a three-hop lookup nearly is not

| Draw | Rule | Primary clusters per state (k) | W against two-hop | W against three-hop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEV, public seed | first-child | 110 | 36 | **4** |
| DEV, public seed | BFS | 121 | 36 | **4** |
| TEST-like, 8 throwaway salts | first-child | 98 to 129 | 24 to 38 (mean 29.6) | **3 to 11 (mean 6.1)** |
| TEST-like, 8 throwaway salts | BFS | 106 to 136 | 25 to 37 (mean 29.8) | **3 to 11 (mean 6.1)** |

The three-hop rule is `TWO_HOP_FIRST_CHILD` one step deeper. First-child rule: VIA the lowest-rank child with a path
of at most 2 steps to the part. BFS rule: VIA the lowest-rank node 1 or 2 steps from the whole with an arrow to the
part. It learns nothing, just like two-hop.

So `TWO_HOP_FIRST_CHILD` no longer reaches the ceiling: its accuracy on the balanced primary cells is about 0.87 to
0.94. But a fixed three-hop lookup would be right on about 0.96 to 0.99 of them. (These are 1 − W / 3k from the
table.) A model that learned only "look three hops" would pass `REACH_TRANSFERS_TO_NEW_STRUCTURE_6`. That is the v0.2
problem moved one hop.

**Fix (the owner chooses before build).** Register `THREE_HOP_FIRST_CHILD` and print its W. Then either:
- keep two-hop as the bar, and name the claim for what it shows, for example `REACH_3_STEP_TRANSFER_6`: "follows
  3-step paths on unseen 6-node shapes; does not show 4-step paths"; or
- make three-hop the bar, and accept that the claim is most likely `NOT_TESTABLE`.

### M5. One neighbour round separates everything; scope the claim words

| Invariant (no learning) | Same-degree pairs it separates, 5 nodes (116 pairs) | 6 nodes (12,588 pairs) |
|---|---|---|
| longest path length | 65 | 6,551 |
| number of reachable pairs | 85 | 8,884 |
| each node's (in, out, descendants, ancestors) | 112 | 11,861 |
| **1 round** of directed neighbour-degree refinement | **116** | **12,579** |
| 2 rounds | 116 | **12,588** |

- Full directed colour refinement leaves **0** non-isomorphic pairs unseparated among all 267 five-node classes (my
  count). The builder's census reports 0 at 6 nodes too.
- So on this universe the "1-WL ceiling" **is** the exact checker. As a ceiling it adds nothing. As a bar it would make
  W = 0, and every analogy and correspondence claim would be `NOT_TESTABLE` for good. **Do not make it a bar.**
- Should the joint (in, out) multiset be a bar? **Yes.** It is the degree bar, and it matches the owner's words "the
  same simple counts". Longest path and the reachability counts describe wiring, not simple counts. Print them as named
  diagnostics per cell, not as bars.
- **Claim words.** At 6 nodes or fewer, a test can show "does better than degree counting". It cannot tell "full
  structural comparison" from "one hop of neighbour-degree matching". Write that sentence into each claim's definition
  now. The names `..._STRUCTURE_TRANSFER_...` otherwise promise more than the test can show.

### M6. The collision "requirement" cannot be met at 5 nodes and has no failure rule

Section 2 says at least half of TEST at each level must be in a degree-collision group inside TEST ∪ WITHHELD.
- **5 nodes.** Over 300 throwaway salts, TEST classes with any same-degree partner in the 207 non-DEV classes: **mean
  21.14, range 13 to 31**. At least 30 happened in **2 of 300** salts (0.67%). TEST_NEW_5 is fixed and may not be
  re-drawn. So this will almost surely fail, and the design does not say what happens.
- **6 nodes.** With a blind WITHHELD_6: **24 to 31, mean 27.5** of 60, so it fails most of the time. With WITHHELD_6
  built from degree groups (B1 fix 3), it holds trivially.

**Fix.** Say now what a miss does. At 5 nodes, make it "printed, not required". At 6 nodes, compute it after the B1
fix. Say explicitly that no salt is re-drawn to meet it.

### M7. DEV cannot copy the TEST analogy recipe

Section 2: "DEV banks draw only from DEV shapes."
- 6 nodes, public DEV seed: DEV shapes with at least 3 same-degree shapes inside DEV: **0 of 60** (with at least 1:
  13 of 60).
- 5 nodes, 300 throwaway salts: **mean 0.21 of 60**.

So DEV analogy and NO_MATCH items cannot be built the way TEST items are. The builder cannot smoke-test the decoy logic
on DEV. He is pushed to relax the recipe, or to reach outside DEV, which breaks custody.

**Fix.** At 6 nodes, split by degree group and add a public `DEV_DECOYS_6` from the DEV foci's groups (B1 fix 3). At
5 nodes there is no free class left, so say that DEV_5 analogy uses a relaxed, named recipe and is feedback only.

### M8. Which runs does a base claim use?

Section 5: each claim needs "every one of the 3 runs". The next experiment has **6** runs: 3 repaired and 3 unchanged.
Nothing says which 3 runs a base claim such as `ANALOGY_STRUCTURE_TRANSFER_6` reads. A reader could report whichever
arm passes. That is a forking path.

**Fix.** Evaluate every base claim **per arm**, and name it with its arm (for example
`ANALOGY_STRUCTURE_TRANSFER_6[repaired]` and `[unchanged]`). Report both, always.

### M9. The "no regression" clause is not program-ready

Section 5.1: the repaired run must not fail "a cell or gate that the unchanged run of the same seed passes", on
"every historical held-back gate and every other claim-bearing v0.3 cell".
- **No list.** "Every historical held-back gate" is not listed. Which gates count can then be chosen after results.
- **Noise.** It compares pass/fail across a threshold. Unchanged passing at p = 0.009 and repaired failing at
  p = 0.011 blocks the claim, though the arms may not differ at all. A large real loss is invisible when both arms fail.
  With about 8 cells and gates times 3 seeds, one noisy crossing is likely.
- **Undefined cases.** Pass/fail is not defined for a cell that reads `can_carry_a_claim: false`.

**Fix.**
1. List every gate now, by id, file and pass rule.
2. Define a regression as a paired test on the same clusters: unchanged right and repaired wrong (c′) against the
   reverse (b′), with regression iff c′ > b′ and the exact one-sided p ≤ 1/100. Also report threshold crossings by
   name, as information only.
3. Leave out cells that cannot carry a claim, and say so.

### M10. Say "joint (in, out) pairs", not "in/out-degree multiset"

"The same in/out-degree multiset" could mean one list of in-degrees plus one list of out-degrees. Those separate lists
are much weaker than the joint pairs. Among pairs that share both separate lists, the joint pairs still separate
**382 of 498** at 5 nodes and **63,139 of 75,727** at 6 nodes. Helm's own world vector carries separate in-degree and
out-degree statistics (`pointer_learner.py`, `IN_DEGREE_SUM_COLUMN` / `OUT_DEGREE_SUM_COLUMN`), so a builder could
reasonably pick the weaker reading. Decoys chosen that way would be beaten by the cheap joint-pair rule.

**Fix.** Define "degree multiset" as the sorted list of (in-degree, out-degree) pairs, `hset_worlds.degree_multiset`,
for decoys and for the bar alike.

### M11. The larger-question fixtures and the custody rule (BLOCKER at `33f83639`; mostly fixed by `9656dc19`)

**What v0.3 says.**
- Section 2: TEST and WITHHELD shapes "never appear in any training update, development evaluation, or candidate
  bank outside a TEST question".
- Section 1: no model is read on any v0.3 cell until the larger-question path (HELM-LARGER-QUESTION-PATH-1) passes
  its AWS tests.

**What that path does** (`BUILDER_REPORT_LARGER_QUESTION_PATH.md`, branch `claude/helm-builder-continuation-20260923`):
- Its public fixture file holds hand-made shapes S5A, S5B and S5C (5 nodes) and S6A, S6B and S6D (6 nodes).
- Its `MAIN` bank is "10 hand-made worlds of 4, 5 and 6 nodes": a candidate bank outside TEST.
- Its check b runs **the 3 trained Helm 2.0 finals** on all 37 answerable fixtures, and grades each with checker v2.
  That is a development evaluation of trained weights.
- The builder flagged the risk himself: "a public fixture shape might coincide with a sealed TEST shape."

**Why this was a blocker as written.** In v0.3, WITHHELD_5 is "the other 147 classes". So every 5-node class outside
DEV_NEW_5 is TEST or WITHHELD. S5A, S5B and S5C are common 4-edge shapes. Each is in DEV with a chance of about
60/267, so all three are in DEV with a chance of about 1%. The precondition v0.3 names would therefore break v0.3's own
custody rule, or trip the fail-closed manifest check at TEST time.

**What `9656dc19` fixed.** The custodian found 0 of the 7 fixture classes among the 60 TEST_NEW_5 classes, and
excluded all 7 from every HSET-2 TEST and WITHHELD draw. So no TEST shape was exposed. I measured the cost: S5A and
S5B form a 2-member degree group, and S5C has no same-degree partner. Excluding them does not touch the 6 large
5-node groups of B1. S6A and S6B sit in a 5-member 6-node group.

**What is still open.**
1. **Write the exclusion into the design.** v0.3 section 2 still defines WITHHELD_5 as "the other 147 classes". The
   next version must say that WITHHELD_5 is every class not in DEV_NEW_5, not in TEST_NEW_5, and not in a named,
   digest-listed public exclusion list. The build must assert it.
2. **Keep the list open-ended.** Any later public 5- or 6-node shape used with a trained model, or in a candidate bank,
   joins the exclusion list before the 6-node draw. Say in the design which public artefacts count as "development
   evaluation", and put them all in the manifest.
3. **A digest leak in the same checks.** Check b writes "graded, no error" and the checker's receipt digest, not the
   correct/incorrect flag. But `checker_v2.check_answer` digests a body that holds both the answer's digest and
   `"correct"`. The answer spaces are small (for example 9 analogy answers with an 8-world bank, or 8 reach answers at
   6 nodes). So anyone who holds the public fixture can try every answer, rebuild each digest, and read off both the
   prediction and whether it was right. This is harmless for the public fixtures. Never use this "no flag" pattern
   for sealed material.

## MINOR

- **m1. Loose numbers are post-hoc choices.** Replace "about 70%", "about 30%" and "about one item in four" with exact
  counts per cell. Fix the number of items and banks per focus, and which TEST classes are used as correspondence
  foci. Name the power grid (for example HSET-1A's `power_given_bar` grid: bar +0.10, +0.20, +0.30 and 1.0, plus the
  never-loses fractions 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0).
- **m2. Presentations for bank questions.** "A random relabelling of node ranks" must relabel **every** world in the
  bank, and the correspondence target, not only the focus. The candidate aliases W0.. come from sorted content-hash
  world ids. If only the focus is relabelled, the other candidates keep their order, and a position habit carries over
  between the two presentations.
- **m3. Correspondence packets need their own bank.** `answers._query_body` requires the target to be in
  `public["bank"]["world_ids"]`. `source.validate_public_source` caps the worlds at 65 and the bank at 64. The HSET-1A
  packet (the frozen 64-world bank plus the focus) has no room for a new target. Say what the correspondence bank holds
  (for example, the target alone) and what the 8-world analogy packet holds. This is new packet code, so the
  `hset2_build.py` review must cover it.
- **m4. NO_MATCH was very likely never trained.** Every trained correspondence target and every analogy bank held a
  same-archetype partner in another domain (`course._partners`), and same-archetype families share one edge list. So
  both kinds very likely always had a match in training. Print the trained answer states beside each cell, as HSET-1A
  did for the BFS rule (59 of 59 VIA).
- **m5. Choosing the bar.** "The best comparator on the cell" is fixed and deterministic. It is still safer to require
  the model to beat **every** registered comparator, each by the same sign test. This is an intersection-union test:
  it needs no multiplicity correction and removes any question of which comparator was the bar.
- **m6. Multiplicity across claims.** There are 6 base claims per arm, plus the paired claims. They are kept separate,
  which is fine. Still state the family-wise risk: up to about 0.01 per claim.

## NOTE

- **N1. Secrecy of 6-node TEST is weak; the manifest check is the real guard.** In 3,000 raw draws the MIX sampler
  produced only 974 distinct classes of 5,074, and its top 10 classes were 0.151 of the draws. An independent run of
  5,000 draws reproduced **52 of 60** throwaway-TEST classes. The HSET-1B spec measured 34 of 40 the same way. Keep the
  manifest check, and include in it DEV readings, the larger-question fixtures, and every smoke or probe input.
  TEST_NEW_5 has also already passed through the HSET-1A AWS build and D-1 check X. Record that in the custody note.
- **N2. Reach W is spread well enough.** At 6 nodes the W clusters come from 19 to 25 distinct shapes, at most 3 from
  one shape (public DEV draw and 4 throwaway salts). Print "distinct shapes behind W" per cell anyway.
- **N3. Checker v2 scope holds.** The analogy bank of 8 is within 64. The W0..W64 aliases fit. At most 48 self-maps
  per 6-node shape (builder census), within `MAX_ADMISSIBLE_ANSWERS = 64`. No 7-node world is in any bank. The
  larger-question path refuses any 7-node or degree-5 world anywhere in the source, which fits v0.3.
- **N4. What the repair can realistically show.** At 3 and 4 nodes the degree multiset identifies every shape. So the
  course never rewarded anything beyond degree matching, and Helm's world vector is handed explicit degree sums. A
  repair that makes Helm compare focus and candidate **by degree** would likely beat the unchanged arm in the paired
  test, yet stay at the degree bar. `REPAIR_IMPROVES_ANALOGY_6` would then fail. That is the correct reading. Make sure
  the per-cell paired result is still reported in that case, so the finding "focus-aware, but only by degree" is not
  lost.

## Direct answers to the six review questions

1. **Shortcuts.**
   - Analogy: flag anchoring (M2), count, domain and distractor status leaks (M3), and split-versus-joint degree
     (M10). The "only candidate with a distinct domain" and "NO_MATCH iff no candidate has the focus's edge count"
