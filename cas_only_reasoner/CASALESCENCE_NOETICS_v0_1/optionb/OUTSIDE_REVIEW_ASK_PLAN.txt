You are an independent reviewer of a study plan. You did not write it. Review the plan below for one purpose: find defects that would make the result wrong, unfair, leaky or unreadable. Report BLOCKER and MAJOR findings only. No style notes.

Background. A small frozen language model ("model B", 0.5 billion parameters) answers multiple-choice questions about short passages. The study asks whether B is right more often when its prompt also holds a "role note": a checked description of which words of the passage occupy which of nine roles (written ^1 to ^9), built by a program from entries that two independent label teams agreed on. The role note (group ROLE) is compared, on the same questions, with: no notes (NONE); same-length padding (FILLER); the same note with wrong role tags (MIXED); plain notes by a strong model told the kinds of question (ORD-INFORMED); plain notes by a strong model told nothing (ORD-FREE); and the same labels and quotes with no role tags (QUOTES-ONLY). There is one pass code, and it needs ROLE to beat all of these. The ten role definitions are fixed canon and are not under review.

Two changes the operator intends to make to this plan before it is sealed. Judge them too:
(1) Question writer. The plan names Gemini gemini-2.5-flash writing in batches of up to 8 passages per call, because the free tier allows about 20 requests a day; at that rate writing takes several days. Intended change: the question writer becomes DeepSeek deepseek-v4-pro, one passage per call, stateless, low reasoning effort. It never sees a label, a note, the labeling guide or a kind tag. Label team B and the ordinary-note writer are DeepSeek's smaller model (asked for as deepseek-chat, answered today by deepseek-flash); label team A was Claude Sonnet. So the question writer would be a different model from both label teams but from the same vendor as team B and as the ordinary-note writer.
(2) Support checker. The plan names Gemini as the checker that says pass or fail on "every sentence of this ordinary note is supported by the passage", with a text-only Claude Sonnet sub-agent as fallback after a day's block. Intended change: the text-only Claude Sonnet sub-agent is the checker from the start. It sees the passage and the note only.

Check in particular:
1. Leakage. Can the right answer, a label, a role note or the kind of a passage reach the question writer, a key solver, the ordinary-note writer or the support checker? Can a key reach a prompt? Do the two intended changes open a path, or tilt the comparison toward ROLE or toward a rival? Say which direction.
2. Fairness of the rivals. Is ORD-INFORMED a fair "strongest ordinary help"? Is anything given to ROLE that the rivals do not get, other than the role information itself? Is the length matching sound?
3. Selection. Questions are kept only when the words they turn on match an entry both label teams agreed on, and dropped when an option matches an entry only one team gave. The plan says QUOTES-ONLY measures the pointing advantage this gives. Is that enough? What can the pass code still not show?
4. Pre-registration. Is every rule, threshold, gate and outcome code fixed before model B runs? Name any choice that could still be made after results are seen, including through the practice gates, the reserve rule, the repair lines of the seal, or the pre-check jobs.
5. Statistics. The passage-level sign-flip test, the 5-point margin, the no-harm check, the exploratory tier (fewer than 200 final role-step tasks or fewer than 120 final passages), and the two readability rules that need both a 5-point gap and a test. Is anything wrong or easy to misread? Is "a miss does not mean no gain" stated clearly enough?
6. Complexity. The build has nine seal steps, repair lines, up to four pre-check jobs and one single paid GPU attempt. Name the parts that are not needed for a sound result and that add risk of an operator or program error. Name the simplest cut for each.
7. Claims. Does the plan say plainly what a pass and each kind of miss would and would not show, and that agreement between two label teams is not correctness?

For each finding give: severity, the exact place (section number), why it matters, and the smallest fix.
