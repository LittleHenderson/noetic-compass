[A9] Clarification 6, "Diagnostic application" (canon lines 219 to 224): the whole section.

When analyzing an example and classifying it in Noetic notation, pose two questions:

1. **What colloquialism would a native speaker use to describe this cognitive phenomenon?**
2. **Which Noetic's authoritative definition does that colloquialism align with?**

If the answer produces a clean match to a single Noetic, single-operator classification is supported. If the colloquialism invokes several Noetics simultaneously, the example probably needs a compound expression (often with ^5 Female as substrate per Clarification 5). If no colloquialism fits at all, the Noetic reading is drifting from authoritative semantics and must be reconciled.

[A10] Clarification 6, "What this clarification does NOT change" (canon line 253): the second bullet.

- Where a colloquialism and a canonical definition appear to disagree, the canonical definition governs; the colloquialism is either imprecise in that specific usage or the proposed example is misclassified.

## Part B — How to label in this study (working convention, v2.1)

Study note (not canon): Female and Male say nothing about gender. Positive and Negative do not mean good and bad.

### What you produce

For each passage, a list of entries. Each entry says: this Noetic is occupied by this thing, for this Mind, relative to this other thing. It points at the thing twice: a short `label` that names it, and the full `support` quote that shows it. It also gives the everyday `saying` that led you to the Noetic.

Passage P00: "Ines came home at dusk. The squeak of the gate reminded her of her first bicycle."

```json
{"passage_id": "P00",
 "none_of_these": false,
 "entries": [
   {"noetic": "^1", "label": "Ines", "support": "Ines came home at dusk.", "mind": "Ines", "relative_to": "", "certainty": "clear", "saying": ""},
   {"noetic": "^8", "label": "The squeak of the gate", "support": "The squeak of the gate reminded her of her first bicycle.", "mind": "Ines", "relative_to": "her first bicycle", "certainty": "clear", "saying": "That reminds me..."},
   {"noetic": "^9", "label": "her first bicycle", "support": "The squeak of the gate reminded her of her first bicycle.", "mind": "Ines", "relative_to": "The squeak of the gate", "certainty": "clear", "saying": "It just popped into my head."}
 ],
 "not_enough_information": []}
```

Rules for the fields:

- Every entry has seven fields: `noetic`, `label`, `support`, `mind`, `relative_to`, `certainty`, `saying`. A field that does not apply is `""`.
- `label`: the short identifying name of the thing that occupies the role. An exact, unbroken copy of passage text, 1 to 12 words. It is the passage's own naming of the thing, never cut down to a bare noun when the passage names it more fully. Rule 16 says how to choose it.
- `support`: the full supporting quote. The whole sentence or sentences that show the role: at most 4, next to each other, copied exactly, each from its first word to its end mark. The `label` lies inside the `support`.
- `mind`: the `label` of your own `^1` entry for that Mind, character for character. Use `""` only where the Noetic has no Mind in its definition (`^6`).
- `relative_to`: the `label` of the related thing, chosen by rule 16 in the same way, or `""` where the table below says so. If the passage never names the related thing, leave `relative_to` empty and add a note under `not_enough_information` (for `^8` and `^9` see rule 2).
- `certainty`: `"clear"` or `"ambiguous"`. If ambiguous, add `"other_candidate"`: another Noetic (`"^n"`), `"none"`, or `"reasoning"` (see rule 15).
- `saying`: the everyday saying that fits this moment (rule 5), in your own words or a canonical one from Part A. 2 to 20 words: a canonical saying of one word needs words around it ("It resonated with me."). Required for `^2` to `^9`; `""` for `^1`. It is not a copy of passage text. It records that you applied the test. It is never compared between teams and never scored.
- `reached_by`: used only under rule 15(b). Write `"reached_by": "reasoning"` on a conclusion the Mind worked out. Leave the field out everywhere else.
- `none_of_these`: `true` when no Noetic `^1` to `^9` is occupied by anything in the passage. Then `entries` is empty.
- `not_enough_information`: short notes for things the passage hints at but does not settle. Do not turn a hint into an entry.

Never force a label. An honest "ambiguous", "not enough information" or "none of these" is a correct answer.

### What to mark for each Noetic

| Noetic | `label` = | `mind` = | `relative_to` = |
|---|---|---|---|
| `^1` Mind | the name or mention of each Mind in the passage, listed once per Mind (see rule 4) | the same mention | `""` |
| `^2` Positive | the Idea the Mind has an affinity for and is attracted toward | that Mind | `""` |
| `^3` Negative | the Idea the Mind is adverse to and repelled by | that Mind | `""` |
| `^4` Vibration | the words that state how strongly or how weakly the Idea impresses the Mind | that Mind | the Idea that resonates |
| `^5` Female | the Idea or Ideas the Mind is composed of (what it has lived, learned, come to believe, become skilled in) | that Mind | `""` |
| `^6` Male | the Idea or Ideas that another Idea is composed of | `""` | the Idea they compose |
| `^7` Rhythm | the words that state the rate and pattern of exposure | the Mind exposed | the Idea it is exposed to |
| `^8` Above/Cause | the Idea that triggers a different Idea to come to Mind | that Mind | the Idea it brings to Mind (the `^9`) |
| `^9` Below/Effect | the Idea brought to Mind | that Mind | its trigger (the `^8`) |

`support` is the whole sentence or sentences that show the role (rule 16 says which).

`^0` Idea is not an entry. Every thing you point to is an Idea. You are not asked whether an Idea is present.

### Working rules

1. **One passage, many entries.** Mark every Noetic the passage settles. The same words may occupy two Noetics; write two entries.
2. **`^8` and `^9` come as a pair when the passage gives both.** This rule says only how to write them. Whether a Cause and an Effect are present is decided by rule 5 and by rule 15, the owner's working ruling on reading a sign. When the passage gives both, write them as a pair: each quotes the other in `relative_to`, from wherever it appears in the passage. The two rule-15 cases keep their flags: a conclusion reached by reasoning (rule 15b) is marked on its own, with `"reached_by": "reasoning"`; a sign whose way of working the passage does not reveal (rule 15d) is marked as an uncertain pair. When the passage gives only one of the two, and rule 5 marks it (for example a thought that comes out of nowhere, with no trigger named), write that one entry alone, with `relative_to` `""` and a note under `not_enough_information`.
3. **No Mind, no `^8`/`^9`.** Rain making a road wet is not `^8`. The definition needs an Idea coming to Mind.
4. **List every Mind once (owner's ruling, 2026-10-05; Part A, A2).** A Mind is an entity, a being, anything that thinks: machine, human, animal, AI, and so on. List every such being the passage names, once, by its first full naming: one `^1` entry each. A thing that does not think (a conveyor, a storm) is not a Mind. A being that is only spoken of is listed too. That last point is the operator's reading of the ruling, not the owner's words; the owner may correct it.
5. **The Colloquialism Test decides `^2` to `^9` (owner's ruling, 2026-10-05).** For every possible entry of `^2` to `^9`, apply the Diagnostic application of Part A (A9), in its order. First ask what saying a native speaker would use for this moment. Then ask which Noetic's definition (A1) that saying aligns with. Mark that Noetic, and write the saying in `saying`. The canonical sayings of Part A (A4, A5) show the kind of saying that belongs to each Noetic; A4 calls them "representative, not exhaustive". If the saying aligns with several Noetics, write an entry for each. If no saying of any Noetic fits, write no entry. If you cannot tell, write the entry with `"certainty": "ambiguous"` and the other candidate. Where a saying and a definition appear to disagree, the definition governs (A10). The annotations of Part A apply: `^3` is visceral, not analytical (A6); `^8` is triggering, not reasoning (A8). Nothing else decides presence. Rules 2, 3 and 15 say how a pair or a lone entry is written; they do not decide presence beyond rule 15, which is the owner's own working ruling.
6. *Removed by the owner's ruling of 2026-10-05; rule 5 now decides.*
7. *Removed by the owner's ruling of 2026-10-05; rule 5 now decides.*
8. **Do not label Worlds, Domains, Drives or Registers.**
9. **Do not use outside knowledge.** Label only what this passage says.
10. *Removed by the owner's ruling of 2026-10-05; rule 5 now decides.*
11. **Several Noetics on one moment are normal.** A trained response can be `^5` (what the Mind is made of), `^7` (the schedule that built it) and an `^8`/`^9` pair (a signal bringing something to Mind) at once. Mark each one the passage settles.
12. **For `^7`, `relative_to` may be an activity.** Quote the words for what the Mind was repeatedly exposed to, whether a thing or an activity.
13. *Removed by the owner's ruling of 2026-10-05; rule 5 now decides.*
14. **Ambiguous between a Noetic and nothing.** Write `"certainty": "ambiguous"` with `"other_candidate": "none"`.
15. **A sign, reasoning, or both (owner's working ruling, 2026-10-04).** When a Mind meets a sign and something else comes to it, read what the passage shows:
    - **(a) The sign directly brings a different Idea to Mind.** Mark `^8` and `^9`. Words that show this: "reminded", "brought back", "made her think of", "came to him", "was suddenly in".
    - **(b) The Mind works through evidence to a conclusion.** Mark the reasoning roles the canon names: `^6` for the named pieces it takes apart, and `^9` for the conclusion it lands on, with `relative_to` `""` and `"reached_by": "reasoning"`. Do not mark `^8`. The flag keeps these conclusions apart from a `^9` brought to Mind by a trigger: the two are counted separately and never mixed. (Clarification 6: deductive causal inference is a compound of `^6` Male and `^9` Below/Effect, not `^8` Above/Cause alone.) Words that show this: "worked out", "checked", "ruled out", "compared", "so it had to be".
    - **(c) Both happen.** Mark both, each attached to its own words.
    - **(d) The passage does not reveal which happened.** Do not mark a clear pair. Mark an uncertain pair: both entries get `"certainty": "ambiguous"` with `"other_candidate": "reasoning"`. Uncertain entries are counted separately and never count as a clear pair. A passage that says only that the Mind "knew", "recognised", "could tell" or that the sign "meant" something is this case.
    - **Speed proves nothing either way.** "At once" or "instantly" does not show that no reasoning occurred.
    - **`^5` may be marked as well.** Experience that shapes the reading is `^5`; it does not exclude a pair.
    - **A bodily reaction alone is not a `^9`.** A flinch or a startle does not show that a different Idea came to Mind. It may be `^4` if rule 5 marks it.
16. **How to point: support first, then label.** Do these steps in order.
    - **Step 1. Support.** Copy the whole sentence or sentences that show the role, each from its first word to its end mark (. ? or !). At most 4 sentences, next to each other. They must hold the naming of the thing, and the naming of the related thing when the entry has one. If 4 are not enough, keep the ones nearest the label.
    - **Step 2. Label.** The label is the naming phrase of the thing, inside the support. Start at the first word of the naming: an article, a possessive, a number or a describing word. Run through the head noun and any words that belong to the name. An activity or a scene is named by more than its noun: keep its doing words and its place words ("pallets moving down the conveyor", "a small girl on a kitchen stool"). A happening or a fact that no noun names alone is named by the stretch that says it. Never cut a name down to a bare noun: not "pallets", not "girl".
    - **Step 3. Stop.** Stop before the words that say what the thing did or what happened next: "Its lanolin scent", not "Its lanolin scent filled the small shop".
    - **Step 4. Pointers.** A pointer is: a pronoun alone (it, this, that, they, there, here, one); a phrase starting with this, that, these or those ("this smell"); "the" plus one bare word ("the noise"); or a general word for what came to Mind ("a memory", "a vivid picture") when the words after it say what it was. Everything else is a naming. If the naming inside the support is a pointer, widen the support to the first sentence that names the thing in full, and take the label from there. If no sentence does, the pointer is the label. A pointer inside a longer naming ("waited here") stays as it stands.
    - **Step 5. One thing, one label.** Use the same label for the same thing in every entry of the record. `mind` and `relative_to` copy it character for character.
    - **Step 6. Length.** At most 12 words, in one unbroken stretch. If the name is longer, stop after the 12th word.
    - **Three rows of the table differ.** For `^1` the label is the name at its first full mention (rule 4), and the support is the sentence that holds that first mention. For `^4` and `^7` the label is the how-much words or the rate-and-pattern words, as short as possible while still stating it. Steps 2 to 4 then choose their `relative_to`, the thing.

=== LABELER INSTRUCTIONS ===
LABELER INSTRUCTIONS
This text goes with the labeling guide, version 2.1 (02_LABELING_GUIDE_v2_1.md).

YOUR TASK
You get the labeling guide and a passage with an id.
Label the passage exactly as Part B of the guide says.
Work only from the guide and from the passage in front of you. Use no outside knowledge.
Label each passage on its own. One passage must not change how you label another.
Never force a label. Do not turn a hint into an entry.

WHAT YOU RETURN
One JSON record for each passage. Each record is ONE JSON object.
Nothing else goes with it: no words before it, no words after it, no code fence.
The message that gives you the passage says how to hand the record in.

{"passage_id": "<the id you were given>",
 "none_of_these": false,
 "entries": [
  {"noetic": "^8", "label": "...", "support": "...", "mind": "...", "relative_to": "...", "certainty": "clear", "saying": "..."}
 ],
 "not_enough_information": []}

- "passage_id": the id of the passage, copied exactly.
- "none_of_these": true only when nothing in the passage holds any of "^1" to "^9". Then "entries" is [].
- "entries": one entry for each thing, in one role, for one Mind.
- "not_enough_information": a list of short notes. Use [] when there are none.

EVERY ENTRY
Write these seven fields in every entry: "noetic", "label", "support", "mind", "relative_to", "certainty", "saying".
A field that does not apply is "". Never leave one of the seven out.
- "noetic" is one of "^1" to "^9". Do not label Worlds, Domains, Drives or Registers.
- "certainty" is "clear" or "ambiguous".
- "saying" is the everyday saying that fits the moment (rule 5 of the guide), in your own words or a canonical one.
  It has 2 to 20 words in every "^2" to "^9" entry. It is "" in a "^1" entry, and only there.
- Add "other_candidate" only with "certainty": "ambiguous". Its value is another Noetic ("^n"), "none" or "reasoning".
- Add "reached_by": "reasoning" only on a "^9" under rule 15(b). That entry has "relative_to": "".

EXACT COPIES
"label", "support", "mind" and "relative_to" are exact copies of passage text: the same letters, the same capitals, the same punctuation, one unbroken stretch. "saying" is your own line, not a copy.
- Do not correct the passage. Do not reword it. Do not join two stretches. Do not cut words out of the middle.
- "support" is whole sentences, each from its first word to its end mark. At most 4, next to each other.
- "label" lies inside "support". It has 1 to 12 words.
- In a "^1" entry, "support" is the sentence that holds the first place the name stands in the passage.
- "mind" is the "label" of your own "^1" entry for that Mind, character for character. It is "" in a "^6" entry, and only there.
- "relative_to" is the "label" of the related thing, or "" where the table of the guide says so.
- If the passage never names the related thing, leave "relative_to" as "" and add a note under "not_enough_information". For "^8" and "^9" see rule 2 of the guide.

THE CHECKER
A fixed program checks the form of your record before it is accepted.
It reads form only. A record that passes is not thereby correct.
If a check fails, you get the checker's messages. Then do this:
1. Read every message. Fix what the messages name. Use the guide to decide the fix.
2. Hand in the whole record again, in the same format, with every entry in it.
You have up to three tries for one passage.
Never remove an entry to make a message go away. Fix its form and keep it.
The one exception: a message that itself says to keep only one of two, or to take the entries out.
