You are an independent reviewer of a study. You did not write it. You are not one of the models that will label. Review the three documents below (a labeling guide, the study plan, and the one-page sign-off sheet for the framework's owner) for one purpose: find defects that would make the label-agreement result wrong, unfair or unreadable, or that would have the owner sign something untrue. Report BLOCKER and MAJOR findings only. No style notes.

Background. Two independent teams of language models will label 440 short passages written by a third model. For each passage a team writes entries: which stretch of text occupies which of nine roles (written ^1 to ^9), for which Mind, relative to what. Each entry has a short label plus a full supporting quote, and the everyday saying that fits. A program pairs the two teams' entries one to one and counts, per role, entries both teams wrote, entries only team A wrote and entries only team B wrote. A first attempt on 428 passages, under an older guide, failed its pass mark for eight of nine roles.

History you should know. An earlier version of this guide (2.0) was rejected by an outside reviewer because its builders added their own rules on WHEN a role is present, some of them against the framework's own table of sayings. The framework's owner then ruled, in these words: "MIND IS AN ENTITY, A BEING, ANYTHING THAT THINKS, MACHINE,HUMAN, ANIMAL, AI ETC.  POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE USE THE COLLOQUILISMS (SHOULD BE  IN LOCAL FILES) THE SAME ABOUT THE OTHER NOETICS". This guide (2.1) is the answer to that ruling: Part A is copied by a script from the owner's own file (the definitions, the owner's sentence on Mind, the Colloquialism Test with its table of sayings, its annotations and its guardrail); the study's own presence rules were removed; one rule (rule 5) sends the labeler to the test; rule 15 is an earlier working ruling of the owner on reading a sign and is kept as written.

Fixed ground you may not question. Part A is canon and belongs to the owner. Rule 15 is the owner's own working ruling. The owner's sentence is quoted as typed.

Check in particular:
1. Does any sentence of Part B that the study wrote still decide WHEN a role is present, other than by sending the labeler to the Colloquialism Test and to the owner's sentence on Mind? Quote it. Does any Part B rule or worked example contradict Part A? Quote both sides.
2. Is rule 5 a faithful, usable statement of the Diagnostic application in Part A? Could two careful labelers follow rule 5 and rule 4 and still part widely? Name the two or three places where that is most likely. Do not propose new presence rules or word lists; say only what the owner should expect.
3. The pointing rule (rule 16) and the matching test ("the two labels overlap in the passage and the words of the shorter one all lie inside the longer one"). Can the pass mark be met for the wrong reason, or missed for the wrong reason? Is a lone Cause or Effect entry (no partner named) handled soundly, given that an Effect reached by reasoning is counted apart only when the labeler sets a flag?
4. The sign-off sheet. Does it say truly and completely what the owner's one pasted line puts in force? Is anything on it worded so that the owner would sign something other than what the guide and the plan do?
5. The plan. Are the measure, the pass marks, the minimum counts, the outcome words and the validity rules fixed before any label exists? Can the practice step (30 passages) be used to tune anything? Is it stated plainly that three things now differ from the first attempt (the pointing rule, the entry-by-entry count, and presence decided by the owner's ruling), so that a change in agreement cannot be credited to one of them alone?
6. Who sees what: can the two label teams, the passage writer or the operator share what they should not? Team A is a set of sub-agents of the same model family as the operator.
7. What a pass and a failure would and would not show. Is anything claimed that the design cannot support?

For each finding give: severity, the exact place, why it matters, and the smallest fix that does not add a presence rule.
Start your reply with one line: VERDICT: ACCEPT, ACCEPT_WITH_NOTES, or REJECT. Keep the reply under 900 words.
