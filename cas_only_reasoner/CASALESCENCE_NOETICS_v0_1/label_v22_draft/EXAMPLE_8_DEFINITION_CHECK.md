# Example 8 (Brenda, W137): each definition checked, and what the guide allows

Written by Claude on 2026-10-07 at the owner's request. It is a check, not a ruling. Example 8 stays open until the owner rules. No recommendation is made here.

The passage, word for word:

> Brenda saw the small queue forming at the 'Ring Toss' game. Its shortness, combined with the way families drifted quickly past, told her exactly what was happening. She knew the prizes were too cheap to entice serious players, and the game attendant looked bored. It meant a slow day for that stall.

## 1. Each definition, word for word

**`^8` Above/Cause: "An Idea that triggers a different Idea than itself to come to Mind."**

- An Idea: the queue's shortness, and the way families drifted quickly past.
- Triggers a different Idea: "told her exactly what was happening"; "It meant a slow day for that stall." The slow day is a different Idea from the queue.
- To come to Mind: "told her". It came to Brenda.
- Result: **met.** The passage also uses "told her", "knew" and "meant", which your answer on signs (example 7) counts as a clear Cause and Effect.

**`^9` Below/Effect: "The Idea brought to Mind by an Above/Cause trigger."**

- "a slow day for that stall" is the Idea brought to Brenda's Mind by the queue and the families.
- Result: **met.**

**`^6` Male: "An Idea impregnated with another Idea, the Ideas an Idea is composed of."**

- Male needs a named Idea that the queue and the families are parts of. The passage offers one candidate: "what was happening", which the last sentence names "a slow day for that stall".
- But the passage says the clues "told her" and "meant" the slow day. It never says the slow day is made of them. "Combined with" joins the two clues to each other; it does not name a whole they make up.
- Result: **not shown in the words.** Male needs a reading the passage does not state. Under the rule you approved in example 5 (the whole the passage says the parts make up), there is no Male entry here. Team B wrote Male entries by taking "what was happening" as the whole.

**Reasoning (guide rule 15(b)).** The rule's words are "worked out", "checked", "ruled out", "compared" and "so it had to be". None of them is in the passage. The only working-through word is "combined with".

## 2. Does the guide allow both relationships at once?

- **Same words in two roles: yes, in general.** Rule 1: "The same words may occupy two Noetics; write two entries." Rule 11: "Several Noetics on one moment are normal."
- **Cause and reasoning at once: no, as written.** Rule 15(b) says that when the Mind works through evidence, "Do not mark `^8`", and the conclusion is a `^9` with no trigger and a reasoning flag. Rule 15(c) allows both only "each attached to its own words".
- **The conclusion gets one `^9` entry.** It is either the Effect of a trigger or a conclusion reached by reasoning, not both. The flag "keeps these conclusions apart".

So the guide today forces the either/or for the same clues. Allowing both would change rule 15(b) and 15(c).

## 3. What each ruling would write for this passage

| If you rule | Entries for Brenda |

Where a passage also names the whole the clues make up, `^6` entries too. A reasoning flag could mark that reasoning was also shown, without removing the `^8`. |
| **Not both**: keep rule 15(b) as it is | With no rule-15(b) word in this passage, your answer on signs (example 7) already makes it a clear `^8` and `^9` pair. Rule 15(b) applies only when the passage shows working-through. |

Either way, this passage has no Male entry unless its whole is named, and the slow day is a `^9`.

For Brenda's passage, both rulings give the same clear pair. The ruling matters for passages that show working-through, such as the guide's own example X6: "He added the first two, compared the sum with the quote, and worked out that the pump was the whole difference." There, "not both" gives `^6` pieces and a flagged `^9` with no Cause, and "both allowed" adds the evidence as a `^8`.
