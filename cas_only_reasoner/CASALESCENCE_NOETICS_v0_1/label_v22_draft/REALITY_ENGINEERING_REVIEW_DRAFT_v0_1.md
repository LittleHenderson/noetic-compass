## 1. What the book says, next to the guide

**The ten definitions are the same words.** The book's canonical list on page 446 matches the guide's Part A, line for line. For example: "^8: Above/Cause An Idea that triggers a different Idea than itself to come to Mind" and "^9: Below/Effect The Idea brought to Mind by an Above/Cause trigger". An earlier table (pages 69 to 70) adds a few words to four definitions, such as "(information and energy)" for `^1` and "the vibratory signature unique to an Idea" for `^4`. In my reading, those words do not change the meaning.

**Where the book's worked examples agree with the guide.** The dog-training table (pages 73 to 74) points at the same kind of thing the guide's table asks for:

| Role | Book's dog example | Guide's label |
|---|---|---|
| `^2` Positive | "The doggy treat used to lure and reward--something the dog likes" | the thing the Mind is drawn to |
| `^3` Negative | the newspaper, "something the dog dislikes" | the thing the Mind is repelled by |
| `^4` Vibration | "how deeply "sit" is registering, how quickly he responds" | the how-much words |
| `^5` Female | "the Ideas that COMPOSE the dog's Mind from genetics and prior conditioning" | what the Mind is made of |
| `^7` Rhythm | "how often and how long the dog encounters the command" | the rate-and-pattern words |

**Where the book goes further than the guide.** Five points need your call. Each one has an example in section 3.

1. **Causes with no thinking Mind.** The book's Physical-world examples include "Gravity (D8) triggers the falling (D9) of a dropped apple" (page 48), sweating triggered by heat, and a plant turning to light (page 51). Guide rule 3 says no Mind, no `^8` or `^9`. See example 11.
2. **A conclusion reached by reasoning.** The book lists "The logical conclusion (B9) derived from premises (B8)" (page 51). Guide rule 15(b) marks the conclusion `^9` but no `^8`, following Clarification 6. This is your open question 2. See example 8.
3. **Recognizing a sign.** The book lists "The thought `I need milk' (B9) triggered by noticing the empty carton (D8)" (page 51). Guide rule 15(d) makes "knew", "recognised" and "meant" an uncertain pair. This is your open question 1. See example 7.
4. **One Cause, two Effects.** In the dog table, the command "Sit" triggers "the action of sitting" in the Cause row, but the Effect row names "The image of the doggy treat popping into the dog's mind". See examples 9 and 10.
5. **Male names the container.** The book's example is "the trainer (Idea) contains within it the instruction to sit (another Idea)". The guide labels the parts and names the whole as the related thing. The two teams split on which whole to name. See example 5.

## 3. Draft worked examples

Each example shows the passage, what each team wrote, what the book says, and a proposed answer. Mark each one "keep" or "change".

### Example 1. Positive: name each thing the Mind is drawn to (W029)

> Jenny planned her route through the fairground. First, she would visit the dodgems for a bit of bumpy fun. After that, the Ferris wheel offered good views, and finally, she would try her luck at the ring toss game. Each activity promised a different kind of enjoyment. She mentally mapped out her exciting afternoon.

- Team A: one `^2`, "her exciting afternoon".
- Team B: three `^2` entries: "the dodgems", "the Ferris wheel", "the ring toss game".
- Book: the `^2` is the treat itself, "something the dog likes".
- **Proposed:** team B. Each activity is a separate thing Jenny is drawn to; the passage says each "promised a different kind of enjoyment".

### Example 2. Negative: walking past is not dislike (W003)

> Julian walked straight past the display of small, darting fish, hardly glancing at them. Their frenetic energy offered him nothing. He paused for a long time before the large tank of sleeping sharks, captivated by their silent power. He found their motionless grace deeply satisfying. The distant chatter of a school group barely registered in his hearing.

- Team A: no `^3`. A weak `^4`, "offered him nothing", for the fish.
- Team B: `^3` "the display of small, darting fish", with the saying "Wouldn't touch it with a ten-foot pole."
- Book: `^3` is "adverse to and repelled by", "something the dog dislikes". Clarification 6 adds that `^3` is visceral.
- **Proposed:** team A. Julian is not repelled; the fish make no impression. That is a weak `^4`, not a `^3`.

### Example 3. Vibration: the label says how hard it hit the Mind (W005)

> Noah had spent thirty years watching ropes being spun. The sound of a snapping fibre, faint and sharp, made him instantly flinch. He remembered a machine malfunction from years ago. Younger workers barely noticed the sound, but for Noah, it was a familiar warning. He walked over to check the tension.

- Team A: `^4` "made him instantly flinch" (Noah), and `^4` "barely noticed" (the younger workers). Both relate to "The sound of a snapping fibre".
- Team B: `^4` "faint and sharp", for Noah.
- Book: "how deeply "sit" is registering, how quickly he responds". The degree is in the Mind, not in the thing.
- **Proposed:** team A. "Faint and sharp" describes the sound, not its impression on Noah.

### Example 4. Female or Rhythm: what Gary is made of, and how often (W002)

> Gary, a lifeboat volunteer, had served for years. When the station alarm blared, his body moved before his brain registered the sound. He was already reaching for his waterproof jacket. His feet carried him automatically towards the main doors. He listened intently for the emergency details, a familiar routine imprinted on him.

- Team A: `^5` "a familiar routine"; `^7` "for years".
- Team B: `^5` "a lifeboat volunteer"; `^7` "had served for years"; and a second `^7`, "a familiar routine imprinted on him".
- Book: `^5` is "I am the sum of my experiences" (page 74); `^7` is the rate and pattern of exposure, "how often and how long".
- **Proposed:** two `^5` entries, "a lifeboat volunteer" and "a familiar routine imprinted on him", because both are part of who Gary is. One `^7`, "had served for years". The routine is the result of the repetition, so it is `^5`, not `^7`.

### Example 5. Male: which whole the parts belong to (W020 and W023)

> W020: The faulty tanning machine puzzled Robert. He broke down the problem into its parts: the power supply, the main motor, the conveyor belt mechanism, and the spray nozzles. Then he considered the sensors, the temperature regulators, and the chemical lines. Each component needed individual checking to find the blockage.

> W023: Beatrice looked at the faulty arcade game. She saw a loose wire connecting the joystick, a jammed coin mechanism, and a dusty screen. The main circuit board appeared scorched near a certain chip. Each problem contributed to the machine's unresponsiveness. Fixing it required addressing these individual components.

- Both teams named the same parts in both passages. They split only on the whole.
- W020: team A "The faulty tanning machine"; team B "the problem". Seven pairs were lost on this alone.
- W023: team A "the machine's unresponsiveness"; team B "the faulty arcade game".
- Book: `^6` is "the Ideas an Idea is composed of"; "the trainer (Idea) contains within it the instruction to sit (another Idea)".
- **Proposed:** team A in both. In W020, "the problem" is a pointer back to the faulty machine, and the guide says to name the thing in full. In W023, the passage says the faults "contributed to the machine's unresponsiveness", so that is the whole they make up.

### Example 6. Male: noticing several things is not breaking one down (W001)

> Evelyn checked the water temperature in tank seven. It was slightly lower than optimal. She observed the fry; they clustered more tightly than usual, sluggish in their movements. The aeration pump in the corner seemed quieter than its usual hum. She noted a small, slow drip from a pipe connection nearby. The pump was losing pressure.

- Team A: no `^6`.
- Team B: four `^6` entries, such as "the fry" as a part of "the fry".
- Book: `^6` needs one Idea made of other Ideas.
- **Proposed:** team A. The passage lists what Evelyn noticed, not the parts of one named whole. A thing is never its own part.

### Example 7. Recognizing a sign: your open question 1 (W022)

> Yolanda walked past the rows of potted saplings. She noticed a subtle yellowing on the underside of a single leaf on an oak sapling. It was barely visible to the casual eye. Yolanda, who had nurtured plants for over forty years, immediately knew it signaled a nutrient deficiency that could quickly spread if left unaddressed. She reached for a specific fertilizer.

- Both teams wrote the same pair, `^8` "a subtle yellowing on the underside of a single leaf" and `^9` "a nutrient deficiency…". Both marked it uncertain, because the guide says "knew" leaves it open. Uncertain pairs do not count toward the 0.80 mark.
- Book: "The thought `I need milk' (B9) triggered by noticing the empty carton (D8)". Noticing a sign brings the thought.
- Your question 1: "Does recognizing a familiar sign count as Cause → Effect when it brings another idea to mind?"
- **Proposed:** yes, as in the book's empty-carton example. Then this becomes a clear pair, which both teams already agree on.

### Example 8. A conclusion reached by reasoning: your open question 2 (W137)

> Brenda saw the small queue forming at the 'Ring Toss' game. Its shortness, combined with the way families drifted quickly past, told her exactly what was happening. She knew the prizes were too cheap to entice serious players, and the game attendant looked bored. It meant a slow day for that stall.

- Team A: an uncertain pair, `^8` "the small queue forming at the 'Ring Toss' game" and `^9` "a slow day for that stall".
- Team B: `^6` pieces ("Its shortness", "the way families drifted quickly past") and `^9` "a slow day for that stall", marked as reached by reasoning.
- Book: "The logical conclusion (B9) derived from premises (B8)". The premises are the Cause.
- Clarification 6 (guide Part A, A8): reasoning is "a compound of `^6` Male and `^9` Below/Effect, not `^8` Above/Cause alone".
- Both sources agree the conclusion is a `^9`. They differ on the premises: a Cause in the book, parts (`^6`) in Clarification 6.
- **Your choice:** (a) the book: a clear `^8` → `^9` pair; or (b) Clarification 6: `^6` pieces plus a `^9`, as guide rule 15(b) does now.

### Example 9. Which Effect, when one memory leads to more (W059)

> Tina reached for a ripe apple on a high branch. The cool morning air and the sweet scent of apples reminded her of a day long ago. She pictured her childhood bedroom window, open to a similar breeze. A sense of youthful freedom came to her then. It was a memory she had not considered in years.

- Both teams: `^8` "The cool morning air and the sweet scent of apples".
- Team A: its `^9` is "her childhood bedroom window, open to a similar breeze".
- Team B: its `^9` is "a day long ago". It also wrote the window and "A sense of youthful freedom" as `^9` entries with no trigger.
- Book: the `^9` is "the specific thought, memory, conclusion, or simpler idea generated by a mental trigger" (page 50). An Effect can also become the Cause of the next one (page 50).
- **Proposed:** pair the trigger with what the passage says it "reminded her of", "a day long ago". The window and the sense of freedom are also `^9` entries of the same trigger, because the passage calls them one memory.

### Example 10. A memory placed right after a sign, with no linking word (W005)

> The sound of a snapping fibre, faint and sharp, made him instantly flinch. He remembered a machine malfunction from years ago.

- Team A: a lone uncertain `^9`, "a machine malfunction from years ago", with a note that no trigger is named.
- Team B: a clear pair, `^8` "The sound of a snapping fibre" and `^9` "a machine malfunction from years ago".
- Book: "The specific memory (B9) of a past event triggered by a related smell (D8)". The book's examples always name the trigger.
- **Proposed:** a clear pair only when the passage links the two ("reminded", "brought back", "made him think of"). When the memory simply follows the sign, write an uncertain pair.

### Example 11. A cause with no thinking Mind (no passage needed)

- Book: "Gravity (D8) triggers the falling (D9) of a dropped apple"; "A plant growing towards sunlight (D9) triggered by the light source (D8)".
- Guide rule 3: "No Mind, no `^8`/`^9`. Rain making a road wet is not `^8`."
- The canonical definitions say the Effect comes "to Mind". In my reading, the book's physical examples describe the Physical-world Elements (D8, D9), not a Noetic label inside a passage.
- **Proposed:** keep rule 3. Label `^8` and `^9` only when an Idea comes to a being that thinks.
