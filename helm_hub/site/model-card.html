Model card

The standard facts sheet for a model, adapted to what HELM is: a small brain of written rules, with checks around it.

What may be said, word for word

Each sentence below is LICENSED (a claim-status word: this exact sentence may be said, nothing wider (NOT LICENSED: it may not be said)): it may be said exactly as written, and nothing wider. Widening the sentence voids it.

The licensed brain (SYNTHETIC_ONLY (a claim ceiling: the result holds on made-up test worlds only))

On synthetic worlds only: placed in GB3's slot, at GB3's interface and marks, a CAS recipe brain (programmed carry and composition, learned requirements, the RPM Gate, the Lacunarity cadence, the restart fallback and a form-only language skin, each on its own switch) held GB3's continuity marks on fresh worlds and named the Limiting Reagent of fresh held-out compositions at least 4 of 32 above GB3, both as built (97 parameters) and widened (385 parameters), with every write receipted; each part is credited only where switching it off lost.

Not licensed (record): (from §7.47): recipes beating transformers in general; learned carry; learned composition; statistical superiority; operation inside HELM's runtime; anything on real data; growth, the eye, the atlas, heads or memory fading. As of this commit the licensed brain is not inside HELM's live loop: helm_end_to_end_loop.py uses the 22x40 stand-in (§7.49). Putting it there is the owner's fix C.

The wide brain candidate (VALIDATED (a claim-status word: it passed its own pre-set test against controls, on the data it was measured on) for this sentence only, NAMED_CORPORA_ONLY (a claim ceiling: the result holds on the named public text collections only))

On 128 real recipes drawn blind from the named corpora, taught by one witnessed run-out batch per ingredient, the wide brain learned every amount exactly on at least 90% of the representable recipes, gave 0 wrong answers, asked only where the source did not settle the answer or a lesson was not learned, and with its switch OFF left HELM byte-identical.

Not licensed (record): that the wide brain is HELM's canonical brain (candidate only); real kitchens or cooks; typed English (every turn is a fixed template around source text); anything about CTT or any transformer; coverage beyond these counts; "better than the licensed brain" outside these items; that the wide brain asks whenever the source does not settle the answer; the 28 drive cells or 22 pathways; statistical claims (one run, one draw).

The transformer ear (a narrow negative)

on HELM's kitchen world, on 128 real sentences other people wrote (X-REAL's frame and frozen key), the from-scratch translator behind HELM's reader, round-trip check and read-back gave no gain over the reader alone, added at most 2 confident wrong proposals, saved nothing wrong, and changed none of the brain's exam results.

Intended use

  • A research instrument: showing that a brain made of recipes can be checked line by line, refuse when unsure, and beat a small transformer on its own exams.
  • A kitchen test world: stock on hand, recipes, how much can be made, and what runs out first.
  • Local demonstration through the HELM UI on one machine (How to run HELM).

Out of scope

  • Real kitchens, real cooks, food safety, nutrition, allergies.
  • Medical, legal, financial or safety-critical advice of any kind.
  • General conversation, general knowledge, open-ended English (the reader is kitchen-only; see the chat question).
  • Controlling machines or running tools. In the UI, tool actions are switched off by default.

Claim ceilings

A claim ceiling (the widest world a claim covers; nothing outside it is claimed) is the widest world a claim covers. Today there are two. Synthetic worlds (a made-up test world built by a program, with known right answers) only, for most results; the named public text collections only, for the real-text tests (listed on Data lineage). No HELM claim covers real users.

What this hub does not have, and why

The usual documents for a large AI language model include sections that do not apply to HELM. They are left out on purpose:

  • Fine-tuning and preference training (SFT, RLHF, DPO: ways of training a big model on example answers or on people's preferences). HELM's brain learns amounts from witnessed batches by exact arithmetic, with no gradients; there is nothing of this kind to report.
  • Standard benchmark tables (such as MMLU or GSM8K, general-knowledge and math-word-problem tests for big models). HELM does not answer general questions. Its scores come from sealed exams built for each part; they are all on the Results scorecard.
  • Compression and serving (quantization, vLLM: ways to run a huge model cheaply). The brain is tiny and the transformer ear has 9,585,566 parameters; nothing is served to the public.
  • Training-log dashboards. The brain has no training curve. The ear's training runs are summarised in their run records (Compute and cost).
  • A data flywheel (collecting users' conversations to train on). HELM collects none; session logs stay on the local machine.
  • A public deployment or API. There is a local page and local endpoints only (How to run HELM). Public hosting is an owner and IP-attorney decision; much of this work is new matter not covered by the provisional patent filing.
