How HELM thinks

Six ideas explain almost everything HELM does. Each has a plain explanation, then a box with the exact wording from the project's dictionary.
The six ideas, one line each
  • A recipe (a written rule saying how much of each ingredient one unit of an outcome needs) is what HELM's recipe brain (HELM's brain made of written recipes and exact arithmetic instead of a neural network) is made of.
  • The limiting reagent (the ingredient that runs out first and so caps how much can be made) decides how much can be made.
  • When something is missing, the RPM Gate (HELM's one gate: it lets a step go ahead, sends it to repair, or refuses, and lets the brain say it cannot tell) lets HELM say it cannot tell, instead of guessing.
  • Every saved fact leaves a receipt (the record a saved fact leaves behind, with a fingerprint, so it can be checked later).
  • Every sentence HELM says must survive the round trip (turning a checked statement into English and back; the result must equal the start).
  • The Unity Eye (a small watcher that looks over HELM, reports and whispers, and never writes) watches, reports and whispers (a typed nudge from the Eye that HELM may ignore; it never changes a stored fact), and never writes.

Each "Precise definition" box quotes the project's dictionary word for word; it may use further project terms, which the glossary explains.

1. Recipes

HELM's brain is built from the vocabulary of CAS (the Cognitive Architecture System, the project's framework for describing how any mind works). Its core unit is the recipe. A recipe for bread might say: one loaf needs 2 flour and 1 water. CAS writes this as Θ(Ω) = a₁·X₁ + a₂·X₂ + ... → Ω, where Ω (omega) is the outcome, each X is an ingredient, and each a is how much of it one unit needs.

HELM learns those amounts by watching. Tell it "we made 5 loaves from 10 flour and 8 water and ran out of flour", and it learns that one loaf needs 10 ÷ 5 = 2 flour, because flour is the one that ran out. The record writes this rule as a := stock / amount made (§7.83). Nothing is guessed; the arithmetic is exact fractions.

Definition: "The proportional weighting of prerequisite primitives required for a unit of production target." Θ(Ω) = a₁·X₁ + a₂·X₂ + ... + aₙ·Xₙ — CFC Clarification 9 §2. "Recipe specification is ALWAYS relative to a specific target Ω."
In HELM: the recipe brain answers by recipe yield — SPEC §7.24; HELM's licensed brain has "8 stock cells and 4 recipes with a FIXED programmed support" — helm_brain_canonical.py.

2. The limiting ingredient

To answer "how much bread can we make?", HELM divides what is on hand by what one unit needs, for every ingredient, and takes the smallest result: yield = min x_i / a_i. The ingredient with the smallest result is the limiting reagent. With 12 flour and 20 water: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 and 20 ÷ 1 = 20, so 6 loaves, and flour is the limiting ingredient.

Precise definition: Limiting Reagent (dictionary entry, quoted)
Definition: "The prerequisite Xᵢ producing the minimum xᵢ/aᵢ ratio in the yield calculation. The bottleneck constraining outcome production." — CFC Clarification 9 §2.
In HELM: voice gloss "the one that runs out first" — helm_voice.py; HELM-R1 wave 1's claim: it "named the Limiting Reagent of fresh held-out compositions at least 4 of 32 above GB3" — SPEC §7.47.

3. Asking instead of guessing

If an amount HELM needs is missing, it does not fill in a number. Its RPM Gate lets the brain answer UNDETERMINED (HELM's honest 'I cannot tell', used when something it needs is missing), and HELM asks a plain question. When it cannot read what you said, it answers with CLARIFY (HELM asking a question instead of guessing, with nothing saved): one question, nothing saved. An earlier version of HELM's pipeline filled missing amounts with an invented 10; that was found and removed (§7.49).

4. Receipts: read back before writing

Before HELM saves a fact, it reads the fact back and checks it. Every saved fact leaves a receipt with a hash (a fingerprint of a file; change one byte and the fingerprint changes), so anyone can check later that nothing changed. A write that fails its read-back is refused, and nothing is saved.

Precise definition: Receipt (dictionary entry, quoted)
Definition: "the record a write leaves behind, with a fingerprint (sha256 digest)" — U3A §1. η∘ε read-back receipt as a hard gate: "every admitted write passed, RB-det 472 of 472 (36 refused by receipt)" — SPEC §7.24.

5. The round trip: saying it the same way both directions

Inside, HELM works in exact CAS statements. Outside, it talks in English. The bridge is CCE (Controlled Core English, a strict small form of English that converts to CAS and back exactly). Any statement HELM says must pass the round trip: turn it into English, read the English back, and get exactly the same statement (parse(render(C)) == C). A round trip proves the form is right, not that the content is true: an old English model gave 21 confident answers that passed the round trip and were all wrong (§7.80).

Precise definition: Round trip (dictionary entry, quoted)
Definition: "roundtrip.py -- the only acceptance test." — language/roundtrip.py. "ε∘η = id — parse(render(C)) == C, required for every sentence said. η∘ε ≠ id — render(parse(E)) == E is NOT required." — CSK §4 (two bullet lines joined).

6. The Eye

The Unity Eye is a small watcher whose only motive is unity, the first of the seven core motivations. It scans HELM's memory and records for damage, and it helps choose which mental job to try on unfinished work. It may report and whisper. It never writes: in the scored runs it made 0 writes (§7.77, §7.78).

The CAS words underneath

CAS describes any mind, human or machine, with a small fixed vocabulary. The ten Noetics (one of ten basic moves a mind makes with Ideas, written ^0 to ^9) are the basic moves. Their names and definitions are canonical and are quoted below word for word. HELM also uses the seven Drives (one of seven core motivations, F1 Unity to F7 Purpose) (F1 Unity, F2 Wisdom, F3 Vitality, F4 Connection, F5 Agency, F6 Structure, F7 Purpose), the four Domains (one of four levels a thought works at: Principled, Cognitive, Evaluative, Operational) and, in some tests, the four Registers (the tone of an operation: Transcendent, Analytical, Affective or Embodied).

^1Mind“Various degrees of awareness that processes, stores, outputs, and intakes Ideas.”awareness — the thing that takes in, holds, works on and gives out Ideas.
^2Positive“An Idea the Mind has an affinity for and is attracted toward.”an Idea the mind is drawn toward ("speaks to me").
