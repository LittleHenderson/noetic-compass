HELM, in one screen

HELM is a small thinking machine you can check line by line. Its brain is a set of written recipes (a written rule saying how much of each ingredient one unit of an outcome needs), worked with exact arithmetic. When something is missing, it asks instead of guessing.

HELM is built on CAS (the Cognitive Architecture System, the project's framework for describing how any mind works). Its brain is a recipe brain (HELM's brain made of written recipes and exact arithmetic instead of a neural network). For each outcome, such as a loaf of bread, it keeps how much of each ingredient one unit needs. It learns those amounts from things it has watched happen ("we made 5 loaves from 10 flour and 8 water and ran out of flour"). It answers by dividing what is on hand by what each unit needs. The smallest result wins, because the limiting reagent (the ingredient that runs out first and so caps how much can be made) runs out first.

When an amount it needs is missing, its RPM Gate (HELM's one gate: it lets a step go ahead, sends it to repair, or refuses, and lets the brain say it cannot tell) lets it answer UNDETERMINED (HELM's honest 'I cannot tell', used when something it needs is missing) instead of guessing. Every fact it saves is read back first and leaves a receipt (the record a saved fact leaves behind, with a fingerprint, so it can be checked later). A watcher, the Unity Eye (a small watcher that looks over HELM, reports and whispers, and never writes), looks over the whole system; it reports and whispers (a typed nudge from the Eye that HELM may ignore; it never changes a stored fact), and it never writes.

HELM is not a chatbot built on a large language model. No LLM (large language model, a big pretrained text-predicting neural network like those behind chat assistants) sits inside it. The first recipe brain had 68 learned values (parameters (an adjustable number a model learns)): 32 requirements, 32 ceilings and 4 bindings (§7.24). The licensed brain's run log printed 32 learned slots, against 97 for the transformer (the kind of neural network behind most modern AI chat assistants) it replaced (§7.47). A separate small transformer (9,585,566 parameters) exists only as an optional "ear" that may propose a reading; it never writes (§7.82). A second brain, the wide brain (the recipe brain with its ingredient list opened up (any ingredient name, up to 10 per recipe, exact fractions, 20 units), behind a switch; HELM's canonical-brain candidate, not yet canonical), reads any ingredient name; after two sealed (frozen and fingerprinted before the run, so nothing can be changed after the fact) tests and the pair wave (wave 6: the wide brain run together with HELM's other parts to read how each pair behaves) it is HELM's canonical-brain candidate across the switches (§7.96), and on the owner's word of 2026-10-20 it is now HELM's canonical brain (§7.56). Since 2026-10-20 the live loop and the chat run it by default; setting HELM_WIDE_BRAIN=0 runs the licensed reference brain alone.

Each card shows a color, the exact words from the record, and the claim ceiling (the widest world a claim covers; nothing outside it is claimed). Tests run in numbered waves (one sealed test round in the HELM-R1 series, with each part on its own switch); the latest, the pair wave (wave 6: the wide brain run together with HELM's other parts to read how each pair behaves), read WIDE_WAVE_NO_GAIN, so the wide brain stays a candidate and the licensed brain stays HELM's main brain. Most results come from sealed (frozen and fingerprinted before the run, so nothing can be changed after the fact) exams. Words ending in EARNED_KEEP (a status word meaning that switching the part off made results worse, so it stays) are the good outcome for a part; a faculty (a mental job HELM can run on an open item, such as asking or verifying) is one of HELM's mental jobs. The colors are the owner's scale and are a reading aid only. The words in grey boxes are the record's own words. SYNTHETIC_ONLY (a claim ceiling: the result holds on made-up test worlds only) and NAMED_CORPORA_ONLY (a claim ceiling: the result holds on the named public text collections only) are the two ceilings in use today. LICENSED (a claim-status word: this exact sentence may be said, nothing wider (NOT LICENSED: it may not be said)), VALIDATED (a claim-status word: it passed its own pre-set test against controls, on the data it was measured on) and ENGINEERED-NOT-VALIDATED (a claim-status word: built and working, with no validated result yet) are claim status (what may be said about a result, using only the words of the spec sheet's legend) words; the others are status words (the frozen reader's one-word verdict for a part, such as EARNED_KEEP).
