# PARKED FUTURE GOAL — CAS Coalescence / CASalescence
## Separate HELMs cooperating, sharing permitted experience, and separating with identity intact

**Origin:** Little Henderson's current request for multiple separate HELMs to meld brain power, capabilities and histories, using RPA to separate back into their individual identities. **Status:** LATER — concept and acceptance questions only. No agents, implementation, experiments, paid calls, source changes or migration of existing models are assigned to this goal now.

The spelling CASalescence is retained as the owner's working name alongside CAS Coalescence. This package does not choose a final product name, change CAS canon or claim that reversible neural merging exists.

## Preserve the goal without confusing different mechanisms

**Cooperation:** separate HELMs exchange permitted requests/results or use a joint routing/aggregation process. Their model states need not merge. This is the first candidate stage to investigate later, not a current implemented feature.

**Capability sharing:** a composition can call different members or transfer a qualified skill through a separately measured learning/migration process. More participants do not automatically imply greater intelligence; communication, routing and disagreement can add cost or reduce accuracy.

**History sharing:** preserve each member's ordered local history and add explicit references to permitted shared events. A union of history records is not proof that weights absorbed those experiences. Conflicting memories remain attributed; permission to share one event does not grant access to every member's memory or protected test history.

**Trainable-structure/weight merging:** a later, distinct research question requiring architectural compatibility, alignment/migration and retention experiments. It must not be claimed from ordinary tool routing or concatenating histories.

These distinctions are proposed engineering interpretations of the owner's goal, not performance claims supplied by the current CAS or HELM sources.

## What “break apart back to their former selves” must mean

Mode A — exact restoration: preserve immutable pre-join checkpoints and compatible harness, optimizer, RNG/cursor, memory and source versions. Splitting can restore those preserved versions. This is restoration from retained state, not proof that a merged network was mathematically inverted.

Mode B — separate with new experience: retain original parent identities and create distinct descendant versions with explicitly attributed, qualified shared-experience deltas. Each resulting member is an evolved descendant, not byte-identical to its earlier self.

Mode C — undo an actual neural merge without original snapshots: a research question, not a promised property. A many-to-one parameter transformation can discard the information needed to recover its inputs uniquely. RPA coordinates alone do not reconstruct lost parameters or optimizer/memory state.

Always state which mode was used. Do not erase shared-event history to pretend the collaboration never happened. Financial costs and external actions do not disappear when a model snapshot is restored.

## RPA's intended role

Use source-bound identities and explicit causal links to record members, versions, join session, contribution, parent continuation, shared event and split descendants. Display/layout/profile coordinates remain separate from authoritative identity. The existing controller and approved lifecycle perform joins/restorations; the renderer does not gain those rights.

A join should identify the exact member checkpoints and admitted shared surfaces, not merely model nicknames. A split should verify expected members and retained versions, not choose the nearest flower. Preserve separate native state/return references just as RPA-RETURN-1 does for task callers. This is a useful later design connection, not evidence that current RPA supports multi-model reversal.

## Later activation conditions

Do not hold current work for this feature. First obtain reliable individual bundles, checkpoint/reload and rollback, exact version identity, qualified memory/skill interfaces, useful single-model measurements and a stable resource/authority boundary. Then issue a separate finite plan for the smallest two-HELM cooperation experiment.

Compare a strong single HELM, a simple ensemble or fixed expert router, and the proposed coalescence mechanism under declared matched total work/information budgets. Separate better task answers from better reliability, latency, memory sharing and specialization. Include both complementary and redundant members, harmful/shared false information, conflict, interruption and split/reload checks. Avoid inferring capability multiplication from an increased parameter sum.

## First later success criterion

Two independently identified HELMs complete one permitted shared task using recorded contributions; the outcome, cost and baselines are measured; then both separate through a predeclared restoration/descendant mode with verified identities and histories. No leakage, silent overwrite, unexplained state loss, fake reversal or cancellation of past costs is permitted.

This is the recorded direction after the present baseline, CAS integration and initial self-improvement work. It is not today's deliverable and not a reason to spawn a fourth research team.
