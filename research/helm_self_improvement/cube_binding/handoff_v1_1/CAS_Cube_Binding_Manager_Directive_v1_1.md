## 1. Mission and desired outcomes

Implement a **cube-aware, role-aware analysis and teaching extension** within the existing CAS family. Do not create a sixth coordinating system or a competing Harness.

The program must ultimately make six different questions answerable with appropriately scoped evidence:

1. **Representation:** Does a selected internal state encode the relevant entities, relationships, contexts, and role assignments?
2. **Commonality:** Does the learner distinguish a shared constituent or relational pattern from superficial association?
3. **Causal use:** Does editing the hypothesized binding change the unchanged model's downstream behavior in the predicted way?
4. **Transfer:** Does the relation remain usable across different fillers, arrangements, and explicitly corresponding views?
5. **Learning:** Does verified teaching improve a later, fresh-process, unaided result rather than only a guided answer or an external probe?
6. **Improvement selection:** Can retained diagnostic experience eventually help HELM select a useful remedy, with the selecting actor and retained benefit demonstrated?

The immediate product is a narrow, functioning vertical slice. The longer-term product is a reusable ability to diagnose and improve relational computation. Positive, negative, and inconclusive findings are legitimate scientific outcomes. They must not erase the requirement to deliver functioning engineering.

**Primary finish line:** On an admitted Parts or Reach path, connect public input to actual HELM capture, one frozen binding hypothesis, reconstruction and/or a controlled binding edit, unchanged downstream scoring, independent evaluation, evidence retention, and read-only RPA reload. Demonstrate baseline neutrality first. A synthetic mechanism can establish wiring, but not natural HELM structure.

**Subsequent finish line:** Use a supported diagnosis to propose one targeted remedy and test it independently. Do not require a complete explanation of every weight before improving a localized defect. Do not call a larger score from an outside solver a better HELM model.

## 3. Separate identity, commonality, relation, role, and occurrence

These concepts must have distinct representations and tests.

**Identity** answers what an idea or entity is in the declared context. **Commonality** identifies what two ideas share, including a constituent or explicitly mapped structural feature. **Relation** identifies the kind of connection. **Role** identifies how an entity participates in that connection. **Occurrence** distinguishes separate appearances or uses of an entity, including repeated use in one structure.

Use the following acceptance illustration:

| Input | Helper | Recipient | Semantic relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The doctor helped the teacher. | Doctor | Teacher | HELP(Doctor, Teacher) |
| The teacher helped the doctor. | Teacher | Doctor | HELP(Teacher, Doctor) |
| The teacher was helped by the doctor. | Doctor | Teacher | HELP(Doctor, Teacher) |

This is a proposed semantic fixture. `HELP`, `helper`, and `recipient` are illustration labels, not newly admitted CAS instructions. The first two inputs contain the same concepts but differ in their bindings. The first and third preserve semantic participant roles while changing surface syntax. A syntactic subject and a semantic helper must not be conflated.

The doctor is not permanently a helper. The role belongs to an event or relationship occurrence. Two events involving the same people must not collapse into one event. Repeated fillers must retain multiplicity and occurrence identity. A sentence or event can itself be a cube and participate as a filler in a larger structure.

For the existing authored whole-part scene:

```text
Bicycle contains Wheel and Frame.
Cart contains Wheel and Platform.
Wheel contains Rim.
```

Wheel is the shared concept constituent of Bicycle and Cart. The branches separately preserve whole-to-direct-part direction. The scene does not assert that two physical objects possess one physical wheel. [C1, §§4 and 6]

**Required distinction:** shared vocabulary is not shared meaning; shared constituent is not object identity; correspondence is not equality; dependency is not automatically causation; graph reachability is not automatically causal influence on a neural answer.

### 3.1 New v1.1 hardening: preserve the pairing, not just endpoint categories

The following is a proposed CB1 counterexample, not a result reported by the paper:

```text
Scene A: A contains B; C contains D.
Scene B: A contains D; C contains B.
```

Both scenes contain the same fillers, the same predicate and the same sets of source and target endpoints. A global bag of `source=A, source=C, target=B, target=D` loses which edge belongs together. A compliant relation-frame/role representation must distinguish the scenes through its bounded structural/event association. Two different event IDs in metadata alone do not prove that the fitted vector representation retains that association.

Similarly, retain repeated occurrences of a filler, including repeated use in the same role in different frames. The chosen structural role grammar must express the necessary association/multiplicity without giving every training example an unrelated one-off role. Audit UUIDs, split IDs and receipt hashes stay outside the fit features. A stable source/target label by itself is not a complete binding design.

Put these cases under CB03/CB05/CB17, including the encoding and reconstruction input, not only JSON round-trip validation. Require the symbolic fixtures to distinguish the structures; learned approximation must be measured rather than presumed.

## 7. Implement three distinct analysis capabilities

### 7.1 Readout: recover the proposed information

Implement separate evaluations for filler identity, predicate identity, role assignment, context/event membership, occurrence tracking, and supported commonality. Evaluate ambiguous or incomplete cases with the task's declared uncertainty policy.

A detector's feature inputs must be declared. The primary activation-only readout cannot access the raw task solution or an answer-bearing authoring field. Where public input or query features are deliberately included, name that hybrid condition and compare against an input-only baseline.

A graph parser reading public edges may establish correct authored structure. It does not establish neural detection. Store `authored_structure` and `model_inferred_structure` separately, even when they agree. Failure to decode a feature does not prove the learner lacks it; the decoder or proposed role scheme may be inadequate.

### 7.2 Reconstruction: test a structured representation hypothesis

Implement a small DISCOVER-inspired reconstructor fitted to admitted hidden-state captures, with the target learner frozen. In the paper-faithful primary fit, optimize representation error and predeclared regularization, not evaluator answers, successful-path labels or downstream correctness. A task-supervised surrogate is a separately named assisted-analysis condition, not an equivalent fit. A reference formulation is:

```text
z_hat(x, s) = W_s @ vec(
    sum( outer(filler_embedding[b.filler], role_embedding[b.role_path])
         for b in bindings_visible_to_site(x, s) )
) + bias_s
```

`vec` has one documented ordering. For dimensions `d_f`, `d_r` and native width `d_h`, verify `f in R^d_f`, `r in R^d_r`, `W_s in R^(d_h x (d_f*d_r))`, and `bias_s in R^d_h` with explicit tensor axes, dtypes and serialization. Bounds apply to the role vocabulary and the effective trainable parameter count, not only to the number of examples. The learned factors are not inherently identifiable across fits; do not compare their coordinates across checkpoints without an admitted alignment. `vec` ordering remains fixed across fitting, reload and editing. `s` denotes an actual capture site and its supported scope. The multiset of bindings preserves occurrences. Role paths distinguish structural position, event scope, and endpoint role where required. For distributed hypotheses they may include both the represented item's role and the receiving site's role, analogous to P1's pair-based task-specific roles. [P1, §§2.3, 3.2, and 6.3]

Do not make every role path a unique sample ID. Do not supply future tokens to a prefix-limited representation or supply computed graph answers as role labels. Full input structure is available only where the actual model/site has that information.

Compare a small preregistered selection from: identity-only/bag-of-fillers; atomic role-filler lookup; positional structure; relation/argument roles; and a richer scoped or pair-based role scheme. When claiming a CAS-specific advantage, also include a capacity-conscious non-CAS structural comparison. Report parameter counts, role vocabulary size, fit budget, regularization, and any unmatchable differences.

Prevent degenerate pair memorization with held-out combinations and an explicit regularization/rank policy. P1's L2,1 regularization is a source-supported option, not a mandatory hyperparameter setting for HELM. Select settings on discovery/validation data only. [P1, §8 and Appendix L]

If normalizing captured vectors, fit statistics only on the training partition, preserve the transform identity, and define zero-variance and nonfinite handling. Restore the model's native scale before downstream substitution. In normalized coordinates, a change in raw space is `delta_raw = sigma * delta_z`; the mean is not added to a delta. Bias is included once per reconstructed representation, not once per constituent.

Assess reconstruction error and downstream behavior separately. Feed an approximation into the actual unchanged consumer at the declared site. Record original-versus-reconstructed answer agreement, actual task accuracy, and score/logit differences. Matching accuracy alone is not behavioral fidelity: two conditions can score equally while answering different items incorrectly.

For each example, store both agreement with the original model and correctness under the unchanged task evaluator. Preserve wrong-to-different-wrong changes and abstentions, not just total accuracy. Use the chosen task's own null/control distribution; the paper's permutation-based `1/n!` baseline does not automatically apply to Parts or Reach.

A reconstruction that improves an answer is not automatically a learned repair. It may be an externally supplied structured representation. Record what the reconstructor received and which original computations it replaced.

### 7.3 Intervention: test whether bindings influence the answer

Implement bounded constituent edits using a frozen fitted representation:

```text
delta = W_s @ vec(sum(new_binding_tensors) - sum(old_binding_tensors))
h_edited = h_original + delta_in_native_coordinates
```

This is a research operation over the selected state, not a new CAS instruction. The affine bias cancels in the difference. The full model and its independent evaluator remain unchanged. P1 supplies the rationale for replacing particular role-filler components and testing downstream effects. [P1, §7]

Support separately declared filler replacement, role reassignment, two-role swap, relation-endpoint swap, and local-versus-distributed edits only as the relevant source and limits permit. Freeze target sites, edit size, expected effect, and conditions before confirmation. The primary plan must not optimize an edit against final-test answers or use a gold-selected donor without an explicit oracle-assisted label.

For multi-site or multi-round intervention, specify the dependency footprint and application schedule. State which descendants are recomputed, where replacement occurs, and how double application, stale captures, and later hooks overwriting an earlier change are prevented. Do not copy P1's autoregressive token schedule into a graph learner without adapting its execution semantics.

Retain baseline, no-op, selected edit, and matched control. As needed compare same-size/rank/norm edits, role-shuffled edits, identity-only edits, and local versus distributed edits. Every condition must pay equivalent instrumentation overhead where applicable.

Record actual hook hits, before/after tensor identities, finite/norm checks, untouched state, exceptions, and cleanup. A missed site is not a completed intervention. Restoring weights alone is insufficient if caches or hooks remain altered.

**Counterfactual scoring rule:** A deliberate role swap may correctly change the answer away from the original-input answer. Score its intended counterfactual effect separately from ordinary task accuracy. Do not call that disagreement a repair gain or a failure without reference to the declared experiment.

Specificity and controls support a scoped causal contribution. They do not automatically establish necessity, sufficiency, a unique symbolic decomposition, a complete algorithm, or an on-distribution internal state. Preserve these alternatives in the finding.
